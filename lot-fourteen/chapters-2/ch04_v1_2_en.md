# Chapter 4: I have nothing to put in it
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.2 · EN

## Day One Hundred and Fifty-Nine · Wednesday 11 March

He did not go looking for the man. He went looking for the calendar.

A man with thirty-one years in a trade that size is on a schedule whether he likes it or not, and most of it is private and about four days of it a year are not. Launches. Handovers. The opening of somebody else's terminal.

And funerals.

---

They are announced, and that is all it takes. A death in that trade goes into two papers and onto a hall's own board, with the room number and the three days, and anybody at all may come.

Georgij found one on the Sunday night in about twenty minutes.

---

The man had been seventy-six. He had run a coastal feeder line out of Mokpo for most of his life, sold it to somebody larger six years ago, and gone on going to things afterwards, which is what men do.

Georgij had never met him. He read what there was and it took under an hour, and at the end of it he knew four true things about a man he was going to use.

---

Mr Ku drove him and did not ask, again.

---

The hall was on the third floor and there was a queue at the desk outside the room, and the queue was moving because these things move.

At the desk there is a book.

Everybody who comes writes his name in it. Then he puts an envelope in the box beside it, and the amount goes in the book too, in the second column, in a different hand, later.

---

Georgij stood in the queue and understood at about the fourth person what he had walked into.

He had known there would be a book. He had not thought about the box, because he does not think about money, because thinking about money has never once been useful to him.

---

He wrote his name.

It is not a thing he has done often. There were papers in the second week of October so that he could sign, and there was an instrument in December, and both of those were his name on a line about what he was permitted to do.

This was a book with a name in it and nothing else next to it.

---

The man at the desk had one hand out for the envelope and was already looking past him at the next in the queue.

"I have nothing to put in it," said Georgij.

---

The hand stopped.

There is a particular quality to that half-second. Contempt would be easier. It is a man recalculating what he is looking at, in a good coat, at a funeral, in a queue.

"That is all right," he said, in the end.

"It is not. I am telling you because you will write it in the second column and somebody will think it was a mistake." Georgij did not move out of the way yet. "It was not a mistake. Put a line through it."

---

The man put a line through it.

Then Georgij went in and bowed twice and came out again, which took about ninety seconds and which he did properly, because a room like that is the last one in which a man ought to be economical.

---

Park Sang-hoon was in the corridor at the end, near the window where the smokers stand, with two men who wanted things from him and were not going to get them today.

He had seen Georgij come out of the room. He had probably seen him in the queue.

---

He came over himself, which was the whole reason it had to be a room and not a telephone.

"Two billion two hundred million," said Sang-hoon.

"Chairman."

"I have thought about it since December and I have not moved off it, and I am not going to." He had a paper cup of something he was not drinking. "I have moved up. Say a number."

---

Georgij put his hands behind his back.

"He was never for sale," he said. "Five words. They were hers and not mine, and I was in the room when she said them."

"I remember who was in the room."

---

"And thank you." He said it without any decoration on it at all. "You have not moved off that figure from December to March. On the day you set it you gave her a reason for it, and the reason was a thing you had watched me do."

"I remember what I said."

"So do I. It is the only compliment anybody has ever paid me that arrived with a number on it. I have thought about that more than is useful. And I would sooner have had it from you than from anybody in this country."

---

Sang-hoon laughed at that, once, and two men down the corridor looked over and then looked away again.

"It was higher than the one that is on anybody," he said. "That is the part your side has never understood. I do not pay what a thing is worth. I pay what it costs to stop having to think about it."

---

Then he stopped, and he looked at Georgij for slightly too long, and he did not say the next thing.

Georgij watched him not say it, and had a fair idea what it was, because there is only one fact about him that a man in that position would hold back and enjoy holding back.

It went across Sang-hoon's face for about a quarter of a second. Then it was gone, and it was still his.

---

"You did not come here for a man from Mokpo," said Sang-hoon.

"No."

"You did not know him."

"I did not know him. I read four things about him on Sunday night and came here on the strength of them." Georgij did not soften it. "You come to these. There is no other room in this country I can walk into and find you in."

"You could have written to me."

"Then you would have had a day to decide what I wanted."

---

Sang-hoon put the cup down on the sill.

"Ask."

---

"You will have had somebody look in a book for you, in a building near the river." Georgij said it flat. "A man who puts a figure on a thing and does not move off it from December to March has been at the paper on it. I do not want what was on the page. I want to know how you got the look."

---

That was the whole of the ask, and Sang-hoon took about four seconds over it, which for him is a long time.

"You want to know whether the door is still there."

"Yes."

"It is there. It is not going to be there in a month." He was not enjoying himself, which was new. "They have a new man. He has been through the room where they keep it and he has been through the people, and the ones who were useful were useful because nobody had ever looked at them."

---

"Who?"

"Settlement." Sang-hoon watched two men go past with their coats over their arms. "There is always somebody in settlement. It is the only desk in the building that touches every line and gets paid the least for it, and it has been that way in every one of these places since before either of us was born."

---

"And the price?"

"For me it was not money." He turned round. "And that is what you have come for, so we can stop walking round it. What do you have?"

---

Georgij had known since Sunday what he had.

"You want to know how it was done."

"I know how it was done. I have had two and a half months and I am not stupid." Sang-hoon's voice did not go up. "I know the report was true. I know the board could not sell and I asked you about it myself, on page fourteen, and bought it anyway. I have been over that evening more times than I have been over anything since I was thirty."

He put one finger down on the sill.

"What I do not have is why it was me."

---

The window was open two inches at the top and the corridor was cold at that end and somebody in the room behind them was crying properly, without any performance in it.

---

"On the eighteenth of November you talked to me for three and a half hours about how you work," said Georgij. "You were pleased to do it. I did not ask you one question that I had not decided on in a car on the way there."

"Go on."

"You told me that when you take hold of a thing you do not put it down, and you said it as a virtue, because it is one, and it is the reason you have what you have." He did not stop and did not hurry. "It is also the only mechanism in you that can be aimed. Everything else about you is judgement, and judgement cannot be aimed at anything. So the report went on the table in the one form that made putting it down impossible, which was as a correction of me."

---

Sang-hoon did not say anything for a while.

"It was you being wrong," he said.

"It was me being wrong in a way that you were the only man in the room able to fix. You did not buy Hanseong because you wanted it. You bought it because I was wrong about it in front of you and you could not leave that where it was."

"And you knew that on the sixteenth."

"I knew it about forty minutes in. I stayed for the other two hours and fifty because it was the best thing anybody has ever told me about this trade, and I was enjoying it. I want that said, because it is the part I am least comfortable with."

---

Sang-hoon looked out of the window at a car park.

"Then I gave it to you," he said.

"You gave it to me. I went and got it, and you handed it over, and both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other."

---

"You know what the worst of it is." It was not a question and it did not get a mark. "I would do the three and a half hours again. With you, next week, if you asked. That is not a man who has learned something. That is a man who found out what he is."

---

"The name in settlement," said Sang-hoon, after a while, "is not one I am going to say in a corridor with a funeral in it."

"No."

"Come out to the car."

---

They went down, and it took four minutes. What Georgij came out with fitted into one line of a notebook, and he did not take the notebook out until he was in the back of his own car with the door shut.

---

Sang-hoon had one more thing at the car door, and he said it as though it had only just occurred to him, which with him it never has.

"When she is finished with you," he said, "you will not come to me. I know that. I am not asking."

"Chairman."

"I am telling you that I will be at these things, four or five times a year, until I am the one in the room." He got in. "That is the whole of it. Somebody should say a thing like that once and then not go on about it."

---

Jang met the car on the gravel, which he does not do.

He did not say anything and he did not need to. He had known since half past nine where a car of that house had gone. A hall of that kind publishes the room number and the three days. And knowing where the cars go is his job, and he has never once been bad at it.

---

Annie was in the small room with the door open.

Georgij stood in it with his coat still on and did the only arithmetic that mattered. It took no time at all.

He had come home to tell her. She had it first. There is no version of the next ten minutes in which he can prove the order.

He had said it out loud on Saturday. *On the day it starts to cost you, you will have all of it that day.*

The trouble with a man who has never said anything untrue is that he has also never had to be believed.

---

"Take the coat off," said Annie.
