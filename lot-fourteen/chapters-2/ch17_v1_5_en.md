# Chapter 17: Four minutes of somebody's time
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.5 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Six · Monday 27 April

Mr Ok leaves his building at twenty past six and walks to the corner and waits for a car that is always eight minutes late. The driver takes his own mother somewhere at six and has done for four years, and Mr Ok has never once mentioned it.

That is the second most useful thing Georgij learned about him and he learned it on the Sunday.

---

He was on the corner at ten past.

"Mr Ok."

"Yes."

"My name will not mean anything to you. I would like four minutes and I am not selling anything and I am not from a newspaper."

---

Mr Ok looked at him the way a man of that sort looks at a stranger who knows his name, which is politely and with the whole of his attention.

"Four minutes," he said. "My car is eight minutes late."

"I know. That is why I am here now."

---

There is a low wall outside that building with a hedge behind it, and they stood at it. Mr Ok put his case down on it the way a man does when he expects to pick it up again shortly.

"On the twelfth of March," said Georgij, "at twenty to five in the afternoon, a page was copied twice on the machine in your corridor."

---

Mr Ok did not say anything.

"It was the last page of the tidied catalogue that the auction house on the river sent to its buyers in the first week of March. Yours came into the office on the third. It has four references on it and a phrase underneath them."

"I am not going to discuss my office with a man on a wall."

"No. I am not asking you to discuss anything. I am going to tell you what happened and then I am going to ask you one question, and you can decide about the question when we get there."

---

Mr Ok picked his case back up.

"Then be quick."

"Mrs Uhm did the copying," said Georgij. "She has done your filing since 2004 and she did not think about it for one second, because a person who is asked to copy a page copies the page. She is not going to be troubled by anybody, ever, and I want that said before the rest of it."

---

That is the sentence that stopped him, and Georgij had known on the Sunday that it would be, and had put it there for that reason and had also meant it.

"One of those copies went to a woman in Ulsan called Nam Byung-hee. She runs four trucks and she chaired an association of six small shippers for twenty years." He said it evenly and did not hurry. "On the twenty-third of April her six members voted her out of the chair. Her lane has no work on it. By the middle of May she will not have a firm."

---

The traffic did what traffic does.

"That has nothing to do with me," said Mr Ok.

"No," said Georgij. "It has nothing to do with you at all, and that is the exact truth, and I am not going to improve it."

"Then why are you standing at my wall?"

"Because it went through your machine," he said. "It did not go through anybody else's. Somebody asked you for something small, in a way that made it small, and it was small, and it is the reason a woman of fifty-eight stayed behind on Thursday and stacked the chairs."

---

Mr Ok put the case down again.

He did not do it deliberately. That is simply where his hand went.

"I want to be clear about what is not going to happen," said Georgij, "because you are about to spend the night working out what it is, and you will get it wrong, and I would rather you slept."

"Then I would like to hear it."

"Nothing is going to happen to your firm. Nothing is going to happen to your family. Nobody is going to speak to your clients, your insurers, your bank or your brother-in-law in Suwon. You will not lose one won over this and neither will anybody who works for you. That is not a negotiating position and there is nothing you can do that would change it."

---

Mr Ok looked at him for a long moment.

"Why?"

"Because you did not choose anything." Georgij kept his hands where they could be seen. "You were asked and you said yes, the way a man says yes to a small thing, and I do not take people apart for that. It is the only rule I have made this month and I made it before I knew it was going to be about you."

"You are telling me I am not worth it."

"I am telling you that you are a hand."

---

That landed harder than any of the rest of it, and Georgij watched it land and made himself go on watching.

"Now the question," he said.

"Who asked you?"

---

Mr Ok did not answer for about six seconds.

"I do not know his name."

"The whole of it, and let me decide what it is worth."

"There was a lunch in January. The port authority does one every year and puts the same forty people on the list, and I go because my father went and because there is nobody left who remembers that he did." He said it looking at the road. "A man I had not met before came and sat down beside me at the end of it. Somebody introduced us and I did not catch it and I did not ask, because you do not, and afterwards I thought he had said his own name and I had missed it."

"What did he ask you?"

"Whether I had ever done business on the Ulsan coast, and what the small operators there were like, and then he asked about one of them by name."

---

Georgij did not move at all.

"He gave you the name."

"He gave me the name. I said I did not know her and that everybody says she is honest and that her lane is too small for anything." Mr Ok stopped. "He was very interested in the word honest. I remember that because I thought at the time that it was an odd thing to be interested in."

"And in March."

"He telephoned. In the first week. He said we had met at the lunch, which we had, and he asked whether I received the catalogues from that house and I said yes." He was speaking more slowly now, the way a man does when he is walking backwards through something and finding out how it looks. "He said there was a page in the back that a friend of his in Ulsan had been asking about, and would I have somebody copy it and send it down. He gave me the address. It was four minutes of somebody's time."

"Two copies."

"He asked for two. He said one to send and one for the file." Mr Ok's voice did something on that. "I have thought about a dozen things since you started talking and that is the one I did not think about at all until now."

"What did you do with the second one?"

"I put it in the tray for Mrs Uhm to file and I never saw it again, and it will be in a folder somewhere on the second floor and I could probably find it in twenty minutes."

---

Georgij looked at him.

"Then that is not the one I am afraid of."

"What did he sound like?"

"Ordinary," said Mr Ok. "Sixty, perhaps a little less. Not tall. He did not eat anything at that lunch, not one thing, and I noticed it because my mother would have made a remark. Pleasant. Quiet. The sort of man who has been in rooms."

"And at the end."

"At the end of the lunch he stood up and shook my hand and said that he was glad we had finally met."

---

Georgij stood on a pavement in Jung-gu at twenty-six minutes past six and did not say anything for about four seconds.

"Finally."

"Finally. We had never met. I have thought about it two or three times since and I decided he had confused me with somebody, and I put it down and I have not taken it up again until this minute."

"Mr Ok."

"Yes."

"He did not confuse you with anybody."

---

The car came, and it was eight minutes late.

"Is there anything I should do?" said Mr Ok.

"No. Nothing at all. Go home." Georgij stepped back from the wall. "If he telephones you again, say yes to whatever it is, and do it exactly as he asks, and do not warn anybody and do not refuse him."

"That is not what I expected you to say."

"I know. It is what I am saying."

---

Mr Ok stood with his hand on the door of his car.

"That woman in Ulsan," he said. "Is there anything at all."

"No," said Georgij. "There is nothing, and I would like you to hear me say that rather than spend a year deciding it for yourself. It is finished and it cannot be unfinished and you could not have prevented it in March, because you did not know one single thing."

"But it went through my corridor."

"It went through your corridor."

---

Mr Ok got into the car and did not close the door for a moment.

"You said your name would not mean anything to me."

"It will not."

"I should like it anyway."

---

Georgij told him.

It meant nothing to him, exactly as it had at a gate in Ulsan in March, and it did not do the thing this time that it had done then.

---

He noticed on the way to the bridge what he had done with the last thirty seconds of it.

Somebody telephoned that man in March and asked him for four minutes of somebody else's time, and he said yes, because it was small.

And a man stood at his wall this evening and told him that if the telephone goes again he is to say yes, and to do it exactly as it is asked, and to warn nobody.

---

There is a word for that and he did not go looking for it.

---

He wrote it in the notebook in the back of his own car before they were out of Jung-gu, and it came to nine lines.

Sixty or a little less. Not tall. Ate nothing. Pleasant, quiet, has been in rooms. Was introduced in a way that did not carry a name. Telephoned in the first week of March, six weeks after a lunch, and knew what post that office receives.

And at the end of it, in his own writing, the thing he had known since the pavement:

*I am glad we finally met.*

---

That is not a description. Any of four hundred men in this city is sixty and quiet and not tall.

But it is a sentence, and a sentence belongs to somebody, and there is one person alive who has been in enough rooms in this city to hear a sentence and put a face on it.

She has been declining to give him that face since December, and she has had a reason every single time, and every one of those reasons was better in December than it is tonight.
