# Chapter 22: I did not offer you anything
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.2 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Fifteen · Wednesday 6 May

Mr Byun kept the register of that house for twenty-six years and left it at the end of February, ten weeks after the fund completed. He did not want to work for people who would ask him what he did all day.

He is sixty-eight. He lives on the eighth floor of a building in Seongdong with a view of the river that he paid about forty per cent extra for. He told Georgij about the forty per cent inside the first two minutes.

---

He opened the door himself and was pleased to have a visitor.

That is the part Georgij had not prepared for and it cost him about half a minute to adjust to.

---

"You are from the fund," said Mr Byun. "You are the fourth one and I have told the other three everything I know, which is a great deal, because I was in that building longer than any man alive."

"I am not from the fund."

"Then you are from the auditors. Sit down. Do you want tea, I am not going to make it but I can offer it."

---

"I am from one of the buyers," said Georgij.

---

He got a small pause for that and then a completely open face.

"Which one. I had two thousand of them."

---

"Four of them paid you to have an entry deleted," said Georgij, "and you took the money and did not do it. October of last year, February of this year, and two others going back four years."

---

Mr Byun sat down on his own sofa and looked at him.

He was not frightened, and he was not angry, and there was one thing that happened on his face and it was not either of those.

He was trying to remember which four.

---

That is the moment Georgij will not be putting down for some time.

---

"The February one," said Mr Byun eventually. "That was a woman. Very direct. She came four times and on the third occasion she did not bring money, and I have thought about that visit since, because she was the only one of them who frightened me."

"And the others."

"The October one is the same woman. That one was easy, that one was a withdrawal before the sale and those are nothing." He waved a hand. "The other two are further back and I am not going to pretend I have them by heart."

---

"One of them was Mrs Sunwoo."

"Yes." He nodded, pleased. "Yes, that is right. Three years ago. Charming woman, very old family. She sent somebody with it in an envelope and I never saw her at all."

---

Georgij did not say anything for a moment.

---

"You do not appear to be worried," he said.

"Should I be worried."

---

"You took money for a service four times and did not perform it."

"I took money for a service that this house had been selling since before I sat down in that chair," said Mr Byun, and he said it comfortably and without any defensiveness at all, "and I did what was done. I did not invent it. The man before me did it and the man before him. It is the only thing that house had to sell that nobody could get anywhere else."

---

"And the not performing it."

---

"Nobody was hurt," said Mr Byun.

---

The river did whatever the river does eight floors down.

---

"There is a woman in Ulsan called Nam Byung-hee," said Georgij. "She is fifty-eight. On the fourteenth of March somebody sent her a copy of the page that has those four entries on it, and she used it. By the middle of this month she will have sold four trucks and lost a firm her brother left her."

---

Mr Byun looked politely interested.

"That is very unfortunate. I do not know her."

"No. She does not know you either."

---

"Then I am not sure what you are here for."

---

"I am here to tell you what is going to happen," said Georgij, "and then I am going to leave, and there is nothing in the middle."

---

"The four buyers are going to be told." He did not raise his voice at any point. "By name. Yours. What each of them paid, which afternoon they paid it on, and that the entry is still there. One of them is the woman who frightened you and one of them is Mrs Sunwoo, and what any of them does after that is theirs and not mine."

---

Mr Byun did not move for about four seconds.

---

Then he got up off his own sofa and stood in the middle of his own sitting room with a view he paid forty per cent extra for.

---

"That is not necessary," he said.

---

"No."

"I can give it back. All four. It is not a great deal of money and I have got it."

"I know you have got it."

---

"Then let me give it back."

---

"They are still going to be told," said Georgij.

---

Mr Byun stood there.

---

"You cannot do that. I live here. My daughter-in-law's family is in this building." He had lost about eleven years off his voice. "Mrs Sunwoo knows everybody. Everybody. She will have it round this city in a fortnight and I will not be able to go to anything."

"Yes."

---

"What do you want?"

---

"I have not asked you for anything," said Georgij.

---

"Everybody wants something."

"I came here to tell you what is going to happen to you and I have told you." He stood up. "I would have been prepared to hear anything you said while I was in the room. I am going to be out of it in about a minute and after that I am not going to answer the telephone."

---

Mr Byun went and stood at the window.

---

"The fourth one," he said. "The man. You said four and you have named two of them and you have not named him, so you do not have him."

---

Georgij did not answer that at all.

---

"He is not like the others," said Mr Byun. "I want that considered. I want it taken into account that I am telling you and that I did not have to."

"Nothing is going to be taken into account. I am not trading with you and I want that said before you say another word, because if you say it thinking that you are buying something you are going to be angry with yourself tomorrow."

---

Mr Byun said it anyway.

---

"I did not take money from him."

---

He turned round from the window.

"Three of them came to me. The woman twice, and the old lady's man with the envelope. That is how it worked and that is what I did for twenty-six years." He had both hands on the back of a chair. "The fourth one did not come to me at all. I was told."

"By whom."

"By the owner. Personally, in his room, standing up, and it took under a minute and he did not sit down and neither did I."

---

Georgij stood very still.

---

"Say the whole of it."

---

"He said there was a lot going out that evening and there would be no invoice for it. I was to enter it and take it out again in the spring, and I was not to write anything at all about who had it." Mr Byun's voice had gone flat, which is what happens to men of that sort when they get to a thing they have kept. "And then he said the part I did not understand and have not understood since. He said the fee had been paid and I was to put it through the book as though it had come to me."

---

"He gave you the money."

"He gave me the money. In the room. From his own pocket, in an envelope that had already been made up." Mr Byun looked at the carpet. "I have taken cash from a great many people in that building and every single one of them wanted something for it. That is the only time in twenty-six years that anybody has ever given me money so that the money would be in the right place."

---

The flat was very quiet.

---

"You wrote something down," said Georgij.

---

"How do you know that."

"Because you were frightened, and a frightened man of that kind writes one line and puts it somewhere and then does not look at it for four years."

---

Mr Byun went into another room and was gone for about two minutes, and came back with a folded sheet out of a diary.

One line, a date, and four words about where the instruction came from.

---

He held it out and Georgij took it and read it and folded it again.

---

"Now the four are still going to be told," said Georgij.

---

"You have got what you came for."

"I did not come for that. I did not know it existed until twenty minutes ago and I did not ask you for it." He put it in the inside pocket. "You offered it to me because you thought you were buying something. I told you before you opened your mouth that you were not, and you did it anyway, and that is the whole of what has happened in this room."

---

Mr Byun stood in the middle of his sitting room.

---

"Then you are a very cruel young man."

---

"No," said Georgij. "I have been in three rooms in the last three weeks and in two of them that would have been true. In this one it is not, and you have not got the first idea why, and I am not going to explain it to you."

---

He got as far as the door.

---

"Twenty-six years," said Mr Byun, behind him. "Do you know what that building was like when I started in it."

"No."

"Nobody does. That is what I am going to be left with."

---

Georgij stopped with his hand on the door and did not turn round.

---

"Mr Byun."

"What."

---

"Mrs Sunwoo paid you to take an evening out of a book three years ago, and she has carried the word round with her ever since like a receipt." He said it perfectly evenly. "She is eighty-one. When I told her in March that it had not been done, she rang a bell and had a catalogue brought out of a drawer. She found the line with one finger and did not need to be shown which one."

---

"You will be able to tell her all about the building when she comes."

---

He had not decided any of it before he went up.

He went there with two things, which were four dates and an address. He had worked out on the stairs that there is nothing available that hurts a man of that sort except what other people think of him.

The rest of it he decided standing in a sitting room, in the four seconds after a man of sixty-eight had to try to remember which four.

---

That is what it is now. Nobody put a ceiling on it, nobody is going to audit it, and there is no room he has to walk into afterwards and say what it cost.

---

He went down eight floors and out onto a street by the river, and read the four words again in the car, and did not like where they pointed.
