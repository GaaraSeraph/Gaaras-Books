# Chapter 29: The rest of what I know
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.8 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Thirty-Eight · Friday 29 May

The fourth stop is a road with a chemist on the corner and eleven apartment blocks behind it, and the blocks are numbered in a way that made sense to somebody in about nineteen eighty.

Georgij came out on the bus.

---

He could have come in the car. Mr Ku offered twice and did not press it the second time, which is Mr Ku saying that he thinks it is a mistake.

A car of that kind at the door of a woman who has been out of work for seventeen days is a sentence about her, delivered to everybody on that stair. She would have had to live in it after he had gone.

---

Second floor. The bell has a strip of tape over the name.

---

She opened the door a hand's width with her hand still on it and looked at him.

"You," said Mrs Jeon.

"Yes."

---

She did not move.

"You do not have your name," she said. "I can see that from here."

"No."

"Then you have come to ask me for something on credit, and I told you in March what I do about that."

---

Georgij stood in a corridor with a strip light in it.

"May I have four minutes?" he said. "If you want me gone at the end of them I will go, and I will not come back, and nobody else will come here either."

---

She looked at him for what was probably three seconds. Then she took her hand off the door.

---

The flat is one room and a kitchen and a window with the eleven blocks in it. There is a table with two chairs, and on the table there is a school exercise book with a green cover and a biro lying across it, closed.

She had been sitting at it when he rang.

---

She did not put it away and she did not move it. She sat down in front of it with both hands flat on the cover, which is how a person sits who has decided that a thing is not going to be taken off the table.

"Four minutes," said Mrs Jeon.

"I have been in the house on the river since March," said Georgij. "I know what happened to your desk and I know who signed the schedule. I went and saw him yesterday afternoon and he told me about the ninth of January."

---

Her hands did not come off the book.

"He told you."

"He told me before he gave me your address, and he said out loud that he was telling me first so that it would not be payment." Georgij did not sit down, because he had not been asked to. "He said you had it in your hands and you were holding it out and he did not put his hand on it. He said every word of what he told you that day was true, and that it is still true, and that it is still the worst thing he has ever said to anybody."

---

Mrs Jeon looked at the window for a moment.

"He is not wrong that it was true," she said. "He could have opened it in January. He has had from January until May to be sorry about it, and being sorry about it is not the same as opening it, and I am not going to be the one who tells him it is."

"No."

"Sit down."

---

He sat down.

"Your four minutes have about ninety seconds in them," she said. "Say the thing you came to say."

"You were right," said Georgij.

---

Mrs Jeon did not answer that.

"Not you were probably right, and not there is something in it," he said. "The fourth reference on that back page is a person, and there was no bill because there was never going to be one. You went through it twice in twenty-three years because you were the only one in that building who could see the shape of it."

---

The biro rolled about an inch and stopped against her hand.

"You cannot know that," she said.

"I can."

"How?"

"Because a fee was paid for that line by the wrong man," said Georgij, "on the night, out of his own pocket, in an envelope that had already been made up. Nobody billed him and nobody was ever going to. He put a payment where an absence would have been."

---

She did not say anything at all.

"An empty space in a ledger is a question," he said. "A payment in a ledger is an ordinary Tuesday. He was not buying a deletion. He was buying the look of one, and he paid a house he did not owe in order to have it. The only reason anybody ever noticed the space was that a woman behind a glass panel could not let it alone."

---

Mrs Jeon put the biro down flat.

"I sat at that desk until midnight," she said.

---

Georgij waited.

"The one with no clearing. Four years ago, in the spring." She said it to the tabletop. "The room went at about half past ten and the runners were down by a quarter to, and at nine minutes past eleven they came and told me to keep the desk open, because there was a clearing coming. I kept it open. I sat there until midnight and nobody came, and in the morning I asked, and I was told that it had gone through the office instead."

"And it had not."

"It had not gone through anywhere." She looked up. "I have been in settlement for twenty-three years and that is the only night I have ever been told to wait for something that did not exist."

---

Georgij sat very still.

"Do you have the lot number?"

---

She did not open the book.

"You are asking me to give you the one thing I have," she said, "and I want to know what you are, because I have had three different answers about you since March and I have not believed any of them."

---

She put her hand flat on the cover again.

"A firm in Yeouido rang me on the twenty-eighth of April," she said. "A Miss Heo, who gave me her name and her extension and the house she was ringing for, and who was perfectly civil and had clearly been asked to be. I went in and sat in front of two of them and they were civil as well."

"And you did not take it."

"I had been told on the tenth that I was going. It was not announced, there is no register anywhere with my name in it, and there is nobody in that building who would put himself out for me." Her hand had not come off the cover. "So at the end I asked them how my name had come to be on their table. The pleasant one looked at the other one first, and then said that it had come through a contact."

---

Georgij put both hands on the edge of the table and left them there.

"Twenty-three years in settlement," said Mrs Jeon. "A line that arrives in the book and nobody will say where it came from is the one thing I know how to be afraid of. I have been sitting on one of those since January, and I was not going to go and work inside a second one for a salary." Her hand stayed where it was. "It was you."

"Yes."

"Then you may have the rest of the question," said Mrs Jeon. "You are somebody's. I could see that in March and I can see it now. Whose."

---

There it was.

---

He could have gone round it. There were three ways round it in the room and he could see all three, and the second one was good enough that she would not have found it for a week.

---

All three of them worked by making her somebody who is handled.

She has been that in a building for twenty-three years, and she was going to know.

"I am on that page," said Georgij.

---

Mrs Jeon did not move.

"The fourth of October," he said. "Two of the four references are that night. Lot eleven and lot fourteen, four lines apart. I was in the room for both of them, and I was one of them, and the one I was is the second one."

---

The flat was very quiet.

"You are telling me that to pay me," she said.

"Yes."

"Do you understand what you have just put on this table?"

---

She had asked him two questions since he came in and both of them had been flat. That one went up at the end.

"Yes," said Georgij.

"Then say it. I want to hear whether you have actually counted it or whether you are being generous at somebody else's expense."

"There is a woman who bought two people on the fourth of October," he said. "That page has four references on it and a phrase, and it has no column for who paid, and the house that wrote it is finished and cannot be asked anything. That is the whole of why she is safe."

"It went to every buyer in March," said Mrs Jeon.

"It went to six hundred and forty of them in March and it has been lying in their drawers ever since, because a page that nobody living will stand behind is a photocopy of an allegation." Georgij kept his hands where they were. "Mr Hwang can say the page is his. He cannot say who was in the room, and what he does know he knows because I told him in May."

---

Mrs Jeon looked at the closed cover under her hands.

"So I am the bill," she said.

"You are the bill," said Georgij. "You were behind the glass for both of them and there is nobody else left who was, and you are the only person in this who has never been paid by anybody. You could sell it. You could take it to a newspaper. You could hold it for eight years and take it out when you needed something, and there would be nothing anybody could do to you afterwards except things that would make it worse."

"And you did it anyway."

"I did it anyway."

---

He had handed her the one line that could end the house he lives in, because she deals in entries and he had nothing else that would clear.

---

Mrs Jeon sat back in the chair for the first time since he came in.

"Why?"

"Because you asked me for a name in March and I have not got one and I am never going to have one," said Georgij. "The only thing I have that a person in settlement would accept is a line on a page. You deal in entries. I have given you mine. It is not a favour and I have not made you a present of anything, and if you use it I will have nobody to blame."

---

She looked at him for a long moment.

"Twenty-three years," she said, "and nobody in that building ever told me anything that could hurt them."

---

She opened the exercise book.

---

It is not a ledger. It is a school exercise book with squared paper and a green cover, and there are about forty lines in it over twenty-three years, and every line has a date and a time and about six words.

She turned it round so that it faced him and put one finger under a line about two thirds of the way down the left page.

"Lot six," she said. "The spring auction, four years ago. Kept the desk open until midnight. No clearing."

---

Georgij read it twice and did not write it down.

"It was in the book," he said.

"It was in the book from the January before until the September after. I put four things through that desk against that reference in that time, and all four of them cleared, and they are on the line under it." She did not take her finger away. "Flowers, twice. A driver. And a doctor."

"A doctor."

"In the July." She got it out level and it cost her the same as it costs him. "I passed the invoice myself. It was a proper invoice from a proper practice, and I did not think about it for four years, and I have thought about it every day since the ninth of January."

---

Georgij looked at the line under it.

"Mrs Jeon."

"What?"

"I am going to find out what happened," he said, "and I am not going to promise you that it ends well, because I do not know and I am not going to say a thing to you that I would have to take back. What I will do is tell you. Whatever it is, and whenever I have it, you will hear it from me and not from anybody else."

---

She closed the book.

"That is the correct answer," she said, "and it is the first correct answer I have had from anybody since January, and I notice that it is also the only one that costs you something to keep."

---

She pushed the book about two inches across the table towards him and took her hand off it.

"Not to keep," she said. "Copy it and bring it back. I have had it for twenty-three years and I would like it in this flat."

---

She had given him everything in it and kept the book, which she had bought herself.

"I will bring it back on Tuesday."

---

At the door she said one more thing.

"There is a man in that building who is proud of keeping everything," said Mrs Jeon. "You may tell him from me that I kept mine too, and that I bought the book myself, and that it did not take a great deal of courage until the day it did."

---

Georgij went down two flights and out past the chemist and stood at the stop for a quarter of an hour.

---

He has a lot number, a season, and four cleared invoices against it.

He also has the answer to a question he did not ask anybody for ten weeks.

The call was made. It was refused in the first minute it was possible to refuse it, and she was right to refuse it, and nobody would have had to explain any of that to him if he had asked once in April.

He also has a woman four stops out of a station he had never used, sitting at a table in front of a window with eleven blocks in it. She can put her hand on a piece of paper that would take Annie apart.

---

He worked it through twice on the bus and got the same answer both times, which is that she will not, and that he is right about that, and that being right about it is not the point.

Nobody gave him leave to do it.

He is going to have to go into the small room tonight and say so.
