# Chapter 23: Three of the four
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.0 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Twenty-Two · Wednesday 13 May

He noticed it in the car before they were off the bridge on the sixth of May, and he has not been able to do anything about it since.

He stood in a sitting room in Seongdong and told a man of sixty-eight that the four buyers were going to be told.

There are four buyers and he can reach three.

---

The fourth is the man he is looking for.

That is not a technicality and he is not going to let himself have it as one. He said a thing in a room that will be three quarters true, and Mr Byun will go to his grave not knowing which quarter, and the only person who is ever going to notice is Georgij.

He wrote it in the notebook under the date, in four words, and did not soften any of them.

---

Mrs Sunwoo received him in the same room, at the same time of day, with the same lamp on.

She did not stand up this time and she said so.

"I stood up in March because I did not know what you were and it seemed safer. Sit down and tell me why you have come back, and do not do the thing where you say one sentence and stop, because I am eighty-one and it was charming once."

---

"His name is Byun," said Georgij. "He kept the register for twenty-six years and he left in November. He took your money in an envelope from a man you sent, three years ago, and he did not do the thing you paid for and he did not intend to."

---

Mrs Sunwoo sat very still.

"Say the rest of it."

"There were four of them and you are one. He could not remember which four when I put it to him, and he was not being difficult about it. He was trying."

---

She did not say anything for a while.

---

"Where is he," she said.

"Eighth floor of a building in Seongdong with a view of the river that he paid forty per cent extra for. His daughter-in-law's family is in the same building."

---

"That was more than you needed to tell me."

"Yes."

---

She looked at him for a moment.

"You are inviting me to do something."

"I am giving you a fact that belongs to you and that I obtained without asking your permission, and I would have brought it to you if there had been nothing at the end of it at all." He did not look away. "What you do is yours. I have not got a view and I would not give you one if I had."

---

"That is exactly what you said in March," said Mrs Sunwoo, "and it was true in March as well, and I have not decided yet whether that makes it better."

---

She rang the bell and asked for the diary.

---

"I am going to be very slow about this," she said, when it came. "It will take about three weeks and it will not involve one raised voice or one untrue word, and at the end of it he will not be asked to anything for the rest of his life."

"Yes."

"You do not want to know how."

"No. I want you to be able to say afterwards that you did not tell me."

---

Mrs Sunwoo wrote three names in the diary in a hand that has not changed since 1971.

---

"Two things before you go," she said. "The first is that the house on the river is finished and I did not do very much of that. Eleven people did it and I only had to be the first one to say it out loud, and I have been thinking about that since the end of April."

"Say the second."

---

"You did not come here in March to give me anything," she said. "You came here because you needed the house shut, and telling me the truth was the only way to get it, and both of those are the case at once. I worked that out in about four minutes after you left and I have never held it against you."

---

"No," said Georgij. "You should."

"I am eighty-one. I have not the time to hold things against people who tell me the truth. There are so few of them that I would run out of afternoons."

---

He told Annie on the Thursday, in the small room, standing, in the same form he had used with Mrs Sunwoo.

---

"You are aware that I already know all of this," said Annie.

"Yes."

"You have known me since October and you are standing at the corner of my own desk reciting my own dates back to me."

"Yes, Mistress."

---

"Then say why you are doing it, because you have a reason and I would like to hear it before I decide whether it is a good one."

---

"Because you are two of the four," said Georgij. "October and February. If I tell Mrs Sunwoo formally and tell you across a supper table, then you are a different sort of buyer from her, and you are not. You paid the same man the same money for the same thing and he did the same nothing with it."

---

Annie looked at him.

---

"That is a better reason than I expected," she said.

"There is a second one and it is worse."

"Go on."

---

"I told a man in Seongdong that four people would be told. I can reach three." He said it flatly. "Doing all three properly is the only part of that sentence I have any control over, and I would rather do the part I can than decide that the whole thing was approximate."

---

She did not answer that immediately.

---

"Sit down," she said. "You have been standing in doorways for a week and it is beginning to look like a position."

---

He sat down on the floor at the side of the desk.

---

"Now the other thing," said Annie. "You have had it since Wednesday and you have not brought it to me, and that is not the arrangement, because there is not an arrangement any more and you know exactly what I mean."

---

"The owner," said Georgij.

"The owner."

---

He got the notebook out and did not open it, because he has had it by heart since the seventh.

---

"The house had one owner from 2011 until the winter," he said. "A man called Yeom. He is sixty-three. He did not build it and he did not particularly want it. He ran it the way a man runs a thing he inherited, which is to say he did not look at it very much and it made money."

"And in the winter."

"In the winter he sold it to the fund."

---

"And he took a position with them," said Annie.

---

Georgij looked up at that.

---

"You knew."

"I did not know. I have watched four men sell four things to four funds since I was twenty-six, and three of them took a position. A fund does not want the building. It wants the man who knows what is in it." She was not being clever about it. "Say where the position is."

---

"An address in Jung-gu," said Georgij. "Third floor. There are ten companies registered at it and none of them has any staff."

---

The clock in the hall did the thing it does.

---

"That is the address," said Annie.

"That is the address I stopped at on the night of the sixteenth of March, at twenty past one. I did not order the filings for it. I did not send for the register extract, and I left the whole of it exactly as I found it."

---

"And you were right to."

"I was right to. It was the correct thing and I have not changed my mind about one part of it." He put his hands flat on the floor beside him. "And the man who told Byun to put a lot through the book without an invoice is sitting on the third floor of it, and has been since January."

---

Annie did not say anything for a long moment.

---

"Say what you want to do," she said, "and do not put it as a question, because you decide these now and I am not going to let you make me do it by asking."

---

"I want to go at the address."

---

"Then say the thing you are not saying."

---

"The last time I stood in front of that structure I behaved correctly," said Georgij, "and behaving correctly in front of a man who reads arrangements is the same as signing my name, and you told me that in December and again in March and you were right both times." He did not soften it. "Backing away was invisible. Going at it will not be. There is no version where I do this and stay the man he cannot see."

---

"No."

"So the question is not whether it is dangerous. It is whether I am prepared to stop being the only thing about me that has been useful since March."

---

Annie got up and went to the window.

---

"You have had seven weeks of being the one man in Seoul he cannot see," she said, "and it has bought you a woman in Ulsan, a broker in Jung-gu, a registrar in Seongdong and a house that no longer exists. Say what it has not bought you."

---

"Him."

"It has not bought you him. It was never going to." She turned round. "Invisibility is a thing you spend. It is not a thing you keep, and a man who keeps it for ever has spent his whole life not being seen and has nothing at the end of it except that."

---

Georgij sat on the floor and did not move.

---

"That is not permission," he said.

"No. It is not permission and it is not an instruction, and you are going to have to decide it, and you are going to have to do it in a room with nobody in it but you." She came back to the desk. "But you asked me a fortnight ago to put the ceiling back on and I said no, and I am not going to spend the first difficult one pretending I have not got an opinion."

"You have got an opinion."

"I have got an opinion, and it is that you have been carrying a very expensive thing around for seven weeks in case it came in useful, and that it is the fourteenth of May and it has not."

---

He sat with that for a while.

---

"There is one more thing you should have before you decide," said Annie. "It is not going to help and I am going to give it to you anyway. It is the second piece I have taken off that shelf since December, and I would like you to notice that I am counting."

"I have noticed."

---

"The man you are looking for does not own anything at that address either," she said. "Not one share of one company on that floor. He is not on any of it and he never has been."

---

"Then what is he."

"He is the reason ten companies with no staff are on the third floor of a building in Jung-gu," said Annie, "and there is no document anywhere in this country that says so, and there never will be, and that is what you are proposing to walk towards."
