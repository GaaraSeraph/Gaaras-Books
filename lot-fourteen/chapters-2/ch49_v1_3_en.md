# Chapter 49: What he asked her
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.3 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Ninety-Two · Wednesday 22 July

He went back to Mrs Bae on the Wednesday, in the afternoon, at the hour when the front of a restaurant belongs to whoever is standing in it and nobody else.

---

Annie had not told him to go and had not told him not to, and had said one sentence about it at breakfast which he had thought about the whole way there.

"You are going because it is correct," she had said, "and it is correct, and it is also the third time this month you have gone somewhere because the alternative was sitting in this house knowing about it."

---

Mrs Bae saw him through the glass before he reached the door, and did not look surprised, and did not look pleased.

"You have come back," she said.

"I have."

"Then you know."

"I know that somebody rang him," said Georgij, "and that it was you, and I have not come to say anything about that, and if you want me to leave before I say the rest, say so now and I will."

---

Mrs Bae put both hands on the desk with the book on it and left them there.

"Say the rest."

"You are not in any trouble and you are not going to be." He stood on the customer side and did not lean. "There is nothing you have done that anybody could put in front of anybody. You told a customer of nine years that a stranger had asked about him. There is not a court, a newspaper or a person in this world who would call that anything at all."

"That is not what I have been awake about."

"No. I know it is not." He kept his hands at his sides. "You have been awake because you gave me something first and him something second, and neither of them felt like the wrong thing at the time, and both of them cannot be right."

---

Mrs Bae looked at him for a moment and then, for the first time, sat down.

"You are a strange sort of man to have in my building twice," she said.

"Yes."

"Sit down, then. I am not going to talk about this looking up at somebody."

---

She made tea neither of them drank, which is what people do when they need four minutes.

"I telephoned him on the Thursday," she said. "Not the day you came. The day after."

"Say why the day after."

"Because on the Wednesday night I decided I was not going to, and I was very satisfied with myself about it, and then I woke up at four and I was not satisfied any more." She turned the cup a quarter turn and did not drink from it. "Nine years. He asked after my sister when she was ill. I do not know how he knew she was ill."

---

Georgij did not say anything to that, because there was nothing to say that would not have been a knife.

"Say what you said to him."

"That a man had come and described him, accurately, and asked me for the name on the booking, and that I had given it." Her chin was level. "All of it. I did not leave any of it out and I did not tell him I was sorry, because I was not going to be sorry to him about a thing I had done to him."

"That is more than most people manage."

"I have kept a book in pencil for twenty-two years. I do not need a lesson from you about telling people the truth in the correct order."

"No," said Georgij. "You do not."

---

She almost smiled at that and did not, and put the cup down.

"Now ask me the question you came for, because you have been in my chair for six minutes and you have not asked it, and I know what it is."

"What did he say?"

---

Mrs Bae took a moment, and when it came it came in order, the way it does from somebody who has been over it.

"He was quiet for about three seconds. I counted them afterward, lying there, because there was nothing else to count." She put one hand flat. "Then he thanked me. Properly, the way he does. He said I had done exactly the right thing and that he would have been sorry if I had not rung."

"Go on."

"Then he asked me one question. Only one." She looked at the desk. "He asked me what the man had looked like."

---

Georgij sat very still.

"Say it exactly."

"He said: what did he look like. And I told him, and he said thank you." Mrs Bae lifted her hand and put it down again. "And then he asked whether my knee had been better this summer, because he asked about it in the winter, and I said it had, and he said he was glad, and he rang off."

---

The front of a restaurant with eleven tables did the ordinary things it does.

"That is the part," said Mrs Bae.

"I know."

"He was not pretending, and that is what I have been awake about, and I would like to say it out loud to somebody who understands what I mean." Her voice did not change at all. "He asked about my knee after I had told him a thing that must have frightened him, and he was not doing it to seem calm. He was glad about my knee. Both of those were the same man in the same minute."

"Yes."

"Then tell me what he is, since you know."

---

Georgij thought about it properly, because she had earned an answer and because he wanted to hear whether it held when it was said aloud.

"He is a man who is kind to everybody who is not the point," he said. "For nine years you have not been the point. Nothing he has done in this room was for anything. That is why you would put your hand in a fire for him, and it is also why he is worth what he is worth to the man he works for."

---

Mrs Bae took that without flinching.

"And somewhere there is a person who is the point."

"There is."

"Is she alive?"

---

Georgij did not answer straight away, and the not answering went across the desk exactly as it was.

"I think so," he said. "I cannot prove it and I am not going to sit here and tell you a comfortable thing I cannot prove."

---

She nodded once, slowly.

---

He had two more things to say and he said them standing, because a man who is about to ask nothing should not be sitting down comfortably while he does it.

"If he rings again, tell him whatever he asks and do not be careful about it," said Georgij. "That is not a tactic and it costs me. It is what will keep this a thing that happened to him and not a thing that happened to you."

"And the second."

"You are the only person in this who has done anything twice." He said it flatly. "Everybody else in five weeks has told me one thing and gone back to their day. You told me, and then you told him, and both times you did it on your own and neither time did anybody make you. I would like it said in this room that I noticed."

---

Mrs Bae looked at him for a long moment.

"Do not come back here," she said. "Not because of any of this. Because you are going to want to, in about a month, when something has gone wrong and I am the only person in the city who will still talk to you. I do not want to be that for you."

"No," said Georgij. "You should not be."

"Then we understand each other."

---

At the door he stopped, which he does when there is one more thing, and she was already looking at him as though she had been waiting for it.

"He counted three seconds," he said. "You said you counted them afterward."

"I did."

"Then he took three seconds to decide what to ask you, and out of everything a man in his position could have asked, he asked what I looked like." Georgij had his hand on the frame. "He did not ask you what I wanted. He did not ask you whether I had given a name, or who I worked for, or whether I had been to any other kitchen, or whether I had asked about anybody besides him. He asked one question that only helps him."

---

Mrs Bae did not follow it, and said so with her face and not her mouth.

"He is not running anything," said Georgij. "A man running something asks four questions. A man on his own asks one."

* * *

He told Annie in the small room before dinner, in order, and it took under three minutes.

---

She listened to the whole of it and then went and stood at the corner of the desk where the twelve stems were, four days old now and beginning to be four days old.

"He asked about her knee," she said.

"He asked about her knee."

"Then you were right on Saturday and now you have got it from a second room." Annie did not touch the flowers. "He has not told anybody. He is standing in the middle of an eleven-year arrangement holding a thing that would cost him everything to hand over. He will go on holding it for exactly as long as he believes he can manage it by himself."

"That is a seam," said Georgij.

"That is a seam." She turned round. "And I would like you to say out loud, once, tonight, what you are going to do with it. You have known since Saturday and you have not said it, and I have watched you not say it for four days."

---

Georgij put both hands on the back of the chair.

"I do not know yet," he said. "That is the true answer and I would give you a better one if I had it."

"That is the true answer and it is not the whole of it."

"No." He looked at the flowers rather than at her. "The whole of it is that there is a man out there who has been kind to a woman about her knee for nine years. I am going to use that, and I have not worked out which end of it I am going to use."

---

Annie let that stand in the room for a moment.

"Write it down," she said.

"Which part?"

"That sentence, in those words, tonight, before you have had time to make it sound better." She picked her pen up and did not use it. "You have started writing the tidy version again. Twice this month. I am not going to keep noticing it out loud for you."

---

He wrote it before dinner, on the first page, in the words he had used, and did not improve it.

---

*There is a man who has asked a woman about her knee for nine years and meant it, and I am going to use it, and I have not decided which end.*

---

And underneath, because he had promised himself in June that the page would hold both halves.

---

*Mrs Bae, and I have taken her off page two and put her on the third list, and she is the only person on it who did something twice.*

*She has told me not to come back and she is right, for a reason I would not have thought of. She can see the month I am about to have more clearly than I can.*
