# Chapter 37: A piece of paper somewhere in the world
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.2 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Fifty-Five · Monday 15 June

Mrs Seo brought the post in at nine and put one envelope apart from the rest, on the corner of the tray. It is not a thing she does unless she has already looked at the hand it is written in.

---

"That one is not for the house," she said, and put the rest of the post down without comment.

---

Georgij looked at it. His own name, block letters, no return address, a Gwangyang postmark, and the ink still slightly uneven where the pen had needed a second start.

---

"Today is the fifteenth," he said.

"Yes," said Mrs Seo. "It is also a Monday, if that means anything to you."

---

It meant a great deal to him, more than the date alone could account for, and he did not say so.

---

He took the envelope to the small room and did not open it straight away. He sat with it on the desk for a while. Mr Hwang had asked for a thing he never agreed to, and this was either the answer to a question Georgij had left open on purpose, or it was something else entirely. He did not want to find out which by rushing it.

---

Inside was one sheet, folded twice, in the same hand as the six words in the exercise book.

---

*On the ninth of January I sat in a room on the third floor of the house on the river and held out a book I had bought myself. I told the man in that room what I had kept and why. He read none of it and asked for none of it, and he did not put his hand on it.*

*He was correct about the desk. He was wrong about the book. I am writing this so that somewhere it says which was which.*

*Jeon Mi-ja*

*15 June*

---

He read it twice, the second time slower, the way Annie reads things that matter, because it seemed the right way to read it.

There was no third reading. Some things do not improve with a third pass, and this was one of them, and he knew that the moment the second reading ended.

---

She had dated it the fifteenth. Her first morning at a desk that would pay her properly, in a firm with an owner's name written on the first page.

She had used part of that morning to finish a piece of business four months old that owed her nothing and cost her something to close.

---

Georgij put the letter in the drawer that does not lock, because it was not the sort of thing that needed a lock, and went to find Annie.

---

She was at the desk with the ledgers from the Gwangyang firm's first week already in front of her, which meant somebody there had sent them the moment there was anything to send.

---

"She started today," he said.

"I am aware."

---

He put the letter down in front of her.

---

Annie read it without picking it up.

---

"You are not going to give this to Hwang," she said. It was not a question.

---

"No. She told me I would keep it and not use it." He kept his hands at his sides. "I have thought about what using it would mean, and handing it to the man it is about is exactly that, whatever the reason I told myself for doing it."

---

"Even the kind reason," said Annie.

"Especially the kind reason. He asked for this because he wanted to be forgiven for something, and if I hand it to him, I am the one deciding that he has earned it, and that is not mine to decide. It was never going to be mine."

---

Annie looked at the letter for a moment longer.

---

"Then what was it for."

---

"It exists," said Georgij. "That was the whole of what he asked for, and I did not understand it until this morning. He did not want it read by anybody in particular. He wanted it to be true and written down, in case the only version of the ninth of January that survives is the one in his own head, which he has already told me he does not trust."

---

Annie sat back.

---

"You could have told him that yourself and saved her the morning."

---

"I could have," said Georgij, "and it would have been worth nothing, because it would have been my word about her instead of hers. A man who kept everything for twenty years believes paper. I gave him a piece of paper. I did not give him mine to give, and that is the difference between doing the thing correctly and doing it quickly."

---

Annie looked at him for a moment the way she looks at a figure she has already checked twice and is checking a third time out of habit rather than doubt.

---

"You have spent a great deal of this year learning the difference between those two," she said.

---

"I have spent a great deal of this year learning that I did not use to know there was one."

---

Something in Annie's face settled, the way it does when an answer has met whatever she was testing it against and held.

---

"Read me the last line again," she said.

---

"He was correct about the desk. He was wrong about the book. I am writing this so that somewhere it says which was which."

---

"That is the entire dispute of this business in two sentences," said Annie, "and she wrote it before her coffee had gone cold, on her first morning at a job she has every reason to have spent thinking about something else."

---

"Yes."

---

She turned back to the ledgers, and then stopped, and looked at him again.

---

"You are going to want to know how her first day went."

---

"I am not going to ask you to tell me."

---

"I know you are not," said Annie. "That is why I am telling you anyway. The nephew who ran it out of a shoebox for three years handed her the keys to a cabinet at half past nine and by eleven she had found four things in it he had never noticed were missing. Nobody there yet knows why she was hired. They only know that the woman who arrived this morning has apparently been doing this since before some of them were born, and that she does not raise her voice."

---

Georgij stood very still for a moment.

---

"She is going to be very good at this," he said.

"She was always going to be very good at this," said Annie. "The only thing that was ever in question was whether anybody would let her be."

---

He went to the door and stopped there, the way he does when there is one more thing.

---

"Georgij," said Annie, before he could say it.

"Yes."

---

"Whatever it is, it can wait until this evening. Go and do something today that is not this."

---

He went, and did not argue with it, and found, on the way down the corridor, that for once she was entirely right about what the morning needed.

---

What the morning needed, it turned out, was the other debt.

---

He had not opened the file on Sang-hoon's four decisions since the day in the rain. The man and the building had eaten every hour he had, and it had felt, each time he thought about it, like the smaller of the two obligations. Sitting with a letter that had cost a woman her first coffee of a new job made the size of that excuse plain to him.

---

He took the thin folder down to the small room and spread out what little there was.

It was the same desk he had sat against, on the floor, on the night he had told Annie everything and then fallen asleep before she had finished reading it.

Eight years of dinners. No guest list survives any of them, because a man who gives dinners in his own house does not need caterers who keep one. What survives instead is Sang-hoon's own calendar, which Georgij had copied by hand in the week after the launch and never gone back to, four entries marked only with a single letter and a time.

---

He read the four letters again, this time with the flower slips and the Byun ledger fresh enough in his head to see them differently.

---

*S. 8pm.* March, four years ago. The same spring, Georgij now saw, as the auction where lot six was kept open until midnight with no clearing behind it.

*H. 7:30.* A late August, three years later. Six weeks before Hwang was brought in to a house that was already dying and did not know it yet.

*K. Late.* No year at all, which meant Sang-hoon himself had not thought it worth dating when he wrote it down, which in turn meant that at the time it had seemed like nothing.

*Y. Dinner, then cards.* The most recent, eleven months old.

---

Four letters. Four decisions Sang-hoon has never been able to account for.

---

He tried the obvious approach first and abandoned it inside ten minutes. Half of Seoul's shipping trade has a surname that starts with one of the four letters, and a list built that way would run to hundreds of names before lunch and be worth nothing at the end of it. Byun had taught him that much in May without meaning to: a letter is not a lead. It is the shadow of one, and shadows point in the direction of a light he still had to find.

---

What he had instead was four evenings, and four moods attached to them in Sang-hoon's own hand. A man who writes *Late* instead of a time is telling you something about how the night went, even if he does not know he is doing it.

*8pm* is a dinner that started on time and was expected to.

*7:30* is early, which for Sang-hoon means important enough to arrive rested for.

*Late* is a man who did not want to be there and delayed as long as good manners allowed.

*Dinner, then cards* is the only one with two parts, which meant it went on past whatever the food was for.

---

Georgij sat with the page for a long time and did not manage to turn any of the four into a name. He admitted to himself, in the notebook and nowhere else, that this was going to take longer than four days had ever been going to buy him.

---

He wrote one line before he closed the folder.

*S, H, K, Y. Not enough letters to be a coincidence and not enough to be an answer either. Ask Sang-hoon what the letters stood for before I guess wrong.*

---

It was not the morning Annie had meant when she sent him away from the desk.

It was also, he thought, closing the folder, exactly the sort of morning she would have approved of if she had known which direction he had taken it in. He decided, on his way back up the corridor, that he would tell her so at dinner and let her disagree with him if she wanted to.

She did not disagree with him at dinner. She asked to see the four letters, read them once, and told him which of the four she would have chased first, and why, and it was not the one he had already half decided on.

She was, he thought later, almost certainly right, and he wrote her reasoning into the notebook before his own, which is not a thing he has always done.
