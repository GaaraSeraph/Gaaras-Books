# Chapter 14: Ask her
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.3 · EN

## Day One Hundred and Ninety-Three · Tuesday 14 April

Mr Kwon has nine trucks and no debt and a yard with a concrete apron that was laid properly. The apron is the first thing Georgij looked at, and the reason he went there on the Tuesday and not the Monday.

A man who lays an apron properly does it once and pays for it and does not talk about it afterwards. That is a whole way of being in the world and it takes about four seconds to read off a floor.

---

He came out himself because there is nobody to send.

"You are the man who has been asking about my drivers."

"No," said Georgij. "Somebody has been asking about your drivers and I am the reason he was, and I am not going to pretend it was anybody else."

---

Mr Kwon looked at him for a moment.

"Come inside then. I have got twenty minutes and a man coming at eleven who is not going to buy anything."

---

The office has a wall of ring binders in date order and a calendar with things written on it, which is a different calendar from the one in the office over the weighbridge.

He did not offer anything to drink and he did not apologise for not offering.

---

"Chairman Woo Jae-sung is going to put seven years of coastal feeder work on this lane," said Georgij. "He is going to do it with five firms and not six. He is going to sign them one at a time, and the first one he signs will set the rate that the other four are offered."

---

Then he stopped.

He had built that on the Sunday, the same way he built the sentence for the house on the hill, and he had taken two things out of it and one of them had been the word *opportunity*.

---

Mr Kwon did not say anything for about eight seconds.

"Say the first part again."

"Seven years. The same paper as the anchor cargo at Yeongjong, drawn by the same man."

"I know what that paper looks like. A cousin of my wife's carries under it." He had not moved. "That paper is why he has a terminal and everybody else has a berth."

---

"Why five."

"Because I am not going to bring him the sixth one."

---

Mr Kwon put both forearms on the desk.

"That is a very careful sentence."

"It is an exact one. I am telling you what I am going to do and not what is going to happen to her, because I do not know the second thing and I would be guessing."

---

"Then why."

---

"Ask her."

---

The room did the thing rooms do.

---

"That is all you are going to say."

"That is all I am going to say about it, and I would like you to notice that I have not said one word against her and I am not going to." Georgij kept his hands where they could be seen. "Ask her. She will tell you the truth, because she does not lie, and you have known that about her for twenty years and it is the only reason this is going to work."

---

Mr Kwon sat back.

"You are a very unpleasant man."

"Today, yes."

---

"I do not know what you are," he said. "You came in a car that costs more than my house. You knew about my apron before you knew about my trucks. You will not say what you want and you will not say who you are, and I have been in a room with two men in my life who did that and both of them were selling something."

---

"I have no title," said Georgij. "No company, no salary, no account, and nothing at all that I can sign. There is no piece of paper anywhere in this country with my name on it and a position underneath it."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the whole of the answer. There is nothing about me to check." He did not soften it. "Chairman Woo will confirm every word I have said to you about the seven years and about the five. He will not confirm anything about me, because there is nothing to confirm, and if he tries he will say something vague and you will hear him do it."

---

Mr Kwon looked at him for a long moment.

Then he picked up the telephone on the desk, which is a real telephone with a cord, and he did not go into another room to do it.

---

It took nine minutes.

Georgij sat where he was and looked at the ring binders and listened to one half of it, which is the half a man can afford to let somebody hear.

He heard Woo say the number of years, twice, because Mr Kwon made him say it twice.

He heard the phrase *a young man who works for somebody I do business with*.

He heard *I am not going to tell you any more than that*.

And after something he could not hear at all, he heard *I would not believe me either*.

He heard Mr Kwon laugh at that, once, and it was not a friendly laugh but it was a real one.

---

"He says the rate is real," said Mr Kwon, putting it down.

"It is real."

"He also says that if I ask him about you again he will hang up on me, and that I should ask you, and that you will tell me the truth and it will not help."

"Both of those are correct."

---

Mr Kwon got up and went to the window, which looks at nine trucks and room for eleven.

"My partner wants out," he said. "He has wanted out since his wife died and he cannot go, because there is nobody on this coast to sell his half to and I cannot buy it with what we make on this lane in a year."

"I know."

"Of course you know. Somebody asked my drivers." He did not turn round. "With seven years of that paper he can go. Somebody in Busan buys the half at a price that is a real price. He goes and lives near his daughter. And I stop watching a man of sixty-three come to work every day for a reason he stopped having."

---

Georgij did not say anything to that at all.

There was nothing available that was both true and useful, and everything available that was merely useful would have been a sale.

---

What he did instead was write four words in the notebook in the car, and they are the second half of a thing he said to Annie in the small room on the third of April.

Seven years of that paper does not only feed a firm. It makes a firm worth something. It makes a half of a firm saleable, and it does that to five firms inside a fortnight.

And Jang found the way into every one of them, in four pages, without being asked for it.

---

The man with nine trucks has a partner of sixty-three who has wanted out since his wife died and cannot go, because there is nobody on that coast who buys anything.

The man with three trucks has seven months left on a loan against his own yard and a son whose term begins in September.

The two brothers have not spoken since 2019 and would each of them sooner sell to a stranger than agree with the other about anything, and the invoice clerk has understood that for four years.

The fourth has been quietly moving work off that lane since February, which is a man who has already decided to go and is waiting to be able to afford it.

And Cho, who is forty, took a firm from his father in the autumn, and has spent six months finding out what his father actually was, and has not wanted it since about January.

---

Five men and five separate afternoons, and on every one of those afternoons there is going to be exactly one buyer on that coast with money in her hand.

She will pay a real price each time, because a bad price is a thing a man tells people about for twenty years. Every one of the five will believe it was the best fortnight of his working life, and every one of them will be right.

---

*Not customers. Not an arrangement.* All five, and not four, and not the two easy ones.

That is what he promised her on the third of April. It was not a figure of speech, and Woo's seven years are what make it possible, and Woo will never know that part. There is nothing in it that costs him anything and no reason to tell a man a thing he cannot use.

---

"You have not offered me anything," said Mr Kwon after a while.

"No."

"Why not."

"Because there is nothing I could offer you that you will not do for your own reasons inside a week. And because a man who takes something from me on a Tuesday spends the rest of his life wondering what it cost." Georgij stood up. "You have got a man coming at eleven who is not going to buy anything."

---

At the door Mr Kwon said the thing that Georgij had been waiting for since the apron.

"The constitution," he said. "Ours. There is a clause in it about who may speak for the members in a dealing with a carrier."

"There is."

"I have never read it. Twenty years and I have never read it, because there has never been a carrier."

---

"There is one now," said Georgij.

---

He got as far as the car.

"One thing," said Mr Kwon from the doorway. "You said ask her. You did not say what to ask her about."

"No."

"Because if you had, it would be your question."

"Yes."

---

Mr Kwon stood in his own doorway with his hands in his pockets and looked at him for a moment.

"I said you were an unpleasant man and you said today. Was that true."

---

There it was, and Mr Kwon had built it well and had probably not meant to.

"No," said Georgij. "I have been this for about three weeks and I do not know yet whether it goes away."

---

He got in the car and Mr Ku pulled out onto a road with a fuel depot on one side of it, and neither of them said anything for a long time.

---

Then Mr Ku said something on the expressway, about forty minutes out, which he has not done since October.

"That was a good yard."

"It was."

"My wife's brother drove for a firm with a yard like that. Twelve years, in Gimhae." He was watching the road and not the mirror. "Then it was a different firm and the yard was the same and he was not there any more."

---

Georgij looked at the back of his head for a moment.

"Is that a question."

"No," said Mr Ku.

---

He did not say anything else for the rest of it, and he was not sulking, and at the house he got out and opened the door the way he does.

Georgij carried that up the drive with him and has not put it down since.

---

He did not go and tell her about the day.

That is the arrangement now, and it is eleven days old, and this is the first week since October in which he has done a week's work and said nothing at all about it to anybody.

It turns out to be quieter than he had expected, and not in the way he had expected.

* * *

The four remaining firms did not need a visit and did not get one.

Woo's lawyer wrote to all five of them on the Thursday, on the same day, in the same words, and asked each of them the same three questions. What tonnage can you commit. What is your insurance position. **And: who is authorised to enter into an agreement on your behalf, and has anybody done so in the last six months.**

That is the third question every lawyer asks and nobody thinks about, and it went out to five men who had never in their lives been asked it.

---

By the Friday afternoon three of them had gone and read the constitution.

By the Saturday two of them had telephoned Nam Byung-hee.

---

Georgij does not know what she said on either call and did not try to find out.

He does not need it. She told them the truth, because that is what she does, and she did not soften it, because she has never once in twenty years softened anything.

It will have taken her under a minute each time.

---

The man outside the yard reported on the Sunday morning and had nothing, which is what Georgij had expected and had asked for anyway.

Nobody came. Nobody has come to that yard in a week who was not delivering something or driving for her. There is no car on that road that does not belong on it.

---

One line at the bottom of it, put there because the man is good at his job and not because anybody wanted it.

On the Saturday evening she did not go home. The light in the office over the weighbridge was on until half past ten and the yard was empty. At some point in the middle of it she came out and stood on the apron. She looked at four trucks for a while, and then she went back in.

---

By the Monday all five had written back to the lawyer, and four of the five had answered the third question in more words than it needed.

The fifth was Mr Kwon. He answered the third question in nine words. Then he telephoned the chair of his association and asked for a meeting of the members. In writing. Under a clause he had read for the first time on the Friday morning, at his own desk, with the door shut.
