# Chapter 15: One word, and it was true
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.2 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Two · Thursday 23 April

The meeting was at two in the afternoon in a room over a bank in Ulsan that the association has used since 2006 and pays four hundred thousand won a year for.

Georgij was in Seoul, at the desk with the east window, and did not go anywhere near it.

There was no version of that room with him in it that was not the whole thing ruined. He had known that since the fourteenth. He had still spent about four minutes on the Wednesday night finding out whether there was one.

---

What he did with the afternoon was the other half.

---

A bound catalogue went out in the first days of March to every buyer that house has. One page of it was photocopied and was on a desk in Ulsan on the fourteenth.

Between those two facts there is a machine that somebody used, and machines leave marks, and the marks are not on the page.

---

The house uses a mailing bureau in Mapo, because a firm of that size does not put six hundred parcels in the post itself and has not since the nineties.

A bureau of that kind runs on a spreadsheet with an address list in it, and it keeps the list, because the client always comes back next season and nobody wants to key it again.

He had that list at about eleven on the Thursday morning.

It took him a little over two hours and he did not enjoy it as much as he had expected to, which he noted and did not examine.

---

Six hundred and forty names.

A hundred and nine of them are companies with no natural person on the line at all, and those he set aside. A man who is careful about four layers of ownership does not have an auction catalogue posted to a shell.

Of the rest, one is Annie. One is Mrs Sunwoo. One is a woman in Daegu who has bought porcelain there twice a year since 1991 and has never bought anything else.

---

And that is where it stopped, because six hundred names is not a list of suspects, it is a telephone directory.

He sat and looked at it and understood that he had been going at it from the wrong end for four days.

---

The right end is not who received a catalogue.

The right end is who received a catalogue and was also at a lunch given by the port authority in the first week of January.

That is a room with six men in it, and Woo gave him five of the names at a car door on the ninth of April, and he has had them in a notebook for a fortnight.

---

He put the five names next to six hundred and forty at about half past one.

Three of the five are on the mailing list.

---

Three is not an answer. Three is a shape.

All three of them are exactly the sort of man who would be at that lunch and would also buy at that house, which is another way of saying that nothing has been eliminated. What he has is that the two lists touch, and that they touch in three places and not in none.

He wrote the three on a separate page and put the six hundred and forty in a drawer, and he did not look at either again that day.

* * *

The telephone went at twenty past four.

---

"It is done," said Mr Kwon.

"Say it in the order it happened, and take as long as you like."

---

There was a pause of the sort a man makes when he has been asked for something he did not know he wanted to give.

---

"Six of us and her. She chaired it, because she is the chair. She opened it herself and read out the request for the meeting and my name at the bottom of it. She read it in the same voice she reads everything in."

"Go on."

"Then I asked her. I had it written down and I did not need it." He stopped. "I asked whether she had written to anybody on association letterhead in the last six months."

---

"And."

"She said yes."

---

"Straight away," said Mr Kwon. "Not a second. Not one of those things where a person takes a breath first."

---

"Then I asked her what was in it and she said that was hers and she was not going to say. And Gil asked whether it concerned the business of the association."

---

Georgij did not say anything.

---

"She said no."

---

"That is all she said," said Mr Kwon. "One word. She could have said it was association business and none of us would have been able to prove otherwise for a month, and by then it would have been over. She had a way out and it was standing open and she looked straight at it and said no."

"She does not lie."

"She does not lie. I have known that for twenty years and I have never once had it cost me anything." He said that part quite slowly. "It cost her everything in about a second and a half and she did not even look surprised."

---

The room over the bank in Ulsan did the thing rooms do.

---

"After that it was not really a discussion," said Mr Kwon. "Somebody has to say the words, so I said them. That she had used the one thing the six of us own together, for something that was not ours, and had not told us for five weeks."

"And she said."

"She said that was correct."

---

"Four for. One against. And Cho abstained."

---

"Cho is the man of forty," said Georgij.

"Cho is the man of forty who has not said one word at a meeting since October, and he said one today." Mr Kwon's voice did something. "He said that she had written to six people on the first of every month for twenty years, and that nobody in that room had ever once written back." Mr Kwon stopped. "And that he did not think a person should lose everything for the first thing she ever asked for herself."

---

"And then he abstained."

"And then he abstained. He said the words and then he put his hand down and did not vote. I have been thinking about it for two hours and I cannot decide whether it was the most honest thing I have ever seen a man do or the most useless."

---

"Both," said Georgij. "They are not in competition."

---

He did not say the rest of it.

A man who says a true thing in a room and then does not act on it has made himself feel better and changed nothing whatever. It is the commonest thing in the world. He has used it in nine rooms since October and has never once had to work for it.

---

"Who is chair," he said.

"Me. I did not want it and there was nobody else and it took four minutes."

---

"And her firm."

"Her firm is a member. It is still a member. Nobody moved to expel her and I would have voted against it if they had." Mr Kwon said it flatly. "She has four trucks and one lane and no work on it. The rest of us are about to have seven years. Everybody in that room understood exactly what that means, and not one of us said it out loud."

---

"No," said Georgij. "They never do."

---

There was a longer pause after that.

---

"You were not there," said Mr Kwon.

"No."

"You were not there and it took forty minutes and every single thing that happened in that room happened because a man came to my yard on a Tuesday and said two words to me."

"Three, if you count the first one."

"Do not do that." It came out harder than the rest of it. "I have got nine trucks and a partner who can go and live near his daughter. I am going to sign that paper on Monday and be pleased about it for about a year. Do not stand at the other end of a telephone and be clever at me about it."

---

"I am sorry," said Georgij.

---

He does not say that often, and he had not planned to, and it came out before he had decided whether it was for Mr Kwon or for himself, which is the reason he almost never says it.

---

"What did she do afterwards."

"She stayed and helped stack the chairs. It is what she has done after every meeting for twenty years and I do not think it occurred to her to do anything else." Mr Kwon stopped. "Then she said goodnight to each of us by name, and she said mine last, and she has never done that before, and she was not being pointed. I do not think she is capable of being pointed."

---

"Thank you," said Georgij.

"I do not want to be thanked by you."

"No."

"I will take the seven years and I will not take the thanks, and I would like that to be the arrangement between us from now on."

"That is the arrangement."

---

He put the telephone down and sat with the chairs for a while, which he had not intended to do.

A woman loses everything at two in the afternoon and then stays behind and stacks the chairs, because it is a Thursday and that is what happens after a meeting, and there is no procedure anywhere for stopping.

---

He has watched a great many people take bad news since October and he can predict most of what they do with the first ten minutes of it.

He did not predict that, and he cannot make it be anything other than what it is, and it does not alter one single thing about the rest of the month.

---

He wrote the date in the notebook after that, and underneath it, in five words: *four for, one against, one abstained.*

He did not write down what Cho said. He could have, and he can still remember the whole of it.

A thing that goes in the notebook stops having to be carried. That is the entire point of the notebook and he has known it since October, and he left it out anyway.

---

He went down at about seven.

Annie was in the small room and the door was open and she looked up when he came into it, and he had not been into that room to say anything since the third of April.

"It is not finished," he said. "That was the first half and it took twenty days."

"I know. I have had the Yeouido people watching the filings since Tuesday and I did not tell you, because you would have wanted to know why and the answer is that I wanted to see it happen." She put down what she was holding. "Sit down and do not report to me. You do not have to and I have not asked."

---

He sat down on the floor at the side of the desk, which is where he sits.

"There is a man called Cho," he said, "and he is forty, and he said the only true thing in that room, and then he put his hand down and did not vote."

---

Annie did not answer for a moment.

"Do you want me to say something about that."

"No, Mistress."

"Then I will not," she said, and went back to what she was doing, and left her hand where it was for the next hour.
