# Chapter 5: The east side
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.2 · EN

## Day One Hundred and Fifty-Nine · Wednesday 11 March

He took the coat off and put it over the arm of the chair by the door, and then he did the difficult part first, because leaving it any later would have turned it into something.

"Jang was on the gravel," he said. "He does not stand out there, and knowing where the cars of this house go is his job. There is no way for me to show you which of us was going to say it first."

"No."

"I am not going to try. I would only be asking you to take my word for the one thing my word is no good for."

---

Annie was standing at the window with the curtain not drawn and the drive dark behind her.

"The order is not what is wrong with today," she said. "Give me all of it."

---

He gave it in order and it took twelve minutes.

The catalogue off the desk in the small room. Four references and three seasons and no column for who paid. Two of them the fourth of October, four lines apart. Two of them older, one three years back, one the year before that.

Then the outside of an evening. A firm in Jung-gu that keeps its dockets for seven years on account of a tax dispute it lost. A drop that is the same street four times in three years. A company with two rooms and a secretary and nobody behind it who had been trying very hard.

Then the name.

---

"Sunwoo," said Annie.

"On the older of the two. I have not got the other one."

"You will not." She did not say it as a challenge. "Go on."

---

"The fourth is not there at all. No car, no dinner, no third piece of paper. The chair firm invoiced for the room and the florist invoiced for the room and that is the whole of the evening."

"And you put that down to the records."

"I put it down to the age of the records on Saturday, and I wrote myself a line saying so." He did not decorate it. "It was reasonable and it is probably wrong. Nobody eats and nobody parks. That is not an old evening, that is an arranged one."

---

Annie let that stand for a moment, and then she asked her first real question, which was not about any of it.

"What did you pay Park Sang-hoon with."

---

"Hanseong. All of it. Why he signed after he had seen the wall, and how the report was put in front of him, and what it was in him that could be aimed."

"Every part of it."

"Every part. He had two and a half months on it and he had the half that anybody could get. I gave him the other half in a corridor in about six minutes."

---

She did not say anything about that at all, which she had not in February either, and he had learned by now not to take the silence as either of the two things it looks like.

---

"And in return."

"There is a desk in settlement. There is always somebody in settlement, because it is the only desk in a building of that kind that touches every line and gets paid the least for it." Georgij kept his hands still. "I have a name. It is not written anywhere except in the book in my coat, and the coat is on that chair."

"How long."

"A month. They have a new man and he has been through the room and through the people, and the ones who were worth anything were worth it because nobody had ever looked at them."

---

Annie turned round from the window then.

"So you have three days of records, one name out of two, a hole where the fourth ought to be, and a door that shuts in April."

"Yes."

"And it cost you Sang-hoon knowing that this house is interested in a book by the river."

"Yes."

---

"Now tell me about the desk at the door."

---

There it was, and he had known since the gravel that it was coming, and he had spent the drive deciding not to have an answer ready.

"There is a book at those things," he said. "Everybody who comes writes his name in it, and puts an envelope in the box, and the amount goes in the second column later in a different hand. I wrote my name. I had nothing for the box and I said so, and I asked the man to put a line through the second column."

---

Annie came away from the window and stood on the other side of the desk from him.

"How many men were near enough to hear you."

"Thirteen. Four in front of me and seven behind, and two came up the stair while I was still at the desk."

"And how many of them will have it in a month."

"The man at the desk will have it for years. He has never had to draw that line before and it is a story he can tell at a table." He did not stop. "Of the other twelve, most of them by Friday. And two of them already knew who I was when I said my name, because they stopped talking."

---

"You have spent nine days making yourself into the one thing in this city that has no line and no owner and no date," said Annie. "This afternoon you wrote your name in a register with a date on it, at the door of a room with half that trade in it. Then you made certain that every man within hearing would remember which name it was."

---

"Yes."

"Do not agree with me. Tell me why you did it."

---

"Because the other version has a figure in it." He said it in the flattest voice he owns. "An envelope is money that is not mine, out of a house that is yours, and it goes into the second column under my name in a book that is kept. That is a number next to me, in ink, with a date. A line through it is nothing at all."

---

Annie put one hand down on the desk.

"That part is correct," she said. "It is the only part of today that is."

---

"In December you gave six hours to a woman who does not belong to you," said Georgij.

"I know what I did in December."

"You made a condition about how it was to be done, and the condition was that it must not be a gift, because a gift would have made her poorer and more careful." He did not hurry it. "What it did in the end was not take the threat off her. Nobody can do that. It took away the thing that made her worth threatening. The ground stopped being a dedication and started being a rent, and the third charge on it stopped being a cliff in the spring and became a line in a schedule."

"The east side."

"The east side. That is the whole of the method and it is yours, not mine, and I have been doing nothing else since Thursday afternoon."

---

Annie waited.

"A page with four entries on it and one name known is a page with a queue on it," he said. "A page with four entries on it and all four dull is not worth carrying up a drive. I am not trying to take the book away from anybody. I am trying to make it not worth the postage."

---

"And you could not ask me first."

"No, Mistress. Not could not. Must not."

---

She did not move.

"Say the rest of it."

"The one thing in this that has any value at all is that nobody sent me." Georgij looked straight at her while he said it, because there was no version of it that survived being said to the carpet. "The moment you tell me to go at that register, it is your hand on it. You are the one person alive who cannot touch that book. Everything you do about it is an answer to a question nobody has asked yet."

---

"That is a good argument," said Annie. "Now say the flaw in it, because you have known it since the gravel and you have been walking towards it for twelve minutes."

---

The clock in the hall did the thing it does at the quarter, and neither of them looked at the door.

---

"I am the line," said Georgij.

"Louder."

"There is no such man as the one I have been describing. A page with four dull entries on it is worth nothing. A page with four entries on it, one of them attached to a man who lives in your house, is worth a very great deal. What makes it worth that is not the entry. It is that I have been walking round the city since March asking about it." He did not stop. "I have spent nine days building a tool that only works in the hand of a man nobody owns. Somebody owns me."

---

Annie sat down at last, on the arm of the reading chair, which is not sitting.

"That is why I have not moved since February," she said. "It was not patience and it was not cleverness. There is no move available to me that does not confirm the line matters, and there is no man available to me who is not visibly out of this house."

"There is one thing you could do about it, and I am going to say it once so that it is not sitting in the room."

"Say it."

"You could let me go. Then the man I have been describing exists, and there is no second way of doing this." He said it evenly. "I have looked at it and put it down. I am telling you because you would have found out that I had looked, and then you would have had to wonder for a week whether I put it down."

---

Annie was quiet for a moment.

"My people in Yeouido could have had the dockets in two days," she said. "They would not have written their names anywhere. They are better at this than you are."

"They are much better at it than I am."

"Then give me the reason and make it a real one."

---

"Every one of them arrives from somewhere." Georgij turned his hand over. "They come in through a door and a diary and a fee, and a man like the one in settlement can see all three of those before he has finished standing up. Nobody comes over to them in a corridor."

"And somebody comes over to you."

"Twice he has tried to buy me, and both times he came to me to do it. Today he crossed a corridor to say it, in front of two men who wanted things from him."

---

"So the asset is that other men want you."

"Yes, Mistress."

---

She looked at him for slightly too long, the way Sang-hoon had, and unlike Sang-hoon she said it.

"That is a filthy thing to have to put in a report."

"It is the only true thing I have that is worth anything to you. I would rather it were the records work. It is not."

---

Annie stood up off the arm of the chair.

"Bring me the coat."

---

He brought it and she did not take it. She waited until he had got the notebook out of the inside pocket himself. She waited again until he had opened it at the page. Then she read one line of his handwriting and gave it back without ever touching it.

"Keep it," she said. "It is no safer with me and I am not going to pretend otherwise."

---

"Now the terms, and they are yours and I am giving them back to you." She stood where she was. "A ceiling. Named counterparties. A date after which it is dead and you come and ask me again."

"Mistress."

"The ceiling is that you do not spend yourself again without telling me the price first. Not the result first and the price afterwards, which is what I have had this evening. The price, before it is paid."

---

"And where there is no time to ask?"

"Then you decide, and you tell me the same night, and you tell me what it cost before you tell me what you got." She let that sit. "You have put those terms on other people since October, and you put them on me on Saturday. You know exactly how much they leave a man."

"Enough to work with."

"Enough to work with."

---

"The counterparties are Park Sang-hoon, the desk in settlement, and nobody else without my saying so. The date is the last day of March, because that is when your door shuts and I am not going to pay for a month you do not have."

---

Georgij put the notebook back in the coat.

"And the paper?"

---

Annie went to the drawer on the left of the desk, which he has watched her open perhaps four times, and put a folded document on the blotter and did not push it across.

"Mr Chae has had this since Monday afternoon," she said. "It is narrower than the one in December. It names three things and a month and it will embarrass you in any room that reads it properly."

"Then it is the right one."

"You asked me on Monday and I told you to ask again when there was a room in it." She put two fingers on the fold and left them there. "There is a room in it. There is a door, a name, and four weeks. I am not giving you this because you have earned it this afternoon. Some of this afternoon was very expensive."

"I know what it cost."

"You do not yet. You will in about a month."

---

He did not touch it either, and they left it on the blotter between them, which is where it stayed for the rest of the evening.

---

"The fourth one," said Annie. "The one with no car and no dinner."

"I have nothing on it at all."

"No."

---

And that was all she said about it.

She did not ask him what he thought it meant, and she did not ask him whether he had gone at it twice, and she did not ask him the second question. Annie asks second questions. He stood there with his hands at his sides and listened to her not ask it, and did the arithmetic, and put it where he puts them.

---

"Come here," she said.

---

He went round the desk. She did not have to say the rest of it, and he went down onto the carpet at her feet, which has stopped needing a word between them. Then she put her hand on the back of his neck the way she had on Saturday. This time she did not go past him and did not go on to anything else. After a while his shoulders came down about an inch, and he had not known they were up.

"You wrote your name in a book today," she said, above him.

"Yes."

"Put a line through it, you said."

"Yes."

"That was the correct thing to do and it was quick, and nobody taught you it." Her hand did not move. "I will not say that twice, and you are not to build anything on it."

---

He said nothing at all, which was not a decision.

---

Later, on the landing, he found that he had spent the whole twelve minutes of the report waiting for one particular sentence and that it had not come. Not once, in any form.

She had not asked him whether he would have told her if Jang had been in the kitchen.

She had decided not to find out. On the eleventh of March, about a man who has never said anything untrue in his life. Downstairs on the blotter there was a folded document with four weeks on it and no room in it for a second mistake.
