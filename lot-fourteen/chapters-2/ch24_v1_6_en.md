# Chapter 24: Four people who could see us
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.6 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Thirty · Thursday 21 May

He counted the room on the way in, because he counts rooms.

Nine tables. Six of them taken at twenty-five past twelve. Two exits, one of which is a kitchen and therefore not one. A window on the street side with the blind at half and the light coming in low off a white building opposite.

Mr Yeom's table is the fourth from the door on the left and it has a reserved card on it that has been used a great many times.

---

He is sixty-three and looks about that, and he stood up.

"You came," he said. "I told my wife you would and she said you would not, and she is right about people about seventy per cent of the time, so I am going to enjoy this for a moment."

---

He ordered without looking at anything and told the waiter what Georgij was having as well, and then apologised for it in the same breath and did not offer to change it.

---

The food came in about twelve minutes.

Mr Yeom ate it.

---

He ate it the way a man of sixty-three eats a thing he has eaten at the same table since 2016, which is to say without ceremony and with obvious pleasure. He talked while he did it and did not put his chopsticks down between sentences.

Georgij watched him do it and did not relax by one degree.

---

It rules out one man. That is all it rules out and it is worth having and it is not worth anything else.

"You are not eating," said Mr Yeom.

"I am eating."

"You are moving it about. I have watched a great many people not eat at this table and you are doing it better than most, and I would like to say for the record that I have noticed."

"I will eat it in a minute."

"Then I shall enjoy that too."

---

He put down what he was holding and became a different man in about a second and a half.

"Now," said Mr Yeom. "I sold that house in December to people who had been asking me for it for two years, and I took a position with them, and there is nothing in either of those sentences that anybody could make interesting."

"No."

"And then in March a woman in Ulsan got a page in the post. In April my old house shut. And in May a young man walked into a register office and put his own name on a form about the third floor of my building." He was looking straight at him. "I would like to know what you are, and I am telling you plainly that I would like to know, because I have found that asking works about as often as anything else."

"What did the fund tell you?" said Georgij.

---

Mr Yeom picked his chopsticks back up.

"That is not an answer."

"No. It is the question I am going to answer yours with, and you can have it in that order or not at all."

---

He considered that for a moment and then did the thing that told Georgij more than anything else in the hour.

He answered it.

"They told me to find out and to be pleasant about it," he said. "Those were the two things and there was not a third, and I would like you to understand that I am repeating the whole of the instruction and not the part that suits me."

"Who told you?"

"A man on the telephone whose name I know and which I am not going to give you." He said it comfortably. "Not because of any of this. Because I have worked for seven people in my life and I have never once handed one of them to anybody, and if I started at sixty-three I would not know who I was in the afternoon."

---

Georgij put his chopsticks down.

"That is a good reason," he said, "and it is the first one anybody has given me since March that I am not going to argue with."

"Now yours."

"I work for the woman who owns the two entries," said Georgij. "October and February. She paid your register four times over seven weeks for a deletion and got a strike-through, and the man who took the money is Byun and he could not remember which four buyers he had done it to."

---

Mr Yeom stopped with his chopsticks halfway.

"Four times," he said.

"Four visits. Two entries."

"He told me it was once."

---

That is when Georgij understood that the hour was going to be worth something after all, and it was not going to be worth what he had come for.

"The rest of it," he said.

"He came to me in February of last year," said Mr Yeom. "He was frightened. He said a buyer had been very persistent about a deletion and that he had taken money for it and that he wanted me to know before somebody else told me." He put the chopsticks down properly this time. "He said it was one. He said it had happened once in twenty-six years and would not happen again. I gave him a great deal of trouble about it for about ten minutes and then I let it go, because he had come to me."

"He had come to you."

"That is the whole of why I let it go. A man who tells you a thing you would never have found out has not got anything else in his pocket." Mr Yeom looked at the table. "That was my view for a very long time and I have just watched it stop being one."

"There were four," said Georgij. "October, February, one three years ago and one four years ago."

"The fourth one. Again."

"Four years ago."

---

Mr Yeom did not say anything at all for about six seconds.

"I paid that one," he said.

---

The room did what rooms do, and at the third table from the window a man of about sixty who is not tall put a spoon down beside a bowl he has not touched.

---

Georgij saw it in the edge of his vision and did not turn his head one degree.

"Not like that," he said.

"Somebody asked me for a favour four years ago," said Mr Yeom. "A lot going out with no invoice, entered and taken out again in the spring, nothing written down about who had it. And I gave Byun the fee out of my own pocket in an envelope. The book showed a payment in the ordinary way and nobody in that building ever wondered why there had not been one."

"And you did that for whom."

"For a man who telephoned me and said he was asking on behalf of somebody who would be grateful."

"You did not ask who."

"No," said Mr Yeom, and he said it without any embarrassment at all, "and I would like you to understand exactly why not, because you are going to think it was cowardice and it was not. It was arithmetic."

"The arithmetic."

"A man who telephones you and does not say a name is offering you a thing worth more than a name." He had both hands flat on either side of the plate. "If I ask who, then he tells me, and then I have done a favour for a person and I am owed by a person. If I do not ask, then I am owed by whoever it is, and whoever it is knows that I did not ask, and that is worth about ten times as much and it costs nothing."

"And it paid."

"It paid in December," said Mr Yeom. "Somebody had been asking me for that house for two years. In the winter the price came up by rather more than the house was worth, and there was a position in it for me. I did not have to ask about that either."

---

Georgij sat at the fourth table from the door on the left.

"So you have never known," he said.

"I have never known and I have never wanted to, and I have spent four years being extremely comfortable about that." Mr Yeom looked at him. "And you have just sat down opposite me and told me that a woman in Ulsan is selling trucks, and I find that I am doing arithmetic again and I do not like the answer."

"Do not say the rest of that out loud in this room."

---

Mr Yeom's face changed by about a millimetre.

"Why not?"

"Because you have just told me you do not know who you did it for," said Georgij, "and that is the only thing keeping you comfortable, and it is a great deal safer than knowing."

---

Neither of them said anything for a moment.

"There is one more thing and then I am going to eat this," said Georgij, "because I said I would and because I would like to have been the sort of person who did."

"You are not going to leave before the one more thing."

"The man who telephoned you four years ago. Not the one behind him. The one who made the call. Did he ever say anything that was not necessary."

---

Mr Yeom thought about that properly, which took about twenty seconds, and Georgij let it.

"He said he was glad we had met," said Mr Yeom.

"And had you."

"No. That is why I remember it. I put the telephone down and I said to my wife that a man had just told me it was nice to see me again and I had never laid eyes on him."

---

Georgij ate what was in front of him.

It took about four minutes and it was very good and he could not afterwards have said one thing about it.

---

At the door Mr Yeom shook his hand and did not make anything of it.

"You are going to tell me that I should be worried," he said.

"No."

"Everybody tells me that."

"You have been worried since the words *four years ago* and you have been eating anyway, and I have watched a great many people do the other thing. You do not need me for this part."

---

He went out past six tables.

---

At the third one from the window there is a man of about sixty who is not tall, sitting alone, in the sort of suit that costs a great deal and is designed so that nobody can tell.

There is a bowl in front of him and a spoon beside it. The bowl has not been touched. It has been there for the whole of an hour and ten minutes and it has gone cold.

He was reading something on the table and he did not look up.

---

Georgij did not look at him.

He did not slow down, he did not turn his head, and he did not do the thing a man does with his shoulders when he has decided not to look at something.

He has been practising that since October and it has never once mattered until today.

---

Out on the street he stood for a moment in the low light off a white building and did the arithmetic he had been holding since the third table.

---

There are three possibilities and he can put them in order.

The man is nobody, and a man of sixty with no appetite ordered a bowl and read something.

The man is somebody and did not know Georgij would be there, in which case Georgij has just seen him and he does not know it.

Or the man is somebody, and knew exactly when the table was booked, and came to look at the thing that has been walking round his arrangement since March.

---

He does not know which and he is not going to.

What he knows is that Mr Yeom ate everything in front of him and talked while he did it. And that a man three tables away sat for an hour and ten minutes with a bowl going cold.

Both of those are facts. Only one of them is a fact about Georgij, and he does not know which.
