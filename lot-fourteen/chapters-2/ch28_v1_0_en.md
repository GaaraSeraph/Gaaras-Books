# Chapter 28: He keeps everything
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.0 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Thirty-Seven · Thursday 28 May

There is no book at the door any more.

The counter is there and the pen is gone and the shelf under it has been emptied, and a man who wanted to write his name in that building today would have nowhere to put it.

Georgij stood in front of it for a moment anyway.

---

Three times in eight weeks he wrote his name in a book at a door, and every one of those three has come back to him with interest.

This is the first door he has walked through this year that did not want it.

He found he did not like that either.

---

The building is nearly done. The first floor has no chairs in it. The glass panel at the settlement desk has been taken out of its frame and stood against the wall behind it. The slot for envelopes is a rectangular hole in a piece of plywood. The single chair on the visitor's side is gone.

He looked at the hole for about four seconds.

---

Mr Hwang was on the third floor in a room with two crates and a table and nothing else.

He had his coat on because the heating in that building was turned off on the first, and he was going through a box file page by page, and he did not stop when Georgij came in.

---

"You," said Mr Hwang.

"Yes."

---

Mr Hwang put the page he was holding face down on the table, squared it, and turned round.

"You have got what you came for," he said. "I remember saying it and I remember meaning it, and I have thought about that afternoon more than I am going to tell you."

"I know."

---

"Then say what this is."

---

"I have come to ask you for something," said Georgij, "and I have nothing to give you for it, and I am not going to dress it up. There is no arrangement here and there is nothing at the end of it for you."

---

Mr Hwang stood with one hand on the box file.

---

"That is at least consistent," he said.

---

"I want an address," said Georgij. "The woman who was behind the glass downstairs."

---

Mr Hwang did not go to a box and did not open anything.

"Jeon Mi-ja," he said. "Twenty-three years at that desk. Her last day was the twelfth of May and she came up here at half past four to give me the keys to the drawer, and she would not put them on the table. She put them in my hand."

---

He had it by heart.

---

"You did not have to look that up."

"No," said Mr Hwang. "I have not had to look that up once since the twelfth of May, and I have tried."

---

Georgij stayed near the door.

---

"Why did you do it?"

---

"Because it was correct." Mr Hwang said it without any defence in it at all, which is new. "The house is winding up. There will be no settlements. A settlement desk in a house with no settlements is a salary against nothing, and I put it in the schedule in the second week of April with eleven other posts, and every one of the twelve was correct."

---

"And?"

---

"And I did the twelve at once, in an afternoon, out of a spreadsheet." He looked at the table rather than at Georgij. "I did not go down to that floor. I have been in this building since the autumn and I have been down to that floor twice."

---

Georgij did not say anything at all.

---

"You are going to tell me that is the same thing again," said Mr Hwang.

"No," said Georgij. "You have just told me. Leave it where it is."

---

Mr Hwang turned back to the table and moved the box file about an inch to the left, which did not need doing.

---

"There is a thing you should know before I give you the address," he said, "and I am going to give it to you, and I would like to have said this first so that it is not payment."

---

"Say it."

---

"She came up here on the ninth of January." Mr Hwang put both hands flat on the table. "She sat where you are standing, because there was a chair there then, and she had a school exercise book with her that she had bought herself. She said she had been keeping a list."

---

The room was very cold.

---

"Of what," said Georgij.

"Of everything that went through that desk in twenty-three years that did not clear." He got it out in one piece and it cost him something. "Not fraud. She was careful about that word and I have thought about how careful she was. Things that went out of the room with no paper behind them. She said there were not very many and that she knew all of them."

---

"How many?"

"She did not say and I did not ask her."

---

Georgij stood in a room with two crates in it.

---

"What did you say to her?"

---

"I said that the house was in wind-up," said Mr Hwang, "and that reopening a matter in wind-up requires the administrator's consent. And that as the administrator I was not going to consent to twenty-three years of it in January, because it would not be finished before the house was gone, and an unfinished inquiry is worse than none."

---

He stopped.

---

"Every word of that is true," he said, "and I have taken it apart every night for three weeks and it is still true, and it is still the worst thing I have ever said to anybody."

---

"You did not read it."

"I did not open it. She had it in her hands and she was holding it out and I did not put my hand on it." Mr Hwang's voice did not change. "She put it back in her bag and she said thank you, and she went downstairs and did four more months."

---

Georgij looked at the two crates and did not look at Mr Hwang at all.

---

"Do you have it?"

"No. She took it with her and it was hers. I have the note of the meeting, because I make a note of every meeting, and it says the date and the time and one line about the subject and nothing else."

---

"You keep everything."

---

Mr Hwang looked up at that.

"She told you that," he said.

"Yes. On the eighteenth of March, through the glass, at the end of a conversation I paid her for." Georgij gave it to him whole. "It was the last thing she said to me, and she said it about you, and she said you were proud of it."

---

Mr Hwang stood very still for a while.

---

"I am going to give you the address and then I want you to leave," he said.

---

He went to the second crate and did not have to look for it. It was a personnel file with four things in it. He took out the second thing and copied an address onto the back of a compliments slip, printed with the name of a house that will not exist in six weeks.

He held it out. Georgij took it.

---

"There is a bus every twenty minutes from the station and it is the fourth stop," said Mr Hwang. "I know that because she wrote it on her application when she came and nobody has ever asked her for a new one."

---

Georgij put it in his inside pocket without reading it.

---

"She asked me for a reference on the twelfth," said Mr Hwang. "I wrote her one. It is two pages and every sentence in it is true and I have never written a better one about anybody."

---

He put the file back in the crate.

---

"And there is not a firm in this country that will read past the letterhead," he said. "I knew that while I was writing it. I wrote it anyway because it was the only thing available, and I have been unable to decide since whether that was decent or whether it was the same thing I have been doing all year."

---

"It was the same thing," said Georgij.

---

Mr Hwang nodded once, slowly, as though somebody had confirmed a figure.

---

"Are you going to do something for her."

---

"No," said Georgij.

---

He could have stopped there and the room would have let him.

---

"I am going to ask her for something," he said. "I said in March, out loud, in a room with somebody in it who remembers everything I say, that I was going to be able to do something about the clerk downstairs. I have had ten weeks and I have done nothing at all, and I am here because I want something out of her."

---

Mr Hwang did not say anything for a moment.

---

"Then we are the same sort of man after all," he said.

"No. You did it out of a spreadsheet." Georgij did not raise his voice at all. "I did it because there was always something more urgent, and there always was, and every single one of them was real. That is worse and I would rather you did not take it off me."

---

The heating stayed off and the room stayed cold.

---

"There is one thing," said Mr Hwang.

"Say it."

---

"If it turns out that she was right in January," he said, "I would like that put somewhere. Not for me. I am finished and I have understood that properly now, and I am not asking you to unfinish it."

---

He straightened the box file again.

---

"She sat in this room with a school exercise book that she bought with her own money, and she held it out to a man who was proud of keeping everything." His hand stayed on the file. "I would like there to be a piece of paper somewhere in the world that says she did."

---

Georgij stood by the door with the compliments slip in his pocket.

---

He did not agree to it. He also did not refuse, and Mr Hwang had been in enough rooms by now to know that he was not being answered, and he did not ask a second time.

---

"Mr Hwang."

"Yes."

---

"You will be in work by October," said Georgij. "It will be beneath you and you will do it properly and none of that has changed. But the man who takes you on is going to get somebody who has learned the only thing you did not know in the autumn. It will have cost him nothing, and he will never find out what it cost you."

---

"Is that supposed to be a kindness."

"No," said Georgij. "It is a forecast. You may check it in October."

---

He went down three floors and past the hole in the plywood and out through a door with no book at it.

---

Mr Ku had the car by the wall.

---

Georgij read the address in the car. It is four stops out of a station he has never used, in a district that does not appear once in anything he has read this year.

On the sixteenth of March she told him to come back when he had his name. She said she would tell him the rest of what she knew, and that it was not very much and was worth having.

---

In March she named her price and it was a name.

---

He is going to arrive at her door without one.

---

Then he got the notebook out, under the date, and wrote two lines that he has not promised to anybody.

*Jeon Mi-ja. 9 Jan. Held it out. He did not take it.*

*She has a list.*
