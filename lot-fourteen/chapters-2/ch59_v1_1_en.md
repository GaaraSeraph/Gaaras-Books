# Chapter 59: Buy her
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.1 · EN

## Day Three Hundred and Nineteen · Tuesday 18 August

She said come in before he had finished knocking, which means she had heard him on the stairs and had heard which stairs.

---

The curtains were open and the room looked like a room, which it does not at night.

---

"You did not eat," said Annie.

---

"No."

---

"Then say it standing up and we will get to the eating afterward."

---

He gave her the whole of it in the order he had got it, and he did not put any of it in the order that would have landed better.

---

Nine people on a rota, counted eleven times by a man who never went through the gate. A standing order that feeds ten and went up by one in the summer four years ago and has never come down.

---

A name on a piece of paper that Sim wrote out at a quarter to eleven at night, with a pen from his own inside pocket, and which Georgij ran for three days and found nothing behind.

---

"And I decided on the fourth of August that it was a false name," he said, "and I was wrong, and I have carried it around in a coat for a fortnight while being wrong about it."

---

"Say what it is instead."

---

"It is a real name belonging to a person who has no paper. No lease, no telephone, no vehicle, no employment record, nothing in the register." He kept his hands where she could see them, which he has not needed to do in this room since November. "Nobody hides that thoroughly. She has never once been written down by anybody except the people who own the building she is in."

---

Annie did not say anything at all.

---

"Do Kyung-ae is Ahn Jung-hee," said Georgij. "She runs that household. She has run it for about three and a half years. She was in an office for nine years before this and it is what she does, and there was nobody else in that building who was going to."

---

Annie got up and went to the window and did not look out of it.

---

"She orders the oxygen," she said.

---

"Yes."

---

"For the other one."

---

"Every thirty days when she arrived. Every eleven days now." He did not soften any of that either. "She is the reason that woman is alive, and she has been kept in that house for four years for a purpose that has nothing to do with the ordering, and she has taken over the ordering anyway. Because the thing in front of her needed doing."

---

"That is the second time you have said that sentence about her."

---

"It is her brother's sentence. I have not found a better one and I am not going to try."

---

Annie turned round.

---

"Now say what you came up here for," she said, "because you have been at the top of my stairs for four minutes and you did not sit down, and the last time you did not sit down was the twenty-seventh of April."

---

Georgij stood in the middle of her room.

---

"I want to take her out of that house."

---

"You cannot."

---

"No. That is the whole of the problem and I have had it since half past nine this morning." His hands had not moved. "The moment anybody reaches into that building she stops being useful and starts being expensive. It takes one telephone call and one afternoon. There is no piece of paper anywhere that either of us could hold up afterward. She has no line. That is what she is for."

---

"And."

---

"And there is exactly one arrangement in which a woman with no paper stops being disposable." He got it out level. "Somebody has to be standing underneath her before the floor goes."

---

Annie waited.

---

"I have no money," said Georgij. "I have no name that opens a door and I have nothing in this country to telephone. I have one thing and I have had it since the fourth of October, and I am about to spend it."

---

"Say the mechanism," said Annie.

---

"Mistress."

---

"No. You have brought me a problem and a person and a quantity of oxygen, and you have not brought me the mechanism, and you have not brought it because you know exactly what it is." She had not moved from the window. "I am not going to say it for you. You have never once let me do that and I am not going to start today."

---

The house did the things a house does at eleven in the morning.

---

Georgij looked at the wall opposite rather than at her, which he has done twice in this room in eight months, and the first time was in June.

---

"There is no legal owner of a person in this country," he said. "There is a consignment, and a lot number, and a signature from a family member, and a set of arrangements that no court would look at and that everybody in that trade honours anyway. A person only leaves that arrangement in one of two ways."

---

"Say both."

---

"They stop being worth the trouble." He did not stop. "Or somebody else takes the arrangement over."

---

"Say it in four words."

---

He did not say it for about six seconds.

---

He has said a great many difficult things in this room. He said what he was in a car on the fourth of October, out loud, to a woman he had known for forty minutes. He has never once been unable to get a sentence out of his mouth for professional reasons.

---

This was not a professional reason.

---

"Buy her," said Georgij.

---

Annie did not move.

---

"Again."

---

"Buy her." It came out flatter the second time and it cost more. "I am standing in your room, wearing what I am wearing, asking you to do to a woman in Gangwon-do the thing you did to me in a cellar in Gangnam. I have had two hours to find another way of saying it and there is not one, and I am not going to stand here and dress it up for my own comfort."

---

Annie came away from the window.

---

"You have asked me for four things in eight months," she said. "A piece of paper. A room with nobody else in it. A woman in Ulsan. And a name on a bridge that you had not said out loud in six months."

---

"Yes."

---

"This is the fifth, and it is the first one that is not about you at all." She stopped about a metre from him. "I want you to notice that, because you will not, and in about a week you will have found a way to make it about you and I would like to have said this first."

---

"Yes, Mistress."

---

"Now the part you are going to hate." Annie did not soften it either. "The answer is yes. It was yes before you got to the top of the stairs. And I am going to make you stand there and hear the whole of what you have just asked me for, because you would do it to me."

---

"I will have to buy a consignment from a house that is going to be finished by Christmas," she said. "That means dealing with it before it is finished, and paying the man who runs it. It means money going from me to a building that has a woman written down in it as a lot number. It will be the only transaction in my life that I will not be able to justify to anybody."

---

"I know."

---

"You do not, and you will in about March." She held his eye. "Then I will hold a person. Not a contract, not a debt, not a note. A person, in a house, on a piece of paper with my name on it, and she will have had no say in any part of it, exactly as you had none."

---

Georgij did not look away.

---

"And on the day after that," said Annie, "she will be living in a property belonging to a woman she has never met, with nothing of her own. Everything she eats will be somebody else's. She will not be able to leave in any way that means anything until I let her. There is a word for the arrangement and we have both been using it all year."

---

"Yes."

---

"Say what you want me to do with it afterward."

---

"Give it to her," said Georgij. "Not in March. On the first day she can hold a pen. Whatever the instrument is, it ends the day she says it ends, and she is told that in the first hour by somebody who is not you and not me."

---

Annie was quiet for a moment.

---

"That is the answer I wanted and I would have done it anyway," she said. "But you have just described the thing I did not do for you, and you did it standing up, and you did not make one word of it a reproach."

---

"There is no reproach in it."

---

"I know it is not. That is what makes it one."

---

She went back to the desk and sat down and pulled the pad towards her, which is what she does instead of agreeing to things out loud.

---

"Four questions," she said. "Answer them badly and I will know."

---

"Go on."

---

"Who at that house takes the money."

---

"The one who has spent four years making it a properly run business and is proud of the ledger." Georgij did not hurry it. "He will take a consignment transfer, because a transfer is a line in a book that he is entitled to write."

---

"Second. What does he tell the man who pays for the house."

---

"Nothing, if it is done in the ordinary way and in the ordinary month." Georgij did not hesitate. "That is the only reason it works. A transfer inside the trade is not an event. It is the most boring thing that building does."

---

"Third. When."

---

"Not until Baek is finished and not until the house is. If she goes first, the man in Seoul finds out that somebody wanted her, and then he wants her, and he has more money than either of us and eleven years of reasons."

---

Annie wrote one word on the pad.

---

"Fourth," she said. "What happens to the woman on the oxygen."

---

Georgij had known that one was coming since the second question and had not got an answer ready, because getting one ready would have been the wrong kind of preparation.

---

"I do not know," he said.

---

"Say the true version."

---

"The true version is that she has eleven years of a man paying for her breathing, and on the day I finish him, that stops." He said it without any arrangement at all. "I have been telling myself for six weeks that she is the reason for the order of operations. She is not. She is the cost of them. I have not worked out what to do about that and I am not standing here telling you otherwise."

---

Annie put the pen down.

---

"Then that is the first honest thing anybody has said in this house about her," she said, "and you have been carrying it since the sixth of July and you have not once put it in front of me."

---

"No."

---

"Put it in front of me on Friday. Not today." She looked at the pad. "Today you have done enough."

---

She did not send him away, and he did not go.

---

After a while he sat down on the floor at the side of the desk, which is where he sits, and she read the eleven lines again and then read them a third time.

---

Then she put her hand down into his hair without looking away from the page, at the back, and left it there.

---

He has stopped counting them. He noticed at some point in July that he had stopped, and could not say when, and has decided not to work it out.

---

"You have not eaten since yesterday," said Annie, above him.

---

"No, Mistress."

---

"Mrs Seo has a plate for you at half past six on Thursday whether you are in it or not, and she told me that this morning before she told you." Her hand did not move. "This house has nine years of somebody counting your meals and you have not noticed one of them. Go down and eat something, and then come back up, because I have not finished and neither have you."
