# Chapter 25: What he collects
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.8 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Thirty-Six · Wednesday 27 May

The reason he did not do this in March is that doing it would have told Park Sang-hoon a great deal.

That reason went into a register office at twenty past nine on the fifteenth of May with his own name on it, and there is nothing left of it, and he has known that since the Tuesday.

---

It is a launch, which is four hours in a shed with a hundred and forty people in it and a hull that everybody has already seen photographs of.

Anybody may come. That is the whole of the point and it is why Sang-hoon said *four or five of these a year* in a car park in March and not *four or five dinners*.

---

Georgij stood in the second room, near the doors, with his hands where they could be seen, and did not speak to anybody.

It took nineteen minutes.

---

Sang-hoon came over himself, through about forty people, and two of them tried to intercept him on the way and did not manage it.

"You came and stood in a corridor," he said.

"There is not a corridor. This is the second room."

"There is never a corridor. It is a figure of speech and you knew what I meant in March and you have known it for two months." He was not smiling and he was not not smiling. "Ask."

"I want to describe a man to you," said Georgij. "I am not going to say a name, because I have not got one, and I am not going to tell you why until you have answered."

"Then describe him."

---

He gave the nine lines.

About sixty or a little less. Not tall. Pleasant and quiet, and the description has not moved since August. Sits on nothing, is in no report, gives dinners. Is introduced in a way that does not carry a name. Arranges two people into a room and goes home.

---

He did not give the ninth one yet.

---

Sang-hoon looked at him for about two seconds.

"I know who that is," he said. "Say the last one anyway, because you are holding one and I would like to hear it."

"He tells people he is glad they have finally met," said Georgij. "The first time. When they have never met."

---

Sang-hoon did not move at all.

"Yes," he said.

---

Somebody made a speech in the first room and about sixty people applauded it and neither of them turned round.

"Now the question," said Georgij. "It is one question and it is going to sound absurd."

"Ask it."

"Have you ever watched him eat?"

---

Sang-hoon stood in the second room of a shed with a hull in it and did not answer for what was probably four seconds.

"Say why you want to know," he said, "because I have got the answer and I am not going to give it to you until I know what it is worth, and I have never said that to you before."

"He does not eat in front of people he is working on," said Georgij. "It is not a discipline and it is not a superstition. He cannot do both at once and he has never in his life noticed that it shows."

"Who told you that?"

"Somebody who has known him for a very long time and who will not give me his name."

"That is the most expensive sentence anybody has said to me this year," said Sang-hoon, "and you have just put it in front of me for nothing, and I would like a moment."

---

He took it. He stood there and took about eleven seconds of it in a room with a hundred and forty people in it, and nobody came near him, because men of that sort are not approached when they are standing still.

"Eight years," he said. "Twenty-something occasions. Dinners at his own house, dinners at other people's, two lunches, a thing on a boat in 2019."

"And."

"And I have never once seen him put anything in his mouth," said Park Sang-hoon.

---

The shed was very loud and it made no difference whatever.

"Not once," he said. "Not one thing. He has a glass in his hand and he moves it about, and he is the best company in the room, and everybody goes home pleased. I have known that man for eight years and I could not tell you one thing that he likes."

---

Georgij did not say anything.

There was nothing available that was both true and survivable, and this is the fifth time in his life he has been in that position and the second one in six weeks.

"Say the rest of it," said Sang-hoon. "You are working out how to put it and there is no way of putting it. Say it flat."

"He has been working on you for eight years," said Georgij.

"Yes."

"You did not know."

"I did not know. I have been sitting at that man's table since I was fifty-one." He said it very quietly and it did not sound like anything at all. "I have made four decisions at those dinners that I have never been able to account for afterwards, and I put every one of them down to being tired."

---

Nobody said anything for a while.

"There is a second thing," said Georgij, "and it is worse, and I am going to give it to you because I am not going to be a person who tells you the first half."

"Go on."

"In November I did the same thing to you," he said. "Nineteen pages and a mistake on page nine. I told you exactly how, on the eleventh of March, in a corridor at a funeral. You took it, and you said the worst of it was that you would do the three and a half hours again. If it turns out that a man had already been doing it to you for six years before I got there, then I did not aim you. I found the handle where somebody else had left it."

---

Sang-hoon looked at him.

"That is a filthy thing to have to hear," he said, "and it is the second one you have brought me, and you have not asked me for anything yet."

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because I came here to ask you one question and you have answered it," said Georgij, "and the answer was no, and no is what I came for. Everything since then has been mine to give you and not the other way round."

---

Park Sang-hoon put his glass down on a table that was not intended for glasses.

"Choi Dae-ho," he said.

---

Georgij stood absolutely still.

"That is what you came for," said Sang-hoon. "You did not ask and you were not going to ask, and I have watched you not ask for four minutes. I would like it on the record that I gave it to you and that you did not."

"Again."

"Choi Dae-ho. Fifty-nine. Former prosecutor, out early and nobody says why. He is a director of a private equity firm in Hong Kong through a chain of lawyers in Singapore and he has never made an investment in his life." He said it flatly and completely. "He is not a financier. He does not own anything. He gives dinners."

---

The twenty-fifth of October.

A ballroom, twelve minutes in, a man of sixty-one at the same table for nineteen years asked whether he had been trained abroad, and then gave him three names to keep away from, in order.

Woo. Sunwoo. Choi Dae-ho.

Third.

---

He has had it since the seventeenth minute of the first evening.

"You have gone somewhere," said Sang-hoon.

"I was given that name on the twenty-fifth of October by a man called Hong, at a table, in a list of three." Georgij's voice did not change at all. "He put it third. He said it as a kindness and I wrote all three down and I have looked at that page perhaps twenty times since."

"And you did not know."

"I did not know. It has been in my notebook for seven months in the third position, which is where a man puts the one he actually means."

"That is not a failure," said Sang-hoon.

"It is the largest one I have."

"No," he said. "Listen to me, because I am about to be useful and I am not going to be again this year. You did not miss it. It was put in a list of three so that you would keep it and not use it. Hong is a decent man and he has never in his life understood why he gives people three names instead of one. I could not tell you either, and I have been at that table."

---

Georgij looked at him.

"You are saying Hong was arranged as well."

"I am saying that I have spent eight years having dinner with a man who has never eaten." He was not looking at Georgij. "As of about six minutes ago I do not know how far out that goes, and neither do you, and I am not going to pretend it is a comfortable position for either of us."

---

Somebody in the first room said something about the hull and about a hundred people applauded again.

"What are you going to do," said Sang-hoon.

"I do not know yet."

"That is the first time you have said that to me."

"It is the first time it has been true in front of you."

---

Park Sang-hoon picked his glass back up and did not drink out of it.

"When you do know," he said, "you are going to want something, and you are not going to come and ask me for it, and we have had that conversation and I am not having it again." He looked out at the shed. "So I am going to say one thing instead and then I am going to go and be pleasant to a hundred and forty people."

"Say it."

"I have four decisions I cannot account for," said Sang-hoon. "You will find out what all four of them were before I do, because that is what you are for and we both know it. When you find them, I want them. Not the man. The four."

"Why not the man?"

"Because the man is yours," he said, "and I have watched you for seven months and I am not going to be in the way of that. And because I have had a great deal taken off me this year by people who were entitled to, and I would like the four things back that I gave away for nothing."

---

He went.

---

Georgij stood by the doors of the second room for another twenty minutes because leaving straight away would have been the signature, and then went out to the car.

---

He wrote one line in the notebook before Mr Ku started it.

Not the name. He does not need to write down the name.

*Hong. 25 Oct. Third of three. Ask who put him at that table.*

* * *

He had four hours between the shed and the drive and he spent about three of them deciding how to say it.

Every version he built was a way of making it easier for her, and he took all of them apart on the bridge. A man who arranges the sentence has decided in advance what the other person is going to feel about it.

---

He came in at ten past eight and did not sit down and did not put it off.

"Choi Dae-ho," he said.

Two words, and nothing at either end of them, and then he stood there and let her have it in whatever shape it came.

---

Annie was at the desk with a pen in her hand and she did not put it down.

She did not move at all for about two seconds. Then she finished the word she was in the middle of. Then she put the pen down.

The order of those three things is the whole of what happened on her face.

"Who," she said.

"Park Sang-hoon. This afternoon, at a launch, in the second room. He gave it to me without being asked and he said so out loud while he was doing it."

"For nothing."

"For nothing."

"Then he is a better man than I have ever been prepared to say," said Annie, "and I am going to have to sit with that, and not tonight."

---

She got up and went to the window.

"How did he come to give it to you?"

"I asked him one question and it was the one you gave me on the twenty-seventh of April." Georgij did not move from the corner of the desk. "Whether he had ever watched him eat."

"And."

"Eight years. Twenty-something occasions. Not one thing, not once, and he had never noticed until I put it in front of him."

---

Annie stood at the window with the curtain not drawn and the drive dark behind the glass.

"He will be at his own table tonight," she said, "and he will be eating, and he will be extremely good company, and there will be four people there who think they are having dinner."

"You have said that as though you have seen it."

"I have seen it fifteen times."

---

Georgij did not say anything.

"Sit down," said Annie. "Not on the floor. In the chair, where I can see the whole of you, because I am going to give you the rest of it now and I would like to watch you take it."

---

He sat in the chair.

"I have known him since I was twenty-four," she said, and then stopped.

"That is not true and I have said it for so long that it comes out by itself." She did not apologise for it and did not make anything of it. "I was fourteen. He was at my father's table before I was allowed at it, and he was doing then what he does now, and I was the only person in that house who ever noticed that he did not eat."

"Fourteen."

"I noticed at fourteen and I did not understand it until I was about thirty. By then it was a thing I had always known and I could not remember not knowing it."

---

The clock in the hall did the thing it does.

"In December I gave you a reason for holding his name and it was a true one," said Annie. "In March I gave you a second and it was also true. On the twenty-seventh of April I gave you the best one I had, which is that the name makes you legible, and every word of that was true as well."

"And."

"And none of them is why."

---

She came away from the window.

"The real one," said Georgij.

"He collects."

---

Nobody said anything at all for a moment.

"Not that one," said Georgij.

"You know what I mean and you knew inside a second, and I would rather say it once and completely than have you sit there assembling it." She stood in the middle of the room. "He buys people. At that house and at two others. He has been doing it for a very long time and I do not know how many and I have never wanted to."

"The fourth line."

---

There are four things that have ever made Georgij put a hand on somebody, and he can name all four, and the largest of them is what grown people intend to do with children.

He did not move in that chair. His hands stayed where they were on his knees and his face did the thing it does, which is nothing at all.

Annie watched him not move and did not mistake it for one second.

"The fourth line is one of them. Four years ago, no invoice, out of the building without a bill and out of the book in the spring." She put all of it down in front of him. "And now you know why a man who could have had it for nothing paid a fee. An absence in a ledger is a question. A payment in a ledger is a Tuesday."

---

Georgij sat in the chair and did not move.

"How long have you known that," he said.

"About him, since I was thirty. About the fourth line, since the twentieth of March." She looked straight at him. "You stood in that room and named three people who were going to be hurt without having done anything. The third one was whoever is on the fourth line, who nobody has ever billed."

"I did the arithmetic while you were still talking," said Annie, "and then I asked you about the woman in Ulsan."

"Sixty-eight days."

"Sixty-eight days," said Annie.

---

He did not ask the next thing, because the next thing was in the room and asking it would have been for him and not for her.

---

She said it anyway.

"That is why I held the name," she said. "Not the legibility. That was a real reason and I used it because it was better than the one underneath."

"The one underneath."

"There is a person somewhere who was bought at that house four years ago by a man who does not eat," said Annie. "That person has no line, no owner and no date anywhere in the world, because it was taken out of the book in the spring and nobody was ever billed for it."

---

Georgij did not move at all.

"And?"

"And it is my sentence," she said. "I said it to you in this house on the first of March, and I said it again on the eleventh of March, and both times I was telling you the thing that keeps you alive."

---

The room was very quiet.

"I did not want you to find out that it is also a description of somebody who is not," said Annie. "I have known since the twentieth of March that you were going to, and I have spent sixty-eight days arranging for it to be later." Her hands stayed at her sides. "This afternoon Park Sang-hoon gave it away at a launch for nothing, and there was nothing I could do about that at all."

---

Georgij sat in a chair with both hands on his knees.

"That was not your decision to make," he said.

"No."

"You made it anyway."

"I made it anyway, for sixty-eight days, and I am not going to tell you that I am sorry about it, because I am not, and you would know inside a syllable." She stood where she was. "I am telling you what I did. You may do whatever you like with it."

---

He stood up.

"May I say two things?" he said.

"Say them."

"The first is that you were wrong and it does not matter now."

"Yes."

"The second is that I have known you for seven months and this is the first time you have ever done anything for a reason that was not exactly what you said it was." He was not accusing her of anything and it did not come out like an accusation. "And you did it about this. Out of everything in this year, you did it about this."

---

Annie stood in the middle of her own room.

"Yes," she said.

---

He did not say the rest of it. There was nothing available that was both true and small enough to survive being said, and she was standing four feet away and had just told him the only untidy thing she has ever done.

"Say what you are going to do," she said.

"Find the person."

"That is not what I asked."

"It is what I am going to do." He said it perfectly evenly. "The rest of it is going to happen to him and it is going to be complete and I am not going to enjoy telling you about it afterwards. But that is not what I am going to do first and it is not what any of this is for."

"He is four years gone," said Annie. "Out of a book, off a page, with no bill and no name, and I have spent sixty-eight days deciding that there is nothing at the end of that road."

"There is a person at the end of it," said Georgij, "and I know exactly what they are, and there is nobody else in this country who does."

---

She looked at him for a long moment.

"No," she said. "There is not."

---

He went as far as the door and she said one more thing, and she said it to the window and not to him, which is how she says the ones that cost her.

"You are going to be gone a great deal now."

"Yes."

"Then come and sit on the floor for an hour before you start."

---

He came back and sat down at the side of the desk with his back against it. She did not read anything and she did not pick the pen up again, and neither of them said one word for the whole hour.

---

Somewhere in the middle of it he got the first piece of it and did not say so.

A lot that was taken out of a book in the spring was in the book before the spring. It was entered. It sat on a page in that building for four or five months, with a reference and a season and a line, and it was ordinary while it was there.

---

Three people have looked at that page in the last four years.

Byun wrote it and took it out and has spent four years being comfortable. Hwang found the fee in December and never saw the entry, because it had gone before he was born into that building.

And a woman behind a glass panel on the first floor, at a counter with one chair on the near side, has been reading every line that house wrote for twenty-three years.

---

She went out of that building for the last time in the middle of May.

It is the twenty-seventh.

---

He does not know where she lives and he has never asked, and there is exactly one person in this country who would have written it down.

Mr Hwang keeps everything.

At some point in the middle of it she put her hand down into his hair and left it there, and he put his head back against the side of the desk.

That is the whole of what happened in that room on the twenty-seventh of May.
