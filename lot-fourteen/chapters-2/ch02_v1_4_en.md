# Chapter 2: The order of loading
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.4 · EN

## Day One Hundred and Fifty-Two · Wednesday 4 March

Mr Ku drove him out to Gimpo on the Wednesday morning and did not ask what for.

---

The two drivers used to go week and week about. That has stopped, without anybody saying so: Mr Pyo has her, and Mr Ku has him.

The yard was off a road with three yards on it and the other two were bigger. Room for eight and four trucks in it. One of the four had its bonnet up, and there was rain standing in the engine bay, so it had been up since the weekend and nobody had put anything over it.

Shin came out himself. There was nobody to send.

---

Georgij had four ways of opening with a man he needed something from. He stood in that yard for about three seconds and put all four of them down.

Three were for rooms in which the other man was still deciding what Georgij was. Shin was not deciding anything about anybody. He was standing in his own gate in March with four trucks and five weeks of nothing coming. There is no version of charm that does not read, to a man in that position, as somebody from a large house enjoying himself.

The fourth was the question about who to be careful of, and that one belongs to a newcomer.

---

You cannot take anything from a man who has nothing. You can only pay him.

So he paid first, before he had asked for anything, which is not an order he had ever used.

"You lost Daehan at the end of January," said Georgij. "I am going to tell you why. Then I am going to ask you two things, and you can decide afterwards whether you answer them."

Shin wiped his hands down the front of his coat and said nothing at all.

"It was not you. It was not your price and it was not the two loads in October." He gave it flat and in order, the way he gives everything. "In August their insurer rewrote one line about carrier concentration. Above a tonnage they may use one named haulier and only one, from the first of January. You are under the tonnage. Nobody at your size in this country kept that contract this year, and the man who took it off you is not better at this than you are."

---

Shin stood there for a while.

"I went and asked them," he said. "Twice. Second time a fellow came out to the car park to tell me there was nothing to tell me."

"There was nothing he was permitted to tell you." Georgij took a folded sheet out of his coat and put it into Shin's hand. "It is in their own filing and it has been public since August. Page twenty-six. The paragraph is four lines long and your name is not in it."

---

Shin looked at the sheet in his own hand and then at the yard.

"I put two men off in February," he said.

"I know."

"I told them both it was the two loads in October." He said it evenly enough. "That's what I had."

Georgij did not tell him that he could go and say something else to those two men now. A man who has just been handed a page works that out on his own, and saying it out loud would have been the second thing given and the first thing taken.

---

Shin put the sheet away in an inside pocket without unfolding it, which was what Georgij had expected.

"Ask your two," he said.

---

The first was what he still had. Four trucks running and a fifth that would run for the price of a pump. The yard on a lease with two years left on it. Two licences that take a year and a half to get, and he had held both for twelve years. One of the two is the whole reason anybody in Seoul would ever look at a road haulier in Gimpo.

The second was what he owed, and Shin gave him that one at the same speed, which not one man in four does.

It was worse than the four lines Annie had turned face down in January had assumed. It was also all in one place, to one lender, and secured on the trucks and not on the licences, which is the difference between a man who is finished and a man who is having a bad year.

Georgij wrote none of it down while he was standing there.

---

Georgij thanked him for them and did not ask anything else.

Shin went on anyway, walking him back over the yard, and this was the part that was not in anybody's note.

"You want to know what it's actually like now, go and stand at the Incheon gate at four in the morning." Shin had his hands in his coat pockets. "I'm there twice a week. Twelve years. It's not the same yard it was in November."

"What is different?"

"The order of loading." Shin stopped walking. "Nine months of the year that place is full. It has always been full and everybody has always waited, and waiting is the job. What's different since January is that the waiting has a shape to it. Same name goes on first. Every sailing. And there's three or four of us who've worked that out from being one of the ones that doesn't."

"There's a fellow out of Ulsan I've known eight years. Chemicals. One lane, nothing else, never had anything else." Shin was looking at his own trucks and not at Georgij. "His boxes have rolled twice since the middle of January. Two sailings. He doesn't sleep at home any more. He sleeps in the cab at the gate on a Tuesday night so he's first through in the morning."

He turned his head.

"Being first through that gate has got nothing whatever to do with what goes on the ship. He knows that. He does it anyway."

"Somebody bought something over the winter," said Shin. "Nobody knows what and nobody in Gimpo is ever going to be told. But you can read it off the quay, and it reads the same every week."

---

Georgij stood in a yard in Gimpo with the rain going into an engine bay eight metres away and read his own handwriting off a stranger's account of a Tuesday night.

He had written it out in November at a dining table with three people sitting at it. *A veto over routes decides which container goes on which sailing when a sailing is full.* He had said it out loud, in that order, and he had been pleased with how clean it was.

He had put it forward as a reason to do it.

A man sleeps in a cab now, on the strength of it.

---

At the gate Shin asked what house he was from, which is what a man asks when he does not want to ask for a name.

"Hers is not on anything you have read," said Georgij. "I advise one person, and I have done it for less than a year."

"And she wants my trucks."

"She wants to know what is in this yard. What is done about it is not decided, and I would not tell you if it were."

Shin took that the way a man takes the first straight answer he has had since January, which it may well have been.

---

Mr Ku had the engine on before Georgij reached the car, because it was cold and because that is the standard of the man.

Georgij sat in the back with nothing in his hands for most of the way and did the arithmetic on it instead.

He had given a stranger in Gimpo a piece of reasoning that perhaps four people in this country would have gone and done, and he had given it away unasked, on a Wednesday, for two facts about trucks.

It was the correct trade. He had been in that yard under a minute when he understood it was the only trade on offer there, and he had made it, and it had come back at about six times what he went for.

It also has a shape. A man who pays in one particular currency, three times, has signed something.

* * *

Annie came out of the small room at half past eight.

He gave her the two things first, because those were the things that had been asked for, and she had them in under a minute and did not look surprised by either.

"And I paid him for them," said Georgij. "Before I asked, not after."

Annie did not put anything down.

"He lost Daehan at the end of January because of one line in an insurer's requirement, and he has spent five weeks believing it was his own fault. I told him it was not. I left him the page."

"It cost you nothing." He had not sat down. "It is public, it has been public since August, and there are perhaps four people in this country who would have gone and read it in order to answer that particular question. That is the part that cost something. He will repeat it, and he will repeat where he had it."

---

Annie looked at him for a moment.

"What else."

---

So he gave her the gate at four in the morning, and the shape of the waiting, and the man out of Ulsan who sleeps in a cab on a Tuesday night.

She took that one differently. She sat down for it, in her own chair, and she asked him six questions.

Two of them he could not answer. One was how long the Ulsan one had been rolling before Shin noticed, which nobody at that gate would know and which decides whether it is eight weeks of trouble or five months of it. The other was whether anybody had gone to him yet.

"How many are there like him."

"Shin says three or four out of Gimpo alone, and Gimpo is one road with three yards on it." Georgij was still standing. "I do not know the number. I know the mechanism, because I wrote it out for you at a table in November and I was pleased with it at the time."

"You wrote it out correctly," said Annie.

"I did."

"And you are telling me that it works."

"I am telling you that it works exactly as far as we said it would." He turned his hand over. "And about four hundred metres further than anybody at that table went to look."

---

Annie did not argue with any part of that, which he had not expected either.

"Bring me the Ulsan one by name," she said. "Not from Shin. From the filings, and then from somebody who has stood at that gate."

"Yes, Mistress."

---

She was already going back into the small room when she stopped.

"You will find that they are all four of them worth having, and that two of them will be gone by June." Her hand was on the frame. "That is not a reason to hurry. It is the shape of the thing."

---

Later, on the stairs, he worked out that she had said nothing at all about the page he had left in a yard in Gimpo.

Not yes. Not no. Not that it was hers to leave.

That was the second time in two days that a question he had put down in front of her had been stepped over rather than answered, and the first time had been about a name.

---

Yesterday he had asked her for a piece of paper saying what he was permitted to do, and she had not given him one.

Today he had given something of hers to a stranger without one, and had been right, and she had let it stand.

He went up the east stairs and did not count them.
