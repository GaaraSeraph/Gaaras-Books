# Chapter 58: Buy her
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.11 · EN

## Day Three Hundred and Nineteen · Tuesday 18 August

The paper has been in the same pocket since the first of August and he has taken it out four times.

---

Sim wrote it in a small room at the back of a restaurant at about a quarter to eleven, with a pen out of his own inside pocket. He did it before he could think better of it, and he said so while he was doing it.

---

*Do Kyung-ae.*

---

Four characters and nothing else on the paper.

---

Georgij ran it the way he runs anything.

---

There is no lease anywhere in this country in that name. There is no mobile registration, no vehicle, no bank product that leaves a public trace, no employment record with any agency that supplies domestic staff to the provinces. There is no entry in the resident register that fits a woman of the right age in the right district.

---

He got all of that inside three days and he did not enjoy any of it.

---

On the fourth of August he wrote the conclusion down, and it was wrong, and it took him a fortnight to find out how wrong.

---

*A working name. He has been given one the way Sim has been given one. That is the whole of it and it is a dead end, and I am not going to spend a week proving that a name is empty.*

---

He put the paper back in the pocket and went to Ulsan and to a settlement office and to a repair shop on a corner, and it stayed there.

* * *

Mrs Seo caught him at ten past seven with the coffee already poured, which is not a courtesy and never has been.

"Eun-ju's changed to the early bus," she said. "So breakfast is out at half six from Thursday and the second sitting has gone. If you're going to be in and out at your hours you can tell me the night before and I'll leave you something covered."

"Yes."

"You've said yes to that twice." She took the cloth off the pot. "The laundry firm has put their price up eleven per cent and they've put it up because they think nobody in this house reads the second page. I'm having them in on Friday."

"Would you like anybody in the room?"

"I would not." Mrs Seo looked at him properly for about a second. "There's a plate for you at half six on Thursday whether you're in it or not. That's nine years of this house and it's not going to stop because of whatever you're doing."

* * *

Annie was at the desk in the small room and the page from the hall was in front of her, unfolded now, with two lines added underneath in the same hand.

"You read it last night."

"I read it in the hall and I read it again upstairs."

"And then you went to bed at one, which is the third time in eight days." She did not look up. "Mr Koh telephoned Mr Sohn on the eleventh and Mr Baek Hyun-woo on the thirteenth. He has not told me what he said to either of them and I am not going to ask him."

"Why not?"

"Because he did not ask my permission to do it and I would like to keep it that way." She turned the page round so that it faced him. "Mr Sohn has since cancelled a dinner he goes to every August. He has gone to it for nine years. He telephoned to cancel it himself rather than having somebody do it, which is the part I would look at if I were you."

---

Georgij looked at the eleven lines and did not pick them up.

"That is a man being careful," he said.

"That is a man being frightened, and he is frightened of a room and not of a person, and he cannot tell anybody why." Annie sat back. "You have spent eleven weeks moving one name at a time and being extremely correct about it. Somebody you spoke to once in June has just moved two of them in a fortnight without being asked. I want you to sit with that rather than be pleased about it."

"I am not pleased about it."

"No, you are calculating what it costs you, which is worse. It costs you nothing. It is the whole point. A man who has worked out that he was used does not need me and does not need you, and the second one of them telephones the next one, this stops being a campaign and starts being weather."

---

Georgij stood at the side of her desk and did not sit down on the floor.

"The part you have not said."

"Weather does not stop when you want it to." Annie looked at him then. "Every man Koh telephones is a man who might telephone the wrong person on a Thursday. I have no way of holding any of it and neither have you." She held his eye. "I have known that since the ninth of June and I have said nothing, because you were not ready to hear it."

* * *

He went down to the small room and put the four characters on the desk in front of him and looked at them for a long time.

---

Then he did the thing he has done exactly once before, at one in the morning in June, which is to open the notebook at a page that was already full.

---

The twentieth of July. Jang's rota and Jang's grocery numbers, in his own hand, four lines apart. He had heard both of them in the same afternoon and written them down inside the same hour, and said them out loud to Sang-hoon before the light went.

---

They have been sitting there for four weeks with nothing between them but a rule about margins.

---

Nine on the rota.

Food for ten.

---

He did the thing he does, which is to argue against himself first and properly.

---

A grocer's order is not a headcount. Kitchens over-order. Nine people who work an eleven-hour rota eat more than nine people who sit at a desk. A house of that size feeds a driver, and a doctor twice a week, and whoever came to look at the boiler.

---

He gave that side four minutes and it held for about three.

---

Because a standing order is not a Tuesday. A standing order is the same number, every week, for four years. It went up once and it went up by one. It has never come down for a holiday or a Sunday or a week when somebody was off sick.

---

Nine people go home. The tenth one does not.

---

He wrote that down and sat looking at it, and the house did the ordinary things a house does at nine in the morning.

---

Then he went back to the first of August, and to a small room with a folded screen and a bowl of soup that neither of them had touched.

---

*I have spoken to the same person for four years. About the heating. About whether the second bathroom was worth doing.*

---

He had heard that as staff and had written it down as staff, and for sixteen days he had gone on treating it as staff, and he could not now find the moment at which he had decided it.

---

A woman on a nine-person rota does not decide whether a second bathroom is worth doing.

---

A woman on a rota goes home on Thursday and comes back on Monday and has an opinion about the shift pattern and no opinion whatsoever about the capital works. She does not take a telephone call from Seoul about the heating in November. In November she is in her own flat with her own heating, forty minutes down the valley. The house is a place she is paid to be in.

---

*The person who decides about a bathroom is the person who is in the building on the day the men come, and on the day after that, and on every day of the winter it makes a difference to.*

---

The name is not a working name.

---

That was the sentence, and it arrived without any warning at all, and he found that he had put one hand flat on the desk.

---

He had spent three days proving that Do Kyung-ae has no lease, no telephone, no vehicle, no bank product, no employment record and no line in the resident register. He had read all of it as evidence that the name was false.

---

It is not evidence that the name is false.

---

It is evidence that the person has no paper.

---

Georgij sat at a desk in a house on the river and looked at four characters on a piece of paper.

---

There is a category of person in this country, and in every country, who has a name that people use to their face and nothing at all behind it. No lease, because somebody else holds the lease. No telephone in their own name, because the telephone belongs to the house. No employment record, because they are not employed. No entry anywhere, because an entry would have to be made by somebody, and the somebody who would have to make it is the reason there is nothing to make it about.

---

He knows exactly what that is.

---

He has been it for seventeen years.

---

*In seventeen years across eleven houses the entire administrative record of my existence was other people's paperwork about me.*

---

He wrote that in January, in a different room, about himself.

---

She is the tenth plate.

She is the person Sim telephones about the heating.

She is the person who has been ordering the oxygen for a woman she has never been introduced to, every eleven days now and every thirty days when she arrived.

---

She is the one who goes out and looks at the tank. A rota does not look at a tank. A person who lives there does.

---

Ahn Jung-hee runs that household.

---

She was in an office four years ago and she solved the thing in front of her in four days without asking anybody. They put her in a house in Gangwon-do, and she has done there what she would have done anywhere.

---

She has taken over the ordering.

---

He did not write any of it down for about ten minutes. When he did, he wrote it in the flattest sentences he could find, because the alternative was to write it in the ones that were actually in his head.

---

Then he stopped, because the second half of it had arrived.

---

He has a line into that house. It is the only one anybody has had in eleven years, and it goes directly to her, and it has been in his coat pocket since the first of August.

---

And he cannot pull it.

---

*If I reach into that house and touch the person who orders the oxygen, and I am seen, she is not moved to another house. She is the reason the house exists. She is moved out of the arithmetic altogether, and it takes one afternoon and one telephone call and there is no paper anywhere that either of us could ever hold up.*

---

*She has no line. That is the whole of why she is useful and the whole of why she can be got rid of.*

*I have spent seventeen years being the same thing. I am the only person in this business who was ever going to see it.*

---

He sat with it until the light on the desk changed.

---

Every road he has taken this year has ended at the same door and he has walked away from it four times and been right every time.

---

Baek pays and cannot be touched until she is safe. The house can be finished and finishing it puts the trust into a room with lawyers in it. Sim has turned and is holding and will go on holding. Sim is not the one who reaches into that building, because Sim reaching into that building would be noticed inside a day.

---

There is exactly one arrangement in the world in which a woman with no paper stops being disposable.

---

Somebody with a great deal of money has to be standing underneath her before anybody takes the floor away.

---

*I do not have money. I do not have a name that opens a door and I do not have a bank product and I have not had one since I was nine years old.*

*I have one thing, and I have had it since October, and I have asked her for four things in eight months.*

---

He put the paper back in the pocket.

---

Then he stood in the small room for a while longer. A man who goes up those stairs at half past ten to say what he is about to say ought to have spent four minutes on them first.

---

He had done the arithmetic before he had finished thinking about doing it. He noticed that on the second step and did not stop.

---

There was no light under her door, because it was daylight, and he stood outside it for the length of time it takes to put a cup down.

---

Then he knocked.

* * *

She said come in before he had finished knocking, which means she had heard him on the stairs and had heard which stairs.

---

The curtains were open and the room looked like a room, which it does not at night.

"You did not eat," said Annie.

"No."

"Then say it standing up and we will get to the eating afterward."

---

He gave her the whole of it in the order he had got it, and he did not put any of it in the order that would have landed better.

---

Nine people on a rota, counted eleven times by a man who never went through the gate. A standing order that feeds ten and went up by one in the summer four years ago and has never come down.

---

A name on a piece of paper that Sim wrote out at a quarter to eleven at night, with a pen from his own inside pocket, and which Georgij ran for three days and found nothing behind.

"And I decided on the fourth of August that it was a false name," he said, "and I was wrong, and I have carried it around in a coat for a fortnight while being wrong about it."

"Say what it is instead."

"It is a real name belonging to a person who has no paper. No lease, no telephone, no vehicle, no employment record, nothing in the register." He kept his hands where she could see them, which he has not needed to do in this room since November. "Nobody hides that thoroughly. She has never once been written down by anybody except the people who own the building she is in."

---

Annie did not say anything at all.

"Do Kyung-ae is Ahn Jung-hee," said Georgij. "She runs that household. She has run it for about three and a half years. She was in an office for nine years before this and it is what she does, and there was nobody else in that building who was going to."

---

Annie got up and went to the window and did not look out of it.

"She orders the oxygen," she said.

"Yes."

"For the other one."

"Every thirty days when she arrived. Every eleven days now. She is the reason that woman is alive, and she has been kept in that house for four years for a purpose that has nothing to do with the ordering, and she has taken over the ordering anyway. Because the thing in front of her needed doing."

"That is the second time you have said that sentence about her."

"It is her brother's sentence. I have not found a better one and I am not going to try."

---

Annie turned round.

"Now say what you came up here for," she said, "because you have been at the top of my stairs for four minutes and you did not sit down, and the last time you did not sit down was the twenty-seventh of April."

---

Georgij stood in the middle of her room.

"I want to take her out of that house."

"You cannot."

"No. That is the whole of the problem and I have had it since half past nine this morning." His hands had not moved. "The moment anybody reaches into that building she stops being useful and starts being expensive. It takes one telephone call and one afternoon. There is no piece of paper anywhere that either of us could hold up afterward. She has no line. That is what she is for."

"And."

"And there is exactly one arrangement in which a woman with no paper stops being disposable." He got it out level. "Somebody has to be standing underneath her before the floor goes."

---

He was describing what had been done to him in October, and he had not got to the word yet.

---

Annie waited.

"I have no money," said Georgij. "I have no name that opens a door and I have nothing in this country to telephone. I have one thing and I have had it since the fourth of October, and I am about to spend it."

"Say the mechanism," said Annie.

"Mistress."

"No. You have brought me a problem and a person and a quantity of oxygen, and you have not brought me the mechanism, and you have not brought it because you know exactly what it is." She had not moved from the window. "I am not going to say it for you. You have never once let me do that and I am not going to start today."

---

The house did the things a house does at eleven in the morning.

---

Georgij looked at the wall opposite rather than at her, which he has done twice in this room in eight months, and the first time was in June.

"There is no legal owner of a person in this country," he said. "There is a consignment, and a lot number, and a signature from a family member, and a set of arrangements that no court would look at and that everybody in that trade honours anyway. A person only leaves that arrangement in one of two ways."

"Say both."

"They stop being worth the trouble." He did not stop. "Or somebody else takes the arrangement over."

"Say it in four words."

---

He did not say it for about six seconds.

---

He has said a great many difficult things in this room. He said what he was in a car on the fourth of October, out loud, to a woman he had known for forty minutes. He has never once been unable to get a sentence out of his mouth for professional reasons.

---

This was not a professional reason.

"Buy her," said Georgij.

---

Annie did not move.

"Again."

"Buy her." It came out flatter the second time and it cost more. "I am standing in your room, wearing what I am wearing, asking you to do to a woman in Gangwon-do the thing you did to me in a cellar in Gangnam. I have had two hours to find another way of saying it and there is not one, and I am not going to stand here and dress it up for my own comfort."

---

Annie came away from the window.

"You have asked me for four things in eight months," she said. "A piece of paper. A room with nobody else in it. A woman in Ulsan. And a name on a bridge that you had not said out loud in six months."

"Yes."

"This is the fifth, and it is the first one that is not about you at all." She stopped about a metre from him. "I want you to notice that, because you will not, and in about a week you will have found a way to make it about you and I would like to have said this first."

"Yes, Mistress."

"Now the part you are going to hate. The answer is yes. It was yes before you got to the top of the stairs. And I am going to make you stand there and hear the whole of what you have just asked me for, because you would do it to me."

"I will have to buy a consignment from a house that is going to be finished by Christmas," she said. "That means dealing with it before it is finished, and paying the man who runs it. It means money going from me to a building that has a woman written down in it as a lot number. It will be the only transaction in my life that I will not be able to justify to anybody."

"I know."

"You do not, and you will in about March." She held his eye. "Then I will hold a person. Not a contract, not a debt, not a note. A person, in a house, on a piece of paper with my name on it, and she will have had no say in any part of it, exactly as you had none."

---

---

"And on the day after that," said Annie, "she will be living in a property belonging to a woman she has never met, with nothing of her own. Everything she eats will be somebody else's. She will not be able to leave in any way that means anything until I let her. There is a word for the arrangement and we have both been using it all year."

"Yes."

"Say what you want me to do with it afterward."

"Give it to her," said Georgij. "Not in March. On the first day she can hold a pen. Whatever the instrument is, it ends the day she says it ends, and she is told that in the first hour by somebody who is not you and not me."

---

Annie was quiet for a moment.

"That is the answer I wanted and I would have done it anyway," she said. "But you have just described the thing I did not do for you, and you did it standing up, and you did not make one word of it a reproach."

"There is no reproach in it."

"I know it is not. That is what makes it one."

---

He had asked her to do properly a thing that had been done to him badly, and he had not noticed that was what he was doing.

---

She went back to the desk and sat down and pulled the pad towards her, and did not write on it.

"Four questions," she said. "Answer them badly and I will know."

Georgij did not sit down.

"Who at that house takes the money?"

"The one who has spent four years making it a properly run business and is proud of the ledger." Georgij did not hurry it. "He will take a consignment transfer, because a transfer is a line in a book that he is entitled to write."

"Second. What does he tell the man who pays for the house."

"Nothing, if it is done in the ordinary way and in the ordinary month." Georgij did not hesitate. "That is the only reason it works. A transfer inside the trade is not an event. It is the most boring thing that building does."

"Third. When."

"Not until Baek is finished and not until the house is. If she goes first, the man in Seoul finds out that somebody wanted her, and then he wants her, and he has more money than either of us and eleven years of reasons."

---

Annie wrote one word on the pad.

"Fourth," she said. "What happens to the woman on the oxygen."

---

Georgij had known that one was coming since the second question and had not got an answer ready, because getting one ready would have been the wrong kind of preparation.

"I do not know," he said.

"Say the true version."

"The true version is that she has eleven years of a man paying for her breathing, and on the day I finish him, that stops. I have been telling myself for six weeks that she is the reason for the order of operations. She is not. She is the cost of them. I have not worked out what to do about that and I am not standing here telling you otherwise."

---

Annie put the pen down.

"Then that is the first honest thing anybody has said in this house about her," she said, "and you have been carrying it since the sixth of July and you have not once put it in front of me."

"No."

"Put it in front of me on Friday. Not today." She looked at the pad. "Today you have done enough."

---

She did not send him away, and he did not go.

---

After a while he sat down on the floor at the side of the desk, which is where he sits, and she read the eleven lines again and then read them a third time.

---

Then she put her hand down into his hair without looking away from the page, at the back, and left it there.

---

He has stopped counting them. He noticed at some point in July that he had stopped, and could not say when, and has decided not to work it out.

"You have not eaten since yesterday," said Annie, above him.

"No, Mistress."

"Mrs Seo has a plate for you at half past six on Thursday whether you are in it or not, and she told me that this morning before she told you." Her hand did not move. "This house has nine years of somebody counting your meals and you have not noticed one of them. Go down and eat something, and then come back up, because I have not finished and neither have you."
