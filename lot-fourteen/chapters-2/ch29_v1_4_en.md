# Chapter 29: Nobody hides flowers
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.4 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Thirty-Nine · Saturday 30 May

He woke on the floor of the small room at about one o'clock with the lamp still on and a coat over him that is not his.

---

He copied the exercise book by hand between then and four.

---

There is a machine on the ground floor that would have done all forty lines in about a minute and put the time and the count in a log.

He has spent five months following one of those logs backwards through a building. He sat at the desk in the small room with a pen instead.

---

Forty lines over twenty-three years. A date, a time, and about six words.

He wrote them out in the order she keeps them and did not tidy any of it, and at about two in the morning he came to the line under lot six and stopped.

---

What she said out loud on Friday was four things. Flowers, twice. A driver. And a doctor.

What is written there is longer than that, because she is a settlement clerk and a settlement clerk writes down what she would need in order to find it again.

---

*6 Apr. 27 Apr. Hyoja-dong Flowers, house acct, both.*

*26 Apr, car, no letterhead kept.*

*3 Jul, practice, no letterhead kept.*

*All four cleared.*

---

She gave him more than she said out loud, and she said nothing about doing it, and she let him find it at two in the morning on his own.

---

Annie was at the desk at ten to seven with her coat back over the arm of the chair.

She is not up at ten to seven on a Saturday. Neither of them made anything of it.

"I would like the day," said Georgij.

---

Annie put the pen down, which she does not do for a question.

"For what."

"The flowers."

---

He put the copied page on the desk in front of her, the right way round, and stood back from it.

"There are three letterheads on that line and she wrote out one," he said. "Hyoja-dong Flowers, twice, on the house account."

"Why that one and not the other two?"

"Because that house bought flowers every week for thirty years and she could have written the name in her sleep," said Georgij. "The car was one invoice and the practice was one invoice. She saw each of them once, four years ago, and she wrote what she had."

---

Annie read the line twice.

"You said last night that the invoice from the practice was the first piece of paper in this made by somebody who is not in the trade," he said. "So is a delivery slip. A van had to go somewhere, and a man had to be told where, and somebody wrote the address down in the ordinary way."

"And nobody has thought about it since."

"Nobody has thought about it once. Nobody hides flowers."

---

Annie sat with the page in front of her for a while.

"Then here is what you are about to do, and I want you to hear it before you enjoy the rest of it," she said. "Every door you have gone through this year belonged to somebody who already knew what he was standing in. Byun knew. Hwang knew. Yeom knew before you sat down."

"Yes."

"That shop does not." She put one finger on the second date. "There has been a piece of paper in a cupboard in Hyoja-dong for four years and it has been worth nothing to anybody. At about half past ten this morning it is going to start being worth something, and you will be the reason, and it is the only copy."

---

Georgij stood on the other side of the desk.

"Then I will take the original in my hand and give it back to her," he said, "and pay for the copy, and say nothing at all about what is on it."

"The last part. Again."

"She does not get told what she has. The day she knows, she is a person who can be visited."

---

Annie opened the drawer and counted out notes and put them on top of the copied page.

"Take Ku and do not park in front of it," she said.

* * *

The shop is in a street of low buildings behind a hill in Hyoja-dong, third one along, with a green awning that has been mended.

Mr Ku put him down two streets away without being asked to, which is Mr Ku having listened to somebody in a hall at some point this week.

---

There are buckets in a rank on the pavement and a cold room at the back with a glass door, and the whole shop smells of cut stems and wet paper.

The woman behind the counter is about seventy and was doing the till roll.

"We open properly at ten," she said, and then looked up. "Come in anyway."

"Mrs Gwak?"

"That is on the awning. It has been on three awnings."

---

Georgij stood on the customer's side of the counter with his hands where she could see them.

"Four years ago this shop sent flowers twice on an account that has since been closed," he said. "The auction house on the river. I would like a copy of the two slips and I will pay for it."

---

Mrs Gwak put the till roll down.

"Is it true that they have shut?"

"Yes."

"Nobody rang," she said. "Thirty years of standing orders and one lot every spring and nobody rang to say. My son found out from a man who does the windows."

---

She stood there for a moment with her hand flat on the counter.

"What is your interest in it?"

"There is a person the flowers went to," said Georgij, "and I am trying to find out what became of them, and that is the whole of the truth about why I am standing here. I am not going to tell you the rest of it, because the rest of it is not mine to hand round a shop."

---

Mrs Gwak looked at him, and then at the cupboard behind him.

"You are the first person in a year to walk in here and not tell me a story," she said. "Min-a. The blue books, from the cupboard."

---

The blue books are duplicate order books, one to a year, with carbons, kept because an accountant said so a very long time ago and nobody has said otherwise since.

The girl went up a ladder and came down with four of them and put them on the counter in a stack that smelled of the cupboard.

---

Mrs Gwak found the year without looking at the spines.

"April," she said. "You said April."

---

She turned the carbons over from the back with her thumb, four or five at a time, and stopped.

"There."

---

The slip is a third of a page. There is a box for the order, a box for the delivery, and a box in the corner for who rang.

---

*6 Apr. Hyoja-dong Flowers. Twelve stems, white, no wrap.*

The delivery box has an address in Pyeongchang-dong, a flat number, and a floor.

Under it, in pencil, in a hand that is not hers: *Card: From an old friend.*

"That is my husband's writing," said Mrs Gwak. "He copied the cards into the book. He said a card is the only part of it anybody keeps."

---

Georgij read the corner box.

---

The name in it is Sim.

There is a telephone number beside it with the old city code in front, and the word *tel* underneath, because he had not come in.

"Turn to the twenty-seventh," said Georgij.

---

She turned four more carbons.

---

*27 Apr. Twelve stems, white, no wrap. Same as 6th.*

The corner box says Sim.

The delivery box has an address that is not in this city.

---

There is no card line on the second slip at all.

---

Georgij stood in a flower shop and read a box that was empty.

"He did not send a card the second time," he said.

"Then he did not send one." Mrs Gwak looked at where his finger was. "We ask. We always ask. If it says nothing there, he was asked and he said no."

---

Under the delivery box on the second slip there is a line in pencil in a third hand, small, the way a man writes when he is standing up.

*Left at the desk. They would not give a room.*

---

The shop was quiet and the cold room hummed.

"That will be Mr Han," said Mrs Gwak. "He drove for us for eighteen years and he wrote on everything. If a delivery did not go into a hand he put down why, because he was not going to be the man who lost a bouquet."

---

Georgij looked at the two slips side by side on the counter.

---

Three weeks apart. Two addresses. And between them, on her page, a car with no letterhead on the twenty-sixth.

Somebody was moved out of the city on the twenty-sixth, and the flowers went to the new address on the twenty-seventh.

The man who sent them did not have to be told where they had gone.

"Mrs Gwak."

"Yes."

"The man who rang. Sim." He had not touched either slip. "Do you remember anything about him at all?"

"I remember all of it," she said, "and I have never once been asked, and I have thought about it more than a florist ought to think about a telephone call."

---

She put her finger on the corner box.

"He was pleasant. He knew what he wanted and he did not want it explained to him." She said it the way people say things they have gone over. "And when he rang off the first time he said that it was good to see us again."

---

Georgij put one hand on the edge of the counter.

"We had never had an order from him," said Mrs Gwak. "Not once, and I looked, because I do not like being wrong about a customer. I sat here and went back three years."

"The sentence exactly."

"*It has been good to see you again.*" She got it out flat. "On the telephone, to a woman he had never spoken to in his life. And then twelve white stems and no wrap, twice, for somebody who was not allowed to have a room number."

---

The girl at the back put a bucket down and the water went over the tiles and neither of them turned round.

"Will you copy those two for me?" said Georgij.

---

Mrs Gwak took the book to the machine at the end of the counter and did it herself, and squared them and folded them once, and put the blue book back on the stack.

---

He paid for the copies and she gave him the change and he took it.

---

At the door he stopped, because there was one more thing and it was hers.

"You said your son found out from a man who does the windows," he said.

"Three weeks after."

"There is one man left in that building and he is doing the whole of it out of a spreadsheet," said Georgij. "Every supplier that house had found out the way you did. That is not a thing anybody decided about you."

---

Mrs Gwak stood behind her counter with the till roll in her hand.

"That is a kinder answer than the true one," she said.

"It is the true one. The kinder one would have been that somebody meant to ring."

---

He walked the two streets back to the car with the folded copies in his inside pocket and did not get in.

---

He has two addresses in his hand and he is not going to either of them today.

Pyeongchang-dong is forty minutes away and the second one is an hour and a half. He could be standing outside both of them before lunch, and he has been able to see the whole of that plan since the counter.

It is the plan he would have run in April.

---

He wrote in the notebook standing up, against the wall.

---

*Sim. Ordered by telephone, 6 Apr and 27 Apr. Says it is good to see people he has never met.*

*Yeom, four years ago, on the telephone: glad we had met. Two people who have never spoken to each other, one sentence.*

*6 Apr, Pyeongchang-dong, flat and floor. 27 Apr, out of the city. No card. No room number.*

*Car on the 26th. Doctor on 3 Jul.*

---

Then he stood with the pen still in his hand and wrote one more line, which is not evidence and is not for anybody.

---

*Somebody sent twelve white stems to a person nobody had ever billed, and then sent them again the day after that person was moved out of the city.*

---

Everything else that anybody wrote down in this was written by somebody who knew it might one day be read.

The only page in it with a person on it was written by a shop that had no idea it was writing anything at all.

---

Mr Ku had the car at the end of the street with the engine off and the window down.

"Home," said Georgij.

"You were fifty minutes."

"I was in a flower shop."

---

Mr Ku looked at his empty hands in the mirror and pulled out.
