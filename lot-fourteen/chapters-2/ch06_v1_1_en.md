# Chapter 6: I have to ask
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.1 · EN

## Day One Hundred and Sixty-Four · Monday 16 March

He did not ask one person about the new man.

A man who is brought into a house to clean it out has cleaned out other houses, and those houses leave paper. Three of them, in six years. Two are still trading and one is not, and the one that is not filed everything it was ever going to file.

From that he got the order it comes in.

---

It is always the same order and it is not the order anybody expects.

The new man does not start with the desk that handles the money. He starts with the desk that handles the paper about the money, because that is where a house keeps the things it has decided not to write down properly.

And the tell, four to six weeks ahead of it, is a request in writing: would you set out, in your own words, how your filing works.

---

Nobody at a desk has ever read that sentence and understood it.

They sit down flattered and write four pages and hand over the whole architecture of their own protection, and six weeks later a man they have never met knows which drawer to open first.

---

Georgij knew the shape of it because he had built one in December.

He wrote that down on the Friday, in one line, and did not soften it and did not look at it again.

---

He went over the whole thing on the Sunday with the three filings side by side and the dates in a column, in that order, before he decided he was going.

* * *

The house is a building near the river with a public stair on the west side.

He had been in it once, in October. He had come in at the back and gone down. He had been in the cellar of it for four hours with a number on him, and had not seen the front of the building until it was over.

He came in through the front door on the Monday at eleven and nobody stopped him.

---

He had settled that on the Sunday and did not open it again on the day.

In that building a clerk with a terminal can look up what is behind the thing round his neck. It takes under a minute, and what such a clerk finds is nothing. He went in anyway. There was no version of the month in which he did not.

---

Settlement is on the first floor at the end, past two rooms with nothing in them.

There is a counter with a glass panel above it and a gap at the bottom for envelopes. There is one chair on the near side, which tells you how long the average conversation is meant to be.

Mrs Jeon was behind the glass. She is somewhere near fifty. She has been at that desk since the house moved into this building, and her whole value for all of those years has been that nobody looked at her.

---

"I am not here to ask you for anything yet," said Georgij. "I want to tell you something first, and it is yours whether the rest of this goes anywhere or not. It will take four minutes."

Mrs Jeon had a receipt book open in front of her and did not close it.

"Four minutes."

---

He gave it to her in order and he did not hurry any of it.

The new man. What he did at the three previous houses and in what sequence. That he does not start with the money and everybody thinks he does. That the letter comes first, and what the letter asks for, and what happens to the people who answer it well.

Then the dates. Six weeks from the letter to the first desk, and about twelve days from the first desk to the second.

---

"And the second desk is this one," he said.

---

Mrs Jeon looked at him through the glass for a while.

"Why are you telling me this."

"Because I am going to ask you for something in a minute, and I did not want to be holding it while I asked."

"That is the same thing."

"It is not, and you know it is not, or you would not have said it in that voice." He kept his hands where she could see them. "You have it now. You can send me down the stairs and you will still have it. That was the whole reason for the order I said it in."

---

She did not thank him.

That is not ingratitude. She had come in that morning with twenty-three years behind the glass and a job. She was going home that evening with twenty-three years and about two months. A man in a good coat had done that to her before eleven fifteen.

---

"Ask, then," she said.

---

"On the back page of the tidied catalogue there are four entries marked retained and not disclosed." He said it as though it were a shipping schedule. "I want to know who settled the oldest of them."

"You have seen the back page."

"I have a copy of the back page. It came up a drive in a flat parcel with a printed label on it, and so did one to every buyer on their list."

---

Mrs Jeon put one hand flat on the counter and left it there.

"You know what you are standing in," she said. "You know what this desk is."

"I know exactly what this desk is."

"Then you know what it costs a woman behind this glass to answer that question."

"I do not." He did not dress it up. "That is why I am asking you and not telling you. Say the price."

---

She took her time about it, which was correct.

"I want a name," she said.

"Whose."

"Anybody's, so long as it is not out of this trade." She said it quite steadily. "In eight weeks I go out of that door with twenty-three years that I cannot put on a single piece of paper. There is no reference from this house that is worth anything anywhere. What I need is one person who will take a telephone call and say one sentence about me, and be somebody whose sentence carries."

---

Georgij did not answer.

---

He knew who he could ask before she had finished the second sentence. He knew what it was worth to her. And he knew that saying yes in that room would have closed the whole thing in under a minute.

He also knew what Annie had said in the small room on the Wednesday. *The price, before it is paid.*

---

"I have to ask," he said.

---

There is a particular silence that follows that sentence at a counter, and he had heard it before from the other side of one.

"You have to ask."

"I am not going to stand here and promise you a thing I do not own." He did not look away and he did not soften his voice. "The name that would do this belongs to somebody else. I can go and ask for it today and I think I will get it. I am not going to say yes to you on the strength of what I think."

---

Mrs Jeon looked at him for what was probably four seconds.

"Sit down," she said.

He had been standing the whole time. He sat down on the one chair.

---

"Do you know how many men have stood on that side of this glass and promised me something." She had not moved her hand. "I do not mean this month. In twenty-three years."

"No."

"Neither do I, and I stopped counting when I was thirty." She let that stand. "Nobody has ever said the other thing. Not once, at this desk, in twenty-three years."

---

"I am going to give you one thing now," she said, "and it is not the answer to your question, and it is not a favour. It is so that you do not come back on Wednesday and waste both of our afternoons."

"Go on."

"The oldest of those four never came through this desk."

---

Georgij sat very still.

"Nothing at all."

"No invoice, no receipt, no clearing, no line. I have been through it twice, because it bothered me at the time and it has bothered me since." Her voice did not change. "Everything in this house comes through this desk. That is the entire point of this desk. Somebody stood in that room and took a person home and this house did not send anybody a bill."

---

"And that is not a thing that happens by accident."

"That is not a thing that happens at all." Mrs Jeon took her hand off the counter at last. "Come back when you have your name, and I will tell you the rest of what I know, which is not very much and is worth having."

* * *

Mr Ku had the car on the far side of the street and did not ask.

---

He was home before three and did not start anything, which is not like him.

When the car came up the drive at twenty past five he went and stood in the small room, because sitting down in it would have been a way of putting it off.

---

"The price first," said Georgij.

"Go on."

"There is a woman at that desk who goes out of the building in eight weeks with twenty-three years she cannot write down anywhere. She wants one name. Somebody outside the trade who will take a call and say one sentence about her and be worth listening to."

---

Annie put the coat on the arm of the chair by the door.

"And you did not give her one."

"I have nothing to give her. I said I had to ask, and I came home."

---

"Say the obvious one first, so that we are not walking round it."

"Yours." He did not hesitate over it. "It would work in an afternoon. It would also put your name in that woman's mouth, in that building, eight weeks before she is turned out of it and has to explain herself to somebody. If anybody ever goes looking for a reason why this house is interested in that house, that is the reason, standing at a bus stop in Incheon with her handbag."

"Yes."

"So no."

"So no," said Annie.

---

She went to the window and did not draw the curtain.

"Park Sang-hoon."

---

Georgij had got there in the car and had spent the rest of the journey wishing he had not.

"He is on your list," he said.

"He is on my list because I put him there on Wednesday, and I put him there for this sort of thing and not for company." She turned round. "And he is the only name in this that explains itself. A man who buys and sells for a living has forty reasons to know a woman who does settlements. Nobody has to be told why he would speak for her. If it were my name, the only question anybody could ask is why."

---

"And what it costs," said Georgij, because she was not going to say it and it had to be in the room.

"Say it, then."

"It is the third thing he learns. He has the fact that this house cares about that book. He gets the fact that it cares enough to go to the counter. And he gets to be the man who fixed it, which with him is the expensive one, because he does not forget and he does not put things down."

---

"And it uses the corridor," said Annie.

---

He had given her that on the Wednesday in the middle of twelve minutes of other things, and she had kept the word.

"It uses the corridor," he said. "He offered it once and said he would not go on about it. This is not what a man means when he says that."

"It is exactly what a man means when he says that. That is why it costs."

---

"Do it," she said. "Today, not tomorrow, and not by telephone if there is any way of not using a telephone. And Georgij."

"Mistress."

"You will tell him what she is going to be able to say about him afterwards, before you ask him. He will say it does not matter. Tell him anyway."

---

He did it that evening. It took seven minutes. It cost precisely what he had said it would cost and produced precisely what Annie had said it would produce.

* * *

What he did afterwards, on the Monday night, was the other half.

A settlement that never happened is still a fact about somebody. A house that does not send a bill has been told not to, and an instruction of that size comes from an owner.

The house has an owner. The owner is a fund, and the fund is four companies deep, and the fourth one is registered at an address in Jung-gu that also holds nine others.

---

He knew the shape of it before he was through the second layer.

He had seen it twice in the autumn, both times from underneath, both times without anybody's name at the end of it. It is not the ordinary kind of nesting. Somebody had built it to be dull, and had gone to a great deal of trouble to make each individual step look like the sort of thing a lazy accountant does.

---

So he stopped.

He did not order the fourth company's filings. He did not send for the register extract on the Jung-gu address. He did not put a single request into a system with a date on it, and he closed the whole thing at twenty past one in the morning and left it exactly as he had found it.

That was the correct thing to do and he did it quickly, out of the same instinct that had made him say *put a line through it* at a desk on the eleventh.

He had no way of knowing that it was the second time.
