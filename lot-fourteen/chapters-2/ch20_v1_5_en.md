# Chapter 20: Cheaper than deciding
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.5 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Nine · Thursday 30 April

He got in at twenty past eleven and the light in the small room was on.

That is not usual. She works late perhaps twice a week and she does not wait up for anybody. She has never once in seven months been in that room at that hour on a night when there was nothing on the desk.

There was nothing on the desk.

---

"Come in and shut it," said Annie.

---

He shut it, which is the second time that door has been shut with him inside the room.

---

"It is the thirtieth of April," she said.

"Yes."

"You told me on the twentieth of March that it would be finished by the end of April. I made you say it again on the first, because I wanted it to be a date and not a thing a man says at midnight." She had her hands flat on the desk. "It is the thirtieth of April at twenty past eleven. Report."

---

He stood at the corner of the desk and did it in order and it took four minutes.

---

The house on the river closed to the public yesterday. Mrs Sunwoo has three more lunches in the diary and does not need any of them.

Nam Byung-hee has two trucks, no chair, no work and no association standing. Mr Kwon signed on the twenty-seventh. The other four sign Chairman Woo's paper in the first week of May, seven years, the same document as the anchor cargo.

The five halves come onto the market between the middle of May and the middle of June, five separate afternoons, and there is one buyer on that coast with money in her hand and it is not a metaphor.

Mr Ok is a hand and was frightened on Monday and nothing else has happened to him or will. The second copy went into his own filing on the twelfth of March and he can put his hand on it in twenty minutes, which is the only thing this month that has turned out smaller than it looked.

The man who chose her has nine lines of description and a sentence and no name.

---

"That is the month," said Georgij.

"That is the month."

---

Annie did not say anything about any of it, which he had expected, because she does not congratulate people and it has never once occurred to her that anybody might want her to.

---

"Now the other thing," she said.

---

"Yes."

"I am not going to ask it gently and I am not going to give you a way of answering it that lets you past. I have thought about how to put it since about six o'clock." She looked at him. "Did you enjoy it."

---

He did not answer straight away, and she let him not answer.

---

"Yes," he said. "Once. For about a second and a half, at a gate, at four o'clock this afternoon."

---

"Say what it was."

---

"She asked what would happen if she talked anyway. And I told her I would come back a fourth time and there would be nothing in my hands then either." He said it perfectly evenly. "It is true, and it was the correct thing to say, and I would say it again in that position tomorrow, and I enjoyed saying it."

---

Annie sat back.

---

"And the rest of the month."

"No. Not one minute of it. Kwon's yard, no. The telephone call on the twenty-third, no, and I said I was sorry to a man who did not want it. Mr Ok at his own wall, no. This afternoon, none of it, and I have never wanted less to be anywhere in my life than I did in that office."

"And the one second and a half."

"The one second and a half, yes."

---

"How do you know."

---

"Because she told me." He looked at the edge of the desk. "She said it was the only thing I had said all day that I enjoyed, and she said it in the voice she reads things in." He did not look up. "Then I got on a train and had four hours and could not find an answer I was prepared to write down."

---

"Then she is better at this than I am."

"She is not. She had the advantage of not caring what the answer was."

---

Annie thought about that for a moment and let it go, and it was not agreement.

---

"Say the rest of what you came in here to say," she said. "You have had four hours and you have been standing there working out how to get to it since I said report."

---

"I would like the ceiling back."

---

The clock in the hall did the thing it does at the quarter.

---

"Say it properly," said Annie.

---

"On the third of April I asked you to take it off me and you did, and it was the right thing to ask for and I would ask again." He had his hands behind his back. "I am asking you to put it back on. The price before it is paid, in this room, every time, and if there is no time to ask then I tell you the same night. I would like it to start tomorrow morning."

---

"No," said Annie.

---

He did not move.

---

"Say why," he said, "because you have got a reason and I would rather have it than the word."

---

"Because you are not asking me for a ceiling." She said it without any heat at all. "You are asking me to be the thing that stops you, and a man who has somebody to stop him has to decide once, on a Thursday in April, and never again."

---

"That is not what I am doing."

"It is exactly what you are doing and you know the shape of it better than anybody alive, because it is what you have done to eleven people since October." She did not raise her voice. "You put a rule in front of a man so that he does not have to make the decision again in June. It works every single time, and it works because a rule is cheaper than deciding."

---

Georgij stood there and did not have anything at all.

---

"I am not going to be your conscience," said Annie. "Not because I do not want the job. Because I would be extremely good at it and you would never have to do it again. In four years you would be somebody who does whatever is in front of him and comes home and has it audited."

---

"And if I get it wrong."

"Then you will get it wrong, and you will find out, and it will cost somebody something." She held his eye. "That is what it costs to be the person deciding. You have had two months of it now and I am not going to take it off you because the second month was unpleasant."

---

He did not say anything for a while.

---

"You are going to have to do it every time," she said. "Every single time, on the afternoon, in the room, with nobody in it but you. And there will be no version where you get to have decided already."

---

"That is worse than the ceiling."

"Yes. It is also the only one of the two that is worth anything, and you would have told anybody else exactly that."

---

He looked at the desk for a moment.

"May I say one thing that is not an argument?"

"Say it."

---

"I have not been afraid of very much since October," said Georgij. "I have been afraid of four things and I could name all four. As of this afternoon there are five, and the fifth one is that I am going to be all right about it."

---

Annie did not answer that for a moment.

---

"Yes," she said. "That is the correct one to be afraid of, and I would have been disappointed in you if it had taken you until June."

---

She got up and came round the desk.

"Sit down on the floor."

---

He sat down at the side of the desk with his back against it, which is where he sits. She sat on the arm of the reading chair, which is not sitting. Neither of them said anything for a while.

---

"What happens to her now," said Annie.

---

She has not asked one question about that woman since the third of April, and he had stopped expecting her to.

---

"Nothing happens to her. That is the whole of it. Two trucks, no work, and a name that is finished on that coast. Nobody on it will employ her, because employing her is a statement, and nobody makes a statement about a woman who has just been put out by her own six."

"And she is fifty-eight."

"She is fifty-eight, and she is the most competent person I have sat in a room with since December, and I am saying that as a piece of information and not as a sentiment."

---

Annie looked at the window.

"Then somebody should employ her."

---

"Yes," said Georgij. "In about four months. Not by me, and she is never going to know where it came from."

---

"Say the reasons and put them in the order they actually go in."

---

"Three of them." He set them out. "She is good and she is going to be wasted, and that is the one I would put last if I am being honest about the order. She is honest, and there is going to be very little of that on that coast by June."

"And the first."

"Somebody who has been armed once can be armed again. A woman with nothing is a woman who says yes to the next man who arrives with a page, and I have spent six weeks finding out how cheap that is to arrange."

---

"That is the one that matters," said Annie.

"That is the one that matters. It closes the last hole in the whole of it."

---

"And there is a fourth and you have not said it."

---

"There is a fourth and I would sooner it were not there."

"Say it anyway."

"The man who chose her is going to hear about it." Georgij said it flatly. "He will hear that his instrument was taken apart in six weeks and then picked up off the floor by the same hand, and there is nothing at all he can do with that except sit with it."

---

"And why four months," said Annie.

"Because in May it is a payment. She would take it, and it would be the last thing that ever happened to her with my hand on it, and I would have bought my way out of this afternoon for the price of a job." He did not move. "She said something to me at a gate today that I have not got an answer to. I am not going to answer it with a favour."

---

"There is one more thing," she said eventually, "and it is not about any of that."

"Mistress."

"You have not slept properly since the eighteenth of March, and neither has anybody in this house who has been paying attention. That is Mrs Seo and Jang and me, and Mr Ku, who is the only one of the four to have said anything about it."

---

"He has not said anything about it."

"He said something to you on an expressway on the fourteenth of April about a yard in Gimhae." She was looking at the window and not at him. "He came and told Mrs Seo that he had said it and that he was not sure whether he should have. He has been worrying about it for a fortnight."

---

Georgij put his head back against the side of the desk.

---

"Tell him it was the most useful thing anybody said to me all month."

"Tell him yourself. He is on the early rota."

---

"Yes, Mistress."

---

She put her hand down without looking away from the window and left it in his hair, and after a while his shoulders came down about an inch, and he had known this time that they were up.
