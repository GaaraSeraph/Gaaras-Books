# Chapter 21: Tidily
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.6 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Thirteen · Monday 4 May

The front door of the house on the river is not locked, because a building that is winding up has men in it moving things and a locked door is a nuisance.

There is nobody at the desk where the visitors' book was. The book is on the counter with a pen beside it and nothing written in it since the twenty-ninth of April.

Georgij wrote his name in it anyway, and the time, and left the third column empty.

That is the third time he has written his name in a book at a door in eight weeks, and the first time nobody has been standing there to watch him do it.

---

The lights are on in one corridor on the first floor and off everywhere else, which is a thing a building does when there is one person left in it who is not paid by the hour.

---

Mr Hwang was in the room at the end with three crates open and a list.

He is about fifty. There is nothing in the room but the crates, a table and a chair, and he was standing.

---

"We are closed," he said, without looking up. "If you are from the fund you are two days early and I have not finished."

"I am not from the fund."

---

He looked up then.

"Then you have walked into a building that is shut and I have about six hours of work in front of me. Say what you want in one sentence and I will tell you whether I have got time for it."

---

"You have four entries on the back page of a catalogue you tidied," said Georgij. "All four were sold as deletions and all four were paid for. You found that out and you left them where they were."

---

Mr Hwang put the list down on the table.

---

"Sit down," he said. "There is one chair and you may have it, and I am going to stand, because I have been standing since seven and if I sit down now I will not get up again."

---

Georgij did not sit down either.

---

"You are correct," said Mr Hwang. "I want to say that first so that we are not doing the part where you prove it to me. I found it in the second week of December, in the settlement ledgers, and it took me about an hour."

---

"Four fees over four years," he said. "Three of them were in that hour. The fourth is dated the nineteenth of February, ten weeks after I stopped the practice, and I did not find it until the catalogue was already at the printer. Two of them are the same buyer. One is a woman who has bought here since I was a boy. One is a man."

---

"And there is a thing about the man that does not fit, and I am going to give it to you now, because you will get there eventually and I would sooner you got there from me."

"Say it."

"He paid the fee. It is in the ledger with the other three and it is the same money in the same hand." Mr Hwang did not move. "But the lot itself never went through settlement at all. No invoice, no clearing, no line at that desk, nothing. This house let a lot walk out of the building and did not send anybody a bill for it."

---

"So he was not billed for the thing," said Georgij, "and he paid to have the record of it taken out."

"Yes."

"Those two do not go together."

"They do not go together at all, and I have had five months on it." Mr Hwang picked the list up and put it down again. "A man who is not billed is a man this house owes something to, or is frightened of, or belongs to. And a man in any of those three positions does not have to pay anybody a fee. He can simply say take it out."

"And he paid anyway."

"He paid anyway, on the same terms as everybody else, in cash, through the same man." He looked up. "I have never been able to make that mean anything except one thing, and I do not like it."

"Say it."

"That he did not want anybody in this building to know that he could have had it for nothing."

"And you did nothing."

"I did not delete them. That is not the same as nothing and I am not going to let you have it as the same." He said it quite steadily. "I stopped the practice on the day I found it, and it was sold once more after that, and I have put that in front of you before you asked me for it. It has not been sold since the man who sold it walked out of this building. That is the whole of what I was brought here to do. I did it in nine weeks and it took ten more before it was true."

---

"Say what you did about the four."

---

"Nothing," said Mr Hwang.

---

The building did what an empty building does.

---

"Then that is the thing I have come about," said Georgij. "Not the practice. The four."

---

"They paid for a service," said Mr Hwang. "The service was not performed. I have been aware of the shape of it since December and of the last of them since the end of February, and I made a decision and I have not changed it. If I delete an entry because somebody paid for it to be deleted, then I am a man who deletes entries when there is a reason, and there is always a reason."

---

"That is a very good sentence," said Georgij. "I would like to take it apart, and it is going to take about a minute, and you are going to be able to follow all of it."

"Then take it apart."

---

"The rule you were brought here to hold is that this house does not sell deletions." He kept his hands at his sides. "It is a rule about the future. It is a rule about what you accept money for from tomorrow onwards. It has nothing whatever to say about four people who paid for something last year and did not get it."

---

Mr Hwang did not answer.

---

"You had three ways out of December and every one of them was clean," said Georgij. "You could have performed the four. You could have refunded them, in full, with a letter saying why. Or you could have told each of them exactly what had been done to them and let them decide."

"Yes."

"You took none of them. You kept the money and you kept the record. That is the position the men before you were in. The only difference between you and them on the thirty-first of December is that your filing is better."

---

Mr Hwang picked the list up again and then put it down without looking at it.

---

"That is not fair," he said.

"No," said Georgij. "It is accurate. Those are different words and I chose the one I meant."

---

"I could not perform them without becoming the thing I was sent to stop."

"You could not perform them. You could have refunded them in an afternoon, and it would have cost this house about a tenth of what it made from them. There is not one sentence in your rule that says a house may keep money for work it did not do." Georgij did not raise his voice at any point. "You did not decline to delete four entries. You declined to give four people back a thing they had already bought, and you called that a principle."

---

The room was very quiet.

---

"You have not been running a clean house for five months," he said. "You have been running the same house tidily."

---

Nobody in that building had ever said it to him, because everybody in that building had thought he was the improvement.

---

Mr Hwang stood with one hand on the edge of the table for what was probably eight seconds.

---

"Say the rest of it," he said. "You did not come here to give me a lecture on obligations. There is a fifth thing and you have been holding it since you came through the door."

---

"On the fourteenth of March one of those four entries was photocopied and sent to a woman in Ulsan who had nothing," said Georgij. "By the twenty-third of April she had lost the chair of her association. By the middle of this month she will have no firm. She is fifty-eight and she has never told an untruth in her life and she has two trucks left."

---

"And the page that did that," said Mr Hwang.

"Is on the back of the catalogue you built. You put the four retained entries on one page because a thing that cannot be found goes wrong later, and you were right about that too." He looked at him. "It has been posted to every buyer this house has. It went to six hundred and forty addresses in the first week of March. It cannot be collected."

---

Mr Hwang sat down on one of the crates, which is not a chair, and put his forearms on his knees.

---

"Who are you."

---

Georgij told him, and gave him the whole of it, which is a thing he has done four times since October.

---

He watched the name go in and he watched what it did, and it took about two seconds, because the man in front of him has read those four entries more carefully than anybody alive.

---

"Lot fourteen," said Mr Hwang.

"Yes."

"The fourth of October. Struck on the nineteenth of February." He said it the way a man reads out a shipping mark. "And retained. And I retained it."

---

"You retained it."

---

Mr Hwang looked at the floor of a room with three crates in it.

---

"Is the house shut because of you," he said.

---

There it was, and it was a direct question from a man who does not ask any other kind.

---

"Yes," said Georgij. "Not by me. I told one customer the truth about what had been done to her, in one sentence, in her own house, in March. She checked it in a catalogue she already had. Everything after that was hers and I have not spoken to her since."

"And you knew what she would do."

"I knew exactly what she would do. That is why I said the sentence."

---

Mr Hwang nodded slowly, once, and did not say anything about it at all.

---

"There is one more thing and it is not an argument," said Georgij. "It is a fact and you have not got to it yet, and I would rather you had it from me standing in this room than worked it out in June."

"Say it."

---

"You are finished."

---

"Not the house. You." He did not decorate any of it. "You are the most honest man in this trade and you have five months on your record and a building that died under you. Nobody in this country is going to read that charitably and nobody is going to ask. In eleven years, when somebody says your name, the sentence after it will be the name of this house."

---

Mr Hwang sat on the crate with his forearms on his knees and did not move.

---

"I did the job," he said.

"You did the job. That is the part I cannot get past, and I have been trying since the twenty-third of April." Georgij waited for him to look up. "Nobody in the whole of this took a single won. Not you. Not me. Not her. Everybody involved did what they thought was correct and a woman of fifty-eight is going to be selling two trucks on Thursday."

---

That is the arithmetic he has been carrying since April, and there is nobody in it to be angry with.

---

Nobody said anything for a while.

---

"You want something," said Mr Hwang. "You have not asked for it yet and you have been in this room for twenty minutes."

"Yes."

"Then ask, because I have got about four hours left in me and I would like to spend some of them on the crates."

---

"The four fees," said Georgij. "Somebody in this house took them. Four times over four years. That man had a name and a position and he signed something each time."

---

"Yes."

"I would like it."

---

Mr Hwang did not answer for a moment, and it was not reluctance and it was not calculation.

---

"You understand that I have spent five months not being the sort of man who does that."

"I understand that you have spent five months not doing a number of things," said Georgij, "and that we have just been over what the last one cost."

---

That landed, and it landed properly, and Georgij watched it land.

---

Mr Hwang got up off the crate and went to the table and wrote on the back of the list. He wrote for about fifteen seconds, which is longer than a name.

Then he tore the strip off and held it out. He did not put it down on the table to be picked up, which is a thing a man does when he wants it clear that he handed it over.

---

"A name, a position, and the dates of the four," he said. "He left at the end of February, a week after the last of them went through, and I have never been able to decide whether those are two facts or one. He told the payroll office he did not want to work for a fund."

"Where is he now."

"On the second line."

---

Georgij looked at it.

---

"Thank you."

"Do not." Mr Hwang went back to the crates. "I have just done in twenty seconds the thing I refused to do for four people in five months. I did it because a man came in here and made me feel it." He did not turn round. "I would like to be quite clear with myself about what that says."

---

Georgij stood in a room with three crates in it and did not have an answer for that either.

---

At the door he stopped.

"Mr Hwang."

"Yes."

---

"You will not be given another house," said Georgij. "You are going to be given work by somebody eventually and it is going to be beneath you, and you are going to take it, and you will do it properly because you cannot do anything the other way."

---

"Is that supposed to be a kindness."

"No. It is the last true thing I have got and I have run out of the other kind."

---

He wrote the time in the second column of the visitors' book on the way out, because there was nobody there to do it. The book will be in a crate by Friday and nobody is ever going to read either line.
