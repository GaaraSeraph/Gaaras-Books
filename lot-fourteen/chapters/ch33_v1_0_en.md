# Chapter 33: That is five
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.0 · EN

## Day Eighty-Seven · Monday 29 December

He came up the drive.

That was the first thing about it, and Georgij thought it was the whole of it until he was proved wrong about an hour later. In November this man had gone over the east wall, at a corner where a camera turns away for eleven seconds. He had walked into a lit room without being announced, and he had been correct about every part of it.

On the twenty-ninth of December he telephoned at half past ten, was given two o'clock, and arrived at four minutes to.

The gate opened for him. Jang was in the hall and did not need to be.

---

Annie had Georgij in the room before Sang-hoon was out of the car.

She did not say why and there was no need. In November the man had asked his question in front of him on purpose, and had said so, and had been right that asking it any other way would have been worse.

Whatever was coming was going to be said in front of the same three people as last time.

---

They gave him twenty minutes of nothing at all first, because that is how it is done.

The weather. A ferry company in Mokpo that had gone under on Christmas Eve and had been going under since June. A man they both disliked, who had become chairman of something.

Park Sang-hoon was entirely pleasant and his coat was over the back of a chair he had not been invited to use, and Georgij watched him not look at the collar even once, which took work.

---

Then he put his cup down.

"I am going to make you an offer," he said to Annie, "and it is a serious one, and I have been three weeks deciding whether to come and say it out loud."

"Then say it out loud."

"Two billion two hundred million won." He did not put anything around it. "For his contract, his release, his papers, whatever the correct word is in whatever arrangement you have. Today, in one payment, and I do not require to know what you paid."

---

The room was very quiet.

"I have made a guess about what you paid," said Sang-hoon, "and I am not going to say it, because it would be discourteous and because I might be wrong by a factor. What I will say is that in thirty-one years I have never watched anything amortise the way that has, and I am offering you the whole of it in an afternoon."

He was not looking at Georgij at all, which was also correct.

"I want to be clear that this is not a compliment to him. It is an accounting judgement. I have run the number twice and I would run it a third time and get the same answer."

---

Annie did not move.

Her hands were on the arms of the chair where they had been for twenty minutes, and they stayed there, and they went nowhere near the pocket of anything.

Georgij was aware of that the way you are aware of a change in air pressure.

In November her hand had been in her jacket before the question finished. Today the jacket was on the back of a chair in another room. She had put it there herself at half past one, and he had watched her do it and had thought nothing about it.

---

Nobody said anything.

Sang-hoon waited, because he had asked a woman a question and she had not answered, and that is her right.

Annie let it go on long enough that it stopped being a pause and became something else, and then she turned her head, about fifteen degrees, and looked at Georgij.

That was all she did.

---

He stood up.

"May I say something first, Mistress?"

"Say it."

---

"On the eighteenth of November, in the car coming back from Yeouido, you told me I had been enjoying myself." He had his hands behind his back. "You said to be pleased with it in that car and nowhere else, because I had got what I got out of a man who was showing off to somebody he had decided was nobody. And you said that if I look pleased in a room, he stops showing off."

"I remember what I said."

"It was correct then and it has been correct every time since." He did not hurry any of it. "I am going to break it in about a minute, in front of you, on purpose, and I would like you to have heard me say that before I do it rather than after."

---

Annie's face did nothing at all.

"Why."

"Because there is nothing left in that man for me to get, and there has not been since the fourth of December. Everything he had, I have. He is not showing off any more and he is not going to." He kept his eyes where they were. "And because there is a thing I am going to do once, and I will not get to do it a second time, and I have wanted to do it since the twenty-second of November."

"That is thirty-seven days."

"Yes."

---

Annie looked at Sang-hoon and then back.

"You have never once asked me for anything for yourself."

"No, Mistress."

"You are asking now."

"I am asking whether I may be pleased about something in a room," said Georgij, "which is the smallest thing I have ever wanted, and I have found out this month that I do not know how to want anything larger."

---

She was quiet for a moment.

"Go on, then."

---

Georgij turned round.

Park Sang-hoon had not moved at all through any of it, and had listened to every word with the flat attention of a man who is being given something for nothing and knows better than to interrupt.

"Chairman Park."

"Mr Georgij."

"I would like to ask you for guidance."

---

Sang-hoon did not smile.

He sat back in the chair he had not been offered, and put one hand on each knee, and something went across his face and did not stay.

"That is five."

"It is five."

"Two of them are mine. Three, now." He said it the way a man reads out a delivery note. "In November I told you that you could have stopped using it."

"You did."

"And you told me that you had not stopped because you were actually asking, and that this was the whole of it, and that it was why the thing worked."

"I did."

---

"Are you actually asking?"

"No," said Georgij.

---

The house made no noise whatever for about two seconds.

"That is the first untrue thing you have ever built out of me," said Sang-hoon, "and you have just told me it is untrue before you use it, which means it is not untrue, which means you have thought about this for some time."

"Thirty-seven days."

"Then it will not work again."

"It will not work again on anybody, and it will not work on you ever." Georgij said it plainly. "It was the only thing I own that costs nothing, and after this afternoon nobody will take it from me, because you will tell four people and every one of them will tell four more. I am spending the whole of it here, once, and I am doing it knowingly."

---

"Then spend it," said Park Sang-hoon.

---

"Please guide me," said Georgij.

"How does a man let go of a sweet, sweet, beautiful, poisoned apple that he has already bitten into? Chewed, and found absolutely tasty?"

He did not raise his voice anywhere in it.

"Please, Chairman, tell me. I prepared this apple to look and to taste very delicious."

---

Nobody in that room said anything at all.

Jang, in the hall, would say afterwards that he heard nothing and had not been listening, and both halves of that were untrue and he was never asked again.

Park Sang-hoon sat entirely still with one hand on each knee for what Georgij counted at eleven seconds.

---

Then he laughed.

It was one syllable and it was not a pleasant sound and it was completely genuine, and it was the first thing that man had done in three months that had not been decided in advance.

"Hanseong."

"Hanseong."

"Every line of it was true."

"Every line of it was true," said Georgij, "and you checked most of it yourself, and you will not find a bad page in there in March, because there is not one."

---

Sang-hoon nodded slowly, twice, at nothing in the middle of the room.

"The routes are good. The berths are good. The fight is winnable and I am going to win it." He was working out loud and not defending anything. "It takes a quarter and it takes all of it, and there is not one thing in this world I can do about the calendar."

"No."

"December to March."

"December to March."

---

"And in that quarter," said Sang-hoon.

He stopped.

Georgij watched a man with thirty-one years in a trade walk the length of it in about four seconds without moving. He arrived where he had already arrived at some point in the last eleven days, and found it confirmed, and took it without one word of complaint.

"The Kims."

---

"There is nothing to announce," said Georgij.

---

"That is not an answer."

"It is the whole answer and it is true in every part," said Georgij. "There is no filing. There is no announcement. There is no purchase of a company and no change of a name, and there will not be one in January or in March or at any time. If you send four people to look for it they will find nothing, because there is nothing, and they will come back and tell you they found nothing."

Sang-hoon looked at him for some time.

"And that is how I will know."

"That is how you will know."

---

He got up and went to the window and stood with his back to both of them, which is a thing this man had not done in any room Georgij had ever been in with him.

"Ye-rin."

Georgij did not answer that, and it was not a refusal, and Sang-hoon did not turn round and did not ask again.

---

"Your offer," said Annie.

It was the first time she had spoken in eleven minutes.

"My offer stands until I am in the car," said Sang-hoon, still at the window. "It will not stand after that, and I am not saying so to be difficult. I have just watched him do something I would not have believed if it had been reported to me, and I am now certain that two billion two hundred million is too little. I am not able to go higher today, and I do not like paying twice."

---

Annie's answer took four words.

"He was never for sale."

---

Park Sang-hoon turned round then.

"I have been in a great many rooms," he said, "and I have heard people say that about a horse, a building, a football club and a daughter. Every one of them was for sale about eighteen months later, at a price they were embarrassed by."

"I know," said Annie.

"Then you know why I asked."

"I know why you asked, and I know why you came up the drive instead of over the wall, and I am going to think about the second one for longer than the first."

---

He collected his coat from the back of the chair.

At the door he stopped, the way he had stopped in November, and he did not offer his hand then either.

"Mr Georgij."

"Chairman."

---

"Two things, and you will get no more from me after today."

"I am listening."

"The first is that the apple was very good, and I would eat it again on the information I had. No man in this country would have done otherwise, and I want you to know that I know it." He put his coat on. "It was not a trick and I will not have it called one. Everything in it was true, and it was put in front of me at the one hour it could cost me most, by somebody who understood the hour better than I did. There are perhaps nine people alive who could have built it."

---

Georgij inclined his head and said nothing.

"The second is for you and not for her." Sang-hoon's voice did not change at all. "You spent your only free thing this afternoon on eleven seconds of my face. I would have paid you for it. I offered, in fact, about forty minutes ago, and you were in the room."

"I was."

"You gave it away instead, to be pleased for eleven seconds, in front of the person who told you not to be." He opened the door himself. "I have done that twice in thirty-one years, and both times it was the most expensive thing I did that decade, and both times I would do it again. That is not advice. There is no advice in it. It is the only guidance I have ever had that was worth anything and it is yours."

Then he went out to his car, and the car went down the drive, and the gate did what the gate does.

---

The room was very quiet again and the light had gone from the window.

Georgij stood where he was and found that his hands were not entirely steady and that this had been true for some minutes.

"Sit down," said Annie.

He sat down.

---

"Was it worth it."

"Yes."

"Say why, and do not be clever about it."

"Because in November he asked what I would cost in front of me and I did not get to answer him." He looked at the carpet and then up. "Today he asked again and I answered, and the answer was not a number, and he heard it, and he understood every part of it before I finished the sentence. That is all it was. That is the whole of what I wanted for thirty-seven days."

---

Annie was quiet.

"You understand what he did to you at the door."

"He put a number in the room," said Georgij. "Then he told me it was too low, and then he told me that he would not pay it twice, and then he walked out with all three of those facts standing up."

"And in March I am going to ask you what you want."

"Yes, Mistress."

"And now there is a figure in your head that was not there this morning, and you did not want it there, and it was put there by a man you had just finished taking apart." She was not making a point. She was reading it out. "He has never once done a stupid thing in front of me. That was not one either."

---

Georgij looked at the window for a while.

"No," he said. "It was not."

---

Mrs Seo came in at six and made up the fire, which she does not do on a Monday, and neither of them said anything about it.
