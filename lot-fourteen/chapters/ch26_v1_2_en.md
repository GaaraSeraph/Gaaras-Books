# Chapter 26: The name and the control
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.2 · EN

## Day Sixty-Three · Friday 5 December

She sent for him at nine in the morning and had the filing on the desk in front of her, printed, which she almost never did.

"Say it," said Annie.

"Say which part?"

"The part you have been carrying since September and did not say yesterday."

---

Georgij stood at the corner of the desk.

"He has eaten it," he said.

"That is not the sentence."

"No, Mistress."

She waited.

---

"He has eaten the apple," said Georgij. "All of it, in twelve days, from seven people at a price that was neither clever nor stupid, and every single thing in those nineteen pages was true, and he checked most of it himself. He is not going to find a bad line in there in March, because there is not one." He kept his hands where they were. "He has bought four shipping routes with real berths and a fight he can win. He will win it, and it will take him a quarter." A short pause. "There is nothing in the world he can do about the calendar."

Annie turned the page over so that it lay face down.

"And how does it feel," she said.

"It is the best work I have ever done."

"That is not what I asked."

"No," said Georgij. "It is the answer I have."

---

She let that stand for a moment.

"The Kims," she said.

"Yes."

"How long have you had it ready?"

"Since the sixth of November." He said it flatly. "It has been waiting for two things that did not exist yet. Chairman Woo's signature was one, and the other one was in this filing."

"Then take it to them."

"Yes, Mistress."

---

"Not a restaurant."

"No. Their own building." He had already been through it. "They have been sitting in there for six weeks watching customs go through their own paperwork, and every person who has come to see them since October has wanted something and has arranged to meet them somewhere quiet. I am going to walk in the front door at an appointed hour with a contract in my hand."

Annie looked at him for a second longer than the sentence needed.

"You have got better at this," she said.

"I have had a good autumn."

---

## Day Sixty-Six · Monday 8 December

The Kim building is eleven storeys in a part of the city that was cheap in 1994 and is not cheap now, and it has a lobby with too much stone in it for the size of the company.

There were four cars in the visitor bays and two of them had been there long enough to have leaves on the windscreens.

Georgij came in at ten to eleven and gave his name at the desk.

He sat in the lobby for the eight minutes with his coat on his knees, which is what a man does when he does not expect to be there long enough to hang it up.

He had one folder.

---

They took him up to the fourth floor, to a room with a long table and a picture window that looked directly at the roof of the building next door.

There were three of them.

Kim Sung-ho had the title and was the older brother, sixty-one, thick through the shoulders in the way of a man who had done something physical for a living before the family had this. He shook hands and did not smile and did not pretend to.

The second was a cousin, somewhere in his fifties, who had brought a legal pad and would not write anything on it.

The third was Kim Do-yun.

---

Georgij had been ready for that since Friday and had built the entire hour around it.

He had put this man at the top of a page in November on the basis of two trips to a bar, and had been wrong. He had written the name on a clean sheet and put nothing under it. Now the name was in a chair by the window with its jacket buttoned, thirty-four years old, entirely at ease, and Georgij looked at him for exactly as long as he looked at the cousin.

"Mr Kim," he said, to all three of them at once, which is a thing you can do in this language and cannot do in most.

---

"You work for the woman," said Sung-ho.

"I do."

"Then say what she wants and I will decide whether to be angry after you have said it."

---

Georgij put the folder on the table and opened it and took out one page.

"Before any of that. This is yours whether the rest of the morning goes well or badly, and it is not conditional on anything, and there is nothing on the back of it."

He turned it round and pushed it across.

"Chairman Woo Jae-sung has signed seven years of anchor cargo across the Yeongjong terminal. Minimum volumes in tonnes. The rate is his own and it is inside the market. It is executed and dated the twentieth of November, and the counterparty on it is your logistics company, and it holds today."

---

Nobody said anything for a moment.

The cousin picked the page up before Sung-ho did, which told Georgij something he filed and did not use.

"Woo does not sign these," said the cousin.

"He has not for six years."

"And he signed one for a family that is being turned over by customs."

"He signed it on the twentieth of November, which is fourteen days after customs went into your building." Georgij did not move. "He knew. I told him, in his own office, before I asked him for anything."

---

Kim Do-yun spoke for the first time.

"What did you tell him about the container?"

That was a good question and it had been asked forty seconds too early, and both of them knew it about half a beat after it was out.

"I told him it was not yours," said Georgij.

"And he believed that."

"He believed it because he has been in that trade fifty-one years and because he has had things put into his own sheds twice." Georgij turned his head and looked at him properly for the first time. "He also asked me who put it there, and I told him that I could tell him and could not prove it, and then I did not tell him."

Do-yun's face did nothing at all.

"That was well done," he said.

"Thank you."

---

Sung-ho put his hand flat on the page.

"Now the rest of it."

---

"The cancellations are what is killing you," he said. "Not the investigation. Nobody in this room needs me to explain that, so I am going to say the shape and then the price and then I am going to stop talking."

He did not sit down and had not been asked to.

"You need money to get to March and you need somebody standing next to you in public before then. My employer has both. What she is offering is capital, security over the routes, and her name in the same photograph as yours, in December, at an address that is not hers and not yours."

He waited until somebody in the room moved.

"What she wants is shares, security, and a veto over routes."

---

The cousin put the legal pad down.

"That is the company," he said.

"No," said Georgij. "That is control of the company. The company stays yours."

"Say the difference, then, and say it in a way I can repeat to my wife."

---

"You keep the name," said Georgij. "The building says Kim on it in March the way it says Kim on it today. Your brother is chairman, your family is on the register, and every customer you have ever had deals with the same three people they dealt with in September. Nobody outside this room is told anything, and there is no announcement, because there is nothing to announce."

He was still standing.

"And she gets the control."

He said it without a single softening word in it anywhere.

"When there is a decision about which box goes on which sailing, she makes it. Not a committee, not a vote, not a consultation with you. Her. That is the veto and it is the whole of what she is buying. If I dressed it up for you this morning you would find it in the document by Thursday, and you would be right to throw me out then instead of now."

---

The room was very quiet.

"You are unusually direct," said Do-yun.

"I am selling you the worst part first," said Georgij, "because the worst part is the part you would find anyway, and I have twenty minutes and no relationship with any of you."

---

Sung-ho had not moved during any of it.

"You said December," he said. "At an address that is not hers and not mine."

"The sixteenth. A house north of the river that belongs to a woman your sister has known for twenty years."

Georgij took the second page out of the folder and did not push it across.

"Forty people. Chairman Woo will be there from seven. There is a photographer until nine and he goes home at nine. Between eight and half past there will be one picture of him standing with your family, and it will be in two papers on the Thursday."

He put one finger on the page and left it there.

"That picture is worth more to you than the money, and I want you to hear me say that out loud."

---

"Why?"

"Because a man who has not been photographed with anybody since 1998 does not stand next to a family that is going to be indicted." He was looking at Sung-ho and not at the others. "Nobody will write that down. Everybody in your trade will do the arithmetic inside a day, and the four customers who have not yet cancelled will decide to wait until March, and waiting until March is all you need."

---

Sung-ho was quiet for a while.

Then he said, "I have to make a telephone call."

"Yes," said Georgij.

"You know who to."

"I do."

"And you are going to sit there and not say anything clever about it."

"I am going to sit here," said Georgij, "and I am not going to say anything at all."

---

Sung-ho went out through the door at the end and did not close it properly, and they heard nothing, because the room next door was two doors away and the man was not a fool.

The cousin looked at the window. Do-yun looked at Georgij.

Nine minutes.

---

Georgij sat with his coat on his knees and did the only thing there was to do.

The entire morning was now happening in a room he was not in, between two people, and he had never met one of them.

He had built the hour for the three men at this table.

The decision was being made by a woman of fifty-four who was not in the building. She holds one point four per cent. Nobody has made her sign anything in twenty years, and nobody at this table would admit they had telephoned her.

*Not quickly and not twice. Once.*

He had not spent it yet. He had not even been in the room where it would be spent. And it occurred to him, sitting there with his coat on his knees, that he had built the whole of December around a conversation with a woman who might simply say no on a Tuesday.

Hana had told him so in plain words at a dinner table in November. He had heard it as a difficulty rather than as a fact.

---

Kim Do-yun got up and went to the window.

"You have not asked me anything," he said.

"No."

"Everybody asks me something. I am the one in this family who knows what a berth costs."

"I know what you are," said Georgij.

Do-yun turned round.

Neither of them said anything for about four seconds, which in that room was a considerable length of time.

"Say it, then."

"No," said Georgij.

---

Do-yun looked at him a moment longer, and then did something with his mouth that was almost a smile and was not one.

"You are going to be a problem," he said.

"I am going to be at that house on the sixteenth from six o'clock," said Georgij, "and so, I hope, are you."

---

Sung-ho came back in at nineteen past and sat down and put both hands on the table.

"Two things," he said. "The first is that we will come on the sixteenth. All of us. My brother-in-law will not, and you will not ask why."

"I will not ask why."

"The second is that nothing about shares or security or routes is decided in this room today. Nobody here has the authority to decide it." He did not look away. "I am telling you that plainly instead of letting you think you nearly had it."

"I know who has the authority," said Georgij.

Sung-ho looked at him.

"Yes," he said. "I thought you might."

---

He put his hand back on Woo's contract.

"This is real."

"It is real and it is unconditional and it is yours whatever happens in March."

"Then I will say the thing I did not expect to say this morning." Sung-ho squared the page against the edge of the table, which is a thing men of that generation do. "Thank you."

---

Georgij inclined his head and did not smile.

He got his coat and got out of the building and was on the street by twelve, and did not allow himself anything at all until the car was moving.

Then he took the notebook out and wrote three words on a clean page, under a line about a stone table in a hall.

*She said yes.*

He looked at it for a moment and then crossed it out.

He did not know that. He would not know it until the sixteenth. He had just written down a thing he wanted instead of a thing that was true.
