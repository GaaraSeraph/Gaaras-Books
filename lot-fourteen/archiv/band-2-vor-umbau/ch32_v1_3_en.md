# Chapter 32: Where the name goes
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.3 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Forty-Two · Tuesday 2 June

He came out on the bus again, which took fifty minutes, with the book in his inside pocket.

---

The tape is still over the name on the bell.

---

She opened the door the whole way this time and went back into the room without waiting for him, which is a different thing from Friday.

---

He put the exercise book on the table before he sat down.

"It is all copied and it is all there," said Georgij. "Nothing has been taken out of it and nothing has been added to it, and there is one copy and it is in a locked drawer in a house on the river."

---

Mrs Jeon put her hand flat on the green cover and left it there.

"You said Tuesday."

"Yes."

"Three people have said Tuesday to me this year."

---

She did not say what happened with the other two.

---

There is a letter on the table.

It has been opened along the fold with something thin, and the envelope is lying beside it, and both pages are lying face up where anybody coming into that room would see them.

---

She has put it where he would have to look at it.

"Sit down," said Mrs Jeon.

---

He sat down.

"It came on Saturday," she said. "First delivery. Somebody paid for that, because nothing comes to this street on a Saturday."

"Yes."

"Two pages, and four of terms behind them. A settlement function in a firm in Gwangyang that has been run out of a shoebox by somebody's nephew. Twenty-three years wanted, terms above what I was on, and a start date I may choose myself."

---

She turned the first page round with one finger so that it faced him, the way she had turned the exercise book round on Friday.

"There is a name on the first page," she said. "It is a woman's."

"Yes."

"Say it."

---

Georgij said it.

He has heard that name said out loud in a great many rooms this year, in boardrooms and on a gravel drive and by men who were frightened of it.

It has never once sounded the way it sounded in that kitchen.

---

Mrs Jeon sat back.

"And underneath it there is a paragraph that tells me why my name came up," she said. "In writing. It says that a man who works for her sat at this table on the twenty-ninth of May, and that nobody at that firm has met me. It says I may ring the number at the bottom and ask them anything I like."

"Yes."

"When did you know."

"On Friday evening," said Georgij. "She said it in front of me at about half past eight and it was decided by a quarter to nine."

"And you were out here on Friday morning and you did not come back on Saturday."

"No."

---

Mrs Jeon looked at him across her own table.

"Why not."

"Because it is not mine," he said. "She decided it, and it is her firm and her name on the page, and it is her money. If I had come out here on Saturday to tell you first, I would have taken a thing that belongs to her and used it to be liked by you."

---

The flat was quiet.

"That is an honest answer and it is not the whole one."

"No," said Georgij. "The other part is that you would have had two days of me sitting in your head being the man who arranged it, and you would have read that letter on Saturday morning as a thing I did. You would have been right to."

---

She did not thank him for that.

"I have been handled," said Mrs Jeon.

---

Georgij did not go round it.

"Yes."

"Do you know how many times in twenty-three years I have been handled by that building." She put both hands on the table. "Every increase, every rota, every time a man decided something about my week and then came and told me in a way that was designed to make me feel that I had been consulted. I know the shape of it in the dark. I have been reading it since Saturday morning and it is the shape."

"It is."

"Then say the difference, because there is one and I want to hear whether it is the same difference I have got or a worse one."

---

Georgij sat with his hands on his knees.

"You can check every word of it," he said. "Who owns the half is in the register and anybody in this country may go and read it. The reason your name came up is on the page in front of you and it is the true reason. And the number at the bottom belongs to people who will answer it whether they have been told to or not."

"And that is not the same as being asked."

"No. It is not the same as being asked."

---

She let that sit for a moment.

"In April I was rung by two civil people who would not tell me how they got my name," she said. "In May I was made redundant by a man who has never once looked me in the face. On Saturday I was handed the best thing that has happened to me in four years by a woman I have never met, and every single one of those three was decided in a room I was not in."

---

She put the second page back on top of the first.

"I am fifty-one," said Mrs Jeon. "I have four months of money and a son who is going to offer next month, and I am going to take it. And I would like it understood by somebody that taking it is not the same as being asked either."

"It is understood."

"By her?"

"She said it before the letter was written," said Georgij. "She said you would work out inside a week that you had been made safe on purpose. She said you would be entitled to resent it and that you would open the post on the first of the month anyway."

---

Mrs Jeon looked at the wall for a while.

"That is a horrible thing to be right about," she said.

"She has been right about me for eight months."

---

She had not asked him anything about himself, and he answered as though she had.

"And how do you find that."

"Some of it I have not got round to yet."

---

She poured water into two glasses without asking whether he wanted one and put one in front of him, which is the first thing she has given him that was not owed.

"You have come for something else," said Mrs Jeon. "You would have brought that book back by courier if it was only the book."

"There is one thing I did not ask you on Friday."

"Ask it."

"The invoice from the practice." Georgij put his glass down without drinking from it. "I asked you what it was. I did not ask you what it looked like."

---

Mrs Jeon put her glass down.

"I have been waiting since Friday for somebody to ask me that," she said, "and I did not know it until you said it."

---

She did not go and get the book. She has not needed the book for any of this.

"It was not a bill for a consultation," she said. "It was an attendance. A doctor went to a place and saw somebody there, and there was a line for the travelling on it and the travelling was more than the attendance."

"How much more."

"About three times." Mrs Jeon turned her hand over on the table. "I remember it because it is the only medical invoice I have ever passed where the getting there cost more than the seeing."

---

Georgij sat very still.

"It was not in this city," he said.

"It was not in this city."

"And where the name goes."

---

Mrs Jeon looked at the table between them.

"There was a box for it," she said. "Every invoice from a practice has one, because they are paid by insurers and the insurers want a person. This one was not going to an insurer. It was going to a house that settles lots."

"Say what was in the box."

"A reference," said Mrs Jeon. "Six characters. The same six that were on my clearing slip, which is the lot."

---

The room did not do anything at all.

"Somebody in a practice was given a lot number and told that it was the patient," she said, "and wrote it in the box where the name goes, and sent the bill to the auction house, and was paid inside the month. And I passed it. I looked at that box and I read six characters and I put it through and I went to lunch."

---

She had both hands flat on the table again.

"That is the ninth of January," she said. "That is what I went upstairs about. Not the four years. The lunch."

---

Georgij left the notebook in his pocket.

"You are not asking me where," said Mrs Jeon.

"No."

"Why not."

"Because you are going to be inside a firm that belongs to her by the middle of the month," said Georgij, "and the less of this that is in you, the better that is for you. You have given me a lot number and four cleared items and I have got what I needed out of that on Saturday. I do not need the rest of it from you and I am not going to take it because it is available."

---

Mrs Jeon sat with both hands round the glass.

"On Friday you gave me a thing that could take her apart," she said, "and today you will not let me give you an address."

"Yes."

"Those two do not go together."

"They go together perfectly," said Georgij. "On Friday I had nothing else to pay you with. Today I have. That is the only thing that changed and I am not going to dress it up as a principle."

---

She looked at him for a long moment and then, for the first time in either of the two mornings he has spent in that flat, something at the corner of her mouth moved.

"You are a very strange young man."

"I have been told."

---

He got up to go at about half past eleven and stopped with his hand on the back of the chair, because there was one more thing and he had been carrying it since Thursday.

"Mrs Jeon."

"What."

"Mr Hwang asked me for something and I did not answer him," said Georgij. "I want to give you the question instead of deciding it, because he asked me to arrange a thing about you without asking you, and I have had five days to notice what that is."

---

She waited.

"He said that if it turned out you were right in January, he would like there to be a piece of paper somewhere in the world. One that says you sat in that room and held the book out, and that he did not take it." He said it as nearly as he could remember it. "Not for him. He said he is finished and he has understood it properly. He said he would like it to exist."

---

Mrs Jeon did not answer for a while.

"He wants it to exist," she said.

"Yes."

"Then it will exist and I will write it," she said, "and he does not get to have it."

---

She granted it exactly as he had asked for it, and took it out of his hands in the same sentence.

---

She got up and took the two glasses to the sink.

"I will write one page and I will sign it and I will put the date on it," she said with her back to him. "And it goes to you, because you are the one who came out here, and because a thing like that is worth nothing in the hand of the man it is about."

"I will keep it."

"You will keep it and you will not use it, and if you ever do use it you will find that I am extremely difficult."

"Yes."

---

At the door she said one more thing, and it was not about any of that.

"Tell her that I read all four pages of the terms and that the third one is generous in a way that somebody thought about," said Mrs Jeon. "And tell her that I noticed it was posted on Friday night."

---

He went down two flights and past the chemist and stood at the stop.

---

Friday night.

She wrote it, signed it and had it out of the house before eleven o'clock on the night he came home and told her what he had given away.

---

Eleven minutes, she had said, and about four days.

It is the fourth day and he is standing at a bus stop with the whole of it in front of him at once. She heard the worst thing he has done to her this year, and she took eleven minutes, and inside those eleven minutes she solved the thing he had put a line through in March and not looked at since.

---

He has not been punished. He has been shown.

---

The bus came and he got on it and sat at the back with the empty inside pocket.

He thought about a woman going to lunch, four years ago, after she had put a bill through for a person with six characters where the name goes.
