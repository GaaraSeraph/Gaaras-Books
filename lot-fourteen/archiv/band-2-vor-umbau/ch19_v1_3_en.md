# Chapter 19: The third time
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.3 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Nine · Thursday 30 April

He wrote the list on the Wednesday morning as he had said he would, on paper, with dates, in the order the meals happened.

Nine of them. The thirty-first of October, the eighteenth of November, the sixteenth of December, and six others.

Then he looked at it for a while and put a line through the whole page.

---

Annie had said *met*, and he had heard *eaten with*, and he had heard it because a list of nine meals is a thing a man can do in an hour at his own desk.

The set of people he has met since October is not nine. It is somewhere over four hundred, and the great majority of them are a name on a docket and a handshake in a room with three hundred people in it.

---

That is the whole of Wednesday's work and it produced nothing except an accurate view of how much there is.

There is exactly one man he could ask who would know and would answer and would want nothing for it.

He did not ask him, because a man who telephones Park Sang-hoon to ask which people have watched a particular person eat has just told Park Sang-hoon a great deal, and Park Sang-hoon does not put things down.

---

The house on the river closed to the public on the twenty-ninth of April, which is a Wednesday and which nobody outside the trade noticed at all.

There was no notice on the door. There is a telephone number, answered by a woman who says that the spring sale has been postponed and that she is not able to say more than that. She has been saying it since the twentieth.

Mrs Sunwoo has three more lunches in the diary for May.

---

Mrs Seo left the envelope on the table outside his room on the Wednesday night without being asked, and there was a third thing in it this time.

Two tickets and the card with the platform, and a small paper bag with two of the rice cakes that the boy makes on Wednesdays.

She did not mention it in the morning and neither did he.

* * *

The yard on the thirtieth of April has two trucks in it and room for nine.

The other two went on the twenty-third and the twenty-fourth, to a dealer in Busan, at a price she will have accepted in about ninety seconds because there was nothing else on the table.

---

She came out before he was through the gate.

"Third time," said Nam Byung-hee.

"Yes."

"You have not brought anything."

"No."

"Then come up."

---

The office over the weighbridge has the same calendar on the wall and there is now nothing written on it at all, in any month.

She sat down first this time and told him to sit, which she did not do in March.

---

"I am going to say a number of things," said Georgij, "and none of them is going to help you, and I am going to say them anyway, and then I am going to answer whatever you ask me. That is the whole of what this is."

"Then say them."

---

"You were unlucky about the containers." He did not decorate any of it. "That clause was never aimed at you. Nobody in Seoul had ever heard your name when it was drafted and nobody thought about your lane for one second. There was no way for you to see it and there was nothing you could have done about it."

---

"You were unlucky about the envelope. In January a man in Seoul was asking about the small operators on this coast. He asked about you by name, and the word he was interested in was honest. That is what you were chosen for. There was no way for you to see that either."

---

Nam Byung-hee did not say anything.

---

"And at the end you were unlucky in the way you thought," said Georgij. "On one afternoon in March, after four days, you decided to put a hand on the woman I belong to."

---

He stopped there for a moment.

"That part was yours. It is the only part of the whole of it that was, and it is the reason for everything that has happened to you since. It was not luck."

---

"No," she said. "Do not."

---

The gulls did whatever gulls do over a yard with two trucks in it.

---

"Now the part that is mine," said Georgij.

---

"On the eighteenth of March, in the hour your letter came out of the envelope, I decided what I was going to do to you."

---

She looked up at that.

---

"I did not admit that to myself until the twentieth. I came down here on the twentieth and told you the truth about the veto, and you stood down. Then I got back on the train and wrote something in a notebook about a woman who is not drowning any more staying where she has been put." He said it perfectly evenly. "And I did not put it on either side of the account. I wrote it in the middle of the page as a thing I had not decided about. I had decided about it. I have known that since the twenty-third of April."

---

"So that is the part I am apologising for," he said. "Not the rest of it. The twentieth of March, and letting you thank me for it, and knowing while you did."

---

Nam Byung-hee sat and looked at the desk for a while.

---

"You could have got out of that," she said. "Nobody in the world would have known. I would not have known. You have just handed me the one piece of it that you did not have to hand anybody."

"Yes."

"Why."

"Because I said I would not tell you one untrue thing, and leaving that out is the only untrue thing available in this room."

---

She did not accept it and she did not reject it. She put it somewhere and went on.

---

"Two things you should know," said Georgij. "They are both true and neither of them is a comfort, and I would like you to have them anyway."

"Say them."

---

"Your nephew started at Mr Kwon's on the twentieth of April. He is on the nine-truck rota, he is on a proper wage for the first time, and he will still be there in ten years if he wants to be. That was arranged and it was arranged by me, and I checked on Monday that it had actually happened, because I do not take anybody's word for a thing I have said I would do."

---

Nam Byung-hee put one hand flat on the desk and left it there.

"He telephoned me on the twenty-first and I could not work out why he was frightened to tell me."

"He was frightened because he thought you would think he had gone over."

"He had gone over."

"Yes," said Georgij. "And he is twenty-three, and he is not the one who wrote the letter."

---

She took a moment over that.

"And the second thing."

---

"The man who chose you is going to get exactly what you have got."

---

"You do not know who he is."

"No. I have nine lines of description and a sentence he said to somebody in January, and I am going to have him before the end of the year." Georgij did not raise his voice at any point. "When I do, he will lose the whole of it, and he will lose it slowly enough to watch it coming. You did not. That is the only respect in which you have been treated better than he is going to be."

---

"You are telling me that to make me feel better."

"I am telling you because it is true and because you asked in your letter to be answered." He looked at her. "It is the only answer I have got and it is four months late."

---

The room over the weighbridge did the thing rooms do.

---

"One question," said Nam Byung-hee.

"Ask it."

---

"Was it you, or was it her."

---

There it was, and she had waited an hour and a half of her own life to ask it, and she asked it in the same voice she reads everything in.

---

"It was me," said Georgij. "I asked her for it on the third of April, standing up, and she could have said no and did not. She permitted it. She did not order it, she did not suggest it, and there is no version of this in which I was doing what I was told."

---

"So it was personal."

"It was entirely personal."

---

"Why," said Nam Byung-hee. "You had never met me. I did not know your name until you wrote it on the corner of a pad in this room."

---

"Because the thing you picked up and pointed at her was me."

---

She did not understand it at first, and he watched her not understand it, and then he watched her arrive.

---

"Lot fourteen," she said.

"Lot fourteen."

"On the page. In my coat, for twelve days."

"Yes."

---

Nam Byung-hee sat back in her chair and put both hands in her lap and did not say anything for a long time.

---

"I have thought about that page every day since the fourteenth of March," she said at last, "and it never once occurred to me that there was a person on it."

"No. That is what a page is for."

---

"And the man in January knew there was."

"The man in January knew exactly whose line it was before he ever heard of you." Georgij said it flatly. "He gave you a page with a man on it and did not tell you the man was on it, and then he went home and had his dinner."

---

She looked out of the window at two trucks.

---

"Then I am going to say something and you are going to let me finish it," she said.

"Yes."

"I am not sorry that I wrote it. I have tried for a week to be and I am not." She was quite steady. "I had forty-one people and no work and nobody would tell me why, and I wrote one letter, and I would write it again tomorrow if I did not know what I know now. That is the truth and I am not going to improve it for you."

---

"No," said Georgij. "I would not have believed you if you had."

---

"But I understand it now," she said, "which I did not on Thursday, and I would rather understand it than not, and I do not expect that to be any use to either of us."

---

He stood up, because there was nothing left that was both true and sayable, and he has been in that position four times in his life and twice of them have been in this room.

---

At the gate she said the last of it.

"You are going to tell me not to repeat any of it."

---

"No," said Georgij.

---

"You can say all of it, to anybody, for the rest of your life." He said it without any weight on it. "Nobody is going to do anything about it, and I would like you to understand why that is, because it is the only part of this you can still get wrong."

"Say it, then."

---

"By the end of May there will not be one firm on this coast that is not better off than it was in December, and every one of them will know exactly who it is better off because of. A man whose yard has just been made worth something does not repeat a thing like that. He does not decide not to. It simply does not occur to him."

---

Nam Byung-hee stood in her own gate with her hands in her coat.

"And if I do it anyway."

---

"Then I will come here a fourth time," said Georgij, "and there will be nothing in my hands then either."

---

She looked at him for a while.

"That is the only thing you have said today that you enjoyed."

---

He thought about that on the platform for twenty minutes and on the train for four hours, and he has not got an answer to it that he is willing to write down.
