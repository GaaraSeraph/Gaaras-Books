# Chapter 84: The consignor of record
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.2 · EN

## Day Three Hundred and Eighty-Six · Saturday 24 October

Mr Hwang telephoned the house on the Saturday morning at nine, which is not a thing that man does, and said so himself in the first sentence.

"I do not telephone people at the weekend and I have not done it since 2019," he said. "I have been through the file properly and there is a thing in it that neither of us said out loud on Thursday, because we both assumed it. Ruining your Saturday is cheaper than ruining your November."

"The third thing."

"An assignment out of a wind-up needs three things and I gave you two of them. The administrator consents. The holder agrees a price and takes the money." He did not hurry any of it. "And the consignor of record instructs the house to make the transfer, and the house cannot act without that instruction, and there is no substitute for it anywhere in the terms."

Georgij stood in the passage with the receiver against his ear.

"Who the consignor of record is on that lot."

"You know who it is. You sat in his workshop in July."

* * *

He got the rest of it in about four minutes and he wrote none of it down, because he did not need to.

The consignor is the person who signed the consignment form at the house on the river in February four years ago. On that lot it is a man of thirty-nine who mends small motors, and the signature is his, and the address on the form is the workshop.

The consignor does not own the lot and has not owned it since the day of the placing. The consignor does not take the money. The consideration goes to the holder, in full, and the consignor is not a party to it and never sees the figure.

"He receives nothing," said Mr Hwang. "I want you to be exact about that when you say it to him, because he will assume otherwise and he will assume it in about a second."

"Then what does he do."

"He signs a form that says the house may transfer a lot he placed to a person who is buying it." Mr Hwang was entirely level. "It is one page and it has four lines on it and one of them is the lot reference."

* * *

Neither of them said anything for a moment.

"It is the same form," said Georgij.

"It is the same form. It is the reverse of the same form, and it is printed on the reverse of the same form, because in 2003 somebody in this house decided it was tidier that way and nobody has changed it since." Mr Hwang did not soften it. "He will turn it over and find his own signature from February four years ago on the other side of the sheet he is being asked to sign."

"Whether that can be avoided."

"It can be avoided by me typing out a fresh sheet, which is not the form, and which the holder's people will look at for four seconds and reject." He stopped. "I have thought about it since seven this morning and there is no version. I am sorry about that and being sorry is worth nothing."

* * *

## Day Three Hundred and Eighty-Eight · Monday 26 October

He went up the hill on the Monday with the form in his inside pocket, which is the first time he has gone up there carrying anything at all.

He noticed that at the third street and did not do anything with it.

The fan is running in the corner.

* * *

Mr Ahn looked at his coat, and then at his face, and put the screwdriver down.

"You've brought something," he said.

"I have."

"Then it's the day, or it's the other thing."

"It is neither. It is worse than the first and better than the second." Georgij did not sit down. "There is a piece of paper and only you can sign it. I am going to tell you the whole of what it is before I take it out of my pocket. Then I am going to put it on the bench and go and stand over there."

Mr Ahn wiped his hands on the cloth.

"Then talk," he said.

* * *

He talked for six minutes.

The house consents. The money exists and is in one place. The holder will be paid in full and the figure is not Mr Ahn's business and never will be. The house cannot move a lot that somebody placed without an instruction from the person who placed it. That person is him. There is nobody else and there has never been anybody else since February four years ago.

"And I get nothing," said Mr Ahn.

"You get nothing. Not a won, not a fee, not a share of it, and you are not a party to the price and you will never be told what it is." Georgij said it exactly the way Hwang had said it to him. "I was asked to be precise about that because you would assume otherwise, and you did, and it took about a second."

"It took less than that."

* * *

"There is a second thing about the form," said Georgij, "and I would rather you had it from me standing here than found it in about four seconds at that bench."

"Standing here is fine."

"It is printed on the back of the consignment form. The same sheet. Somebody in that house decided in 2003 that it was tidier and nobody has changed it." He did not look away. "You will turn it over and your own signature from February will be on the other side of it."

Mr Ahn did not say anything at all for about five seconds.

Then he put the cloth down on the bench and held his hand out.

* * *

Georgij took the form out and put it on the bench, face up, and went and stood by the door, which is eleven feet away and is as far as that room goes.

Mr Ahn did not turn it over.

He read the four lines on the front twice, with both hands flat on the bench on either side of the paper, and he took about ninety seconds over four lines.

Then he turned it over.

* * *

He looked at the back of it for a long time.

The shop did what a shop does on a Monday morning, which is compressor, and then not, and then the fan.

"I was thirty-five," he said.

"Yes."

"I've thought about that afternoon about nine thousand times and I've never once been able to remember what the room looked like." He had not moved his hands. "I remember the pen. It was a house pen and it was chained to the desk, and I remember thinking that a place that chains its pens doesn't think much of the people who come in."

Georgij stayed by the door.

* * *

"Come here," said Mr Ahn.

He went over.

"Read me the four lines on the front. I have read them and I want them said out loud by somebody who is not me."

Georgij read the four lines out loud.

They are not dramatic. They say that the undersigned, being the consignor of record on the reference given, instructs the house to give effect to a transfer of the said consignment to the buyer named in the schedule. And that the undersigned makes no claim on the consideration.

"Again," said Mr Ahn.

He read them again.

* * *

"That's a better sentence than the one on the back," said Mr Ahn.

"There is a why, because you have a reason and I would like it."

"The one on the back says I'm placing her." He had one finger beside the paper and not on it. "This one says the house is to give effect to something. It isn't me doing it. It's me telling them to stop being in the way."

"That is what it is."

"Then somebody in 2003 was cleverer than they knew, and I'm going to be grateful to a dead stationery decision for the rest of my life."

* * *

He signed it.

He signed it with a biro out of the tin on the bench, and the tin has eleven pens in it and he did not look at which one he took, and it took about two seconds.

Then he sat down on the stool, which he had not done, and put both hands on his knees.

"What happens now," he said.

"The form goes to the house tomorrow morning. The house serves it on the holder with the price. The holder has no reason to refuse money for a thing he is about to have to move anyway, and every reason to take it, and Mr Hwang thinks it is days rather than weeks."

"And then somebody drives up there and she comes out."

"Yes."

* * *

Mr Ahn looked at the form on the bench and did not pick it up.

"I want the words she gets when they come," he said. "Not ours. Theirs."

"I do not know and I have thought about it since the fifteenth. There is no version in which somebody in that house sits down with her and explains an assignment."

"So she gets a morning where men come in and she is told to bring her things."

"That is the likely one."

Mr Ahn put one hand flat on the bench beside the paper.

"Then whoever is in the car has about four minutes to be the first person in four years who says a true sentence to her," he said, "and it had better not be a man."

* * *

"I want to say the thing I am not going to be able to say afterwards," said Mr Ahn, "and I am going to say it now, at eleven o'clock on a Monday, because afterwards there will be too much of it and it will come out wrong."

"Then now, and not afterwards."

"I've been in this shop for four years knowing there was nothing I could do." He was looking at the bench. "There was one thing. It was one signature on the back of a piece of paper I'd already signed once. I couldn't have known it and nobody could have told me, and I've still spent four years being right that there was nothing."

"You were right. There was nothing until the fifteenth of October."

"I know that. It isn't going to help me at four in the morning and it's true." He looked up. "Say the other thing. The one you have been holding since you came in."

* * *

Georgij put both hands at his sides.

"There is a woman in that building on oxygen and she has been there eleven years," he said, "and there is no form for her, because there was never a consignment, because nobody ever signed anything. She has no lot and no reference and no line in that house."

"Then how does she come out."

"I do not know. There is a solicitor in Yeouido who has paid for her oxygen forty-four times in eleven years and there is a name he will not say to me until this is over." Georgij did not soften it. "That is the whole of what I have, and it is nothing that gets a person out of a building, and I am telling you because you asked me a question in September that I answered badly."

Mr Ahn nodded once.

"Then when it's your turn to go up that road," he said, "you're not going up it for one."

"No."

* * *

He came down the hill and it took eight minutes and he did the arithmetic in the car with the engine off.

*Four years and eleven weeks, and the whole of it turned on a man who has been forty feet from a signature the entire time.*

*He did not sign it because he was persuaded.*

*He signed it because a clerk in 2003 printed the second form on the back of the first one, and because he read four lines twice and worked out what they said.*

---

*And I stood by the door for that, which is eleven feet, and it is the only decision in this whole arrangement that I have not been standing next to when it was made.*
