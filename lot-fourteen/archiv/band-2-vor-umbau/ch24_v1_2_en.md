# Chapter 24: Ten minutes on a Friday
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.2 · EN

## Day Two Hundred and Twenty-Four · Friday 15 May

He did it at twenty past nine on the Friday morning, at the desk with the east window, and it took ten minutes.

There was nobody in the room. Nobody had put a ceiling on it, nobody was going to audit it, and there was no room he had to walk into afterwards and say what it had cost.

That is what Annie meant on the thirtieth of April and he had not understood the size of it until he was doing it.

---

What he ordered is the dullest thing in this country.

A commercial register extract on ten companies at one address in Jung-gu. Officers, shareholdings, changes in the last five years, and the filings that go with them. It is a form and a fee and it is done about four thousand times a day by clerks who are thinking about lunch.

It goes in with a date on it and the name of the person who asked.

---

He put his own name on it, because there is nobody else's he could have used.

---

That is the whole of the decision. It is not a raid and it is not a threat and there is no version of it that anybody could complain about, and it is the single most visible thing he has done since October.

Somebody on the third floor of that building has an arrangement with somebody at that registry, or does not need one, and either way there will be a line in a log by Tuesday saying who asked and when.

---

He sat with the receipt for about a minute afterwards and waited to feel something about it.

Nothing came. It is a form and a fee.

---

He told Annie at seven that evening and she did not ask him one question about it.

"When."

"Twenty past nine this morning."

"And you have been walking round this house since twenty past nine this morning without saying anything."

"Yes."

---

"Good," said Annie.

---

He waited for the rest of it and the rest of it did not come. She went back to what she was doing, and that is the whole of what was said in that house about the largest decision he has made in his life.

---

That is also what she meant on the thirtieth of April, and it is worse than the first part.

---

The Saturday and the Sunday were the two longest days he has had since October.

There is nothing whatever to do about a form once it has gone in. He read three things he had already read. He went twice to a window that looks at a drive. At some point on the Sunday afternoon he found himself standing in the hall for a reason he could not afterwards account for.

---

Jang saw him do it and did not say anything about it.

Half an hour later there were two men on the gate instead of one, and there have been two on it every day since, and nobody has mentioned that either.

---

Nothing happened about the form on the Monday.

---

Something else did. On the Monday the first of the five was signed.

Mr Kwon's partner, sixty-three, sold his half at a price that was arrived at in one meeting and did not have to be argued about. Then he went out of the office and sat in his own car for a while before he started it.

He is going to live about forty minutes from his daughter. Mr Kwon telephoned Georgij about it and Georgij did not take the call, because there was nothing to say into it that was not either a lie or unkind.

---

The buyer of the half is a company that has existed since 2019, has an office in Busan and one employee, and has never previously bought anything.

Nobody on that coast has looked into it and nobody is going to, because a man who has just been paid a real price does not spend the afternoon on a register search.

---

Nothing happened on the Tuesday morning either.

---

At twenty to three on the Tuesday afternoon the telephone in the small room rang, which happens perhaps six times a day, and Mrs Seo answered it in the hall as she does.

She came up herself, which she does not.

---

"There is a gentleman on the telephone," she said. "He has asked for you by name and he has your name correctly, and he has not asked for her at all."

---

Georgij stood up from the desk.

"Did he give one."

"He gave a name and a company and both of them are real, because I asked him to spell the company and he spelled it without any hesitation and did not sound annoyed about it." Mrs Seo did not move from the doorway. "I have been answering that telephone for nine years and nobody has ever asked for you."

---

"No."

"That is what I came up about."

---

He went down and took it in the small room with the door open, because there was no reason to shut it and because shutting it would have been a decision as well.

---

The voice was about sixty and completely comfortable.

---

"My name is Yeom," he said. "You put a form in on Friday morning about an address in Jung-gu, and I am on the third floor of it." He was not hurrying. "I thought it would be quicker to telephone you than to let you spend four hundred thousand won finding out that there is nothing in those filings."

---

"That was quick," said Georgij.

"It was Friday. It is Tuesday. That is not quick, that is a long weekend." He was not being clever about it, which was the unpleasant part. "I would have telephoned on Monday and my secretary was away."

---

Georgij did not say anything for a moment.

---

"You are not going to ask me how I know it was you," said Mr Yeom.

"No."

"Why not."

"Because you have just told me. You said it in the second half of your first sentence and you did not notice, or you did notice and you wanted me to have it. Either way it is the same information."

---

There was a pause on the other end of it that was about half a second longer than it needed to be.

---

"That is a very good answer," said Mr Yeom. "I am going to think about it later. Now. You have been in the register at that house. You have been to see Byun in Seongdong. You were in the building at the beginning of May talking to Hwang. I do not know what you want and I would like to."

---

"I would like to as well," said Georgij.

"I am sorry?"

"You have just told me three things I did about a house you sold in December, and you told them to me in the order they happened, and you have been out of that business for five months. So I would like to know what you want, and I have got a stronger reason for asking than you have."

---

Mr Yeom laughed at that.

It was a real one and it went on for about two seconds, and there was nothing anywhere in it that he could take hold of.

---

"Come and have lunch," he said.

---

The room did the thing rooms do.

---

"When?" said Georgij.

"Thursday. There is a place near the office and I have had the same table there since 2016, and it is not private and I want you to know that in advance. Four other people will be able to see us the whole time."

"Who else is coming."

"Nobody. It is lunch. If I wanted a room with people in it I would have suggested a room with people in it."

---

Georgij stood in the small room with the telephone in his hand and the door open.

---

"Thursday," he said.

"Half past twelve. I will telephone you here on Wednesday to confirm it and I will ask for you by name again. I would like you to notice that I am doing that rather than having somebody bring you a card."

---

"I have noticed."

"Good," said Mr Yeom, and put the telephone down.

---

He stood there for a while afterwards with his hand still on it.

---

There is one more thing in that telephone call and he got to it on the stairs.

Mr Yeom knows he was in that building at the beginning of May. There was nobody at the desk that day, nobody in the corridor, and one man in a room with three crates.

---

Either Hwang told him, which is entirely possible and which Georgij would not hold against a man who tells everybody everything as a matter of principle.

Or somebody has opened a visitors' book that has been in a crate since the eighth of May, and read two lines in Georgij's own handwriting with the time in and the time out.

---

He wrote both of them himself, at a desk nobody was standing at.

It seemed to him at the time that a man who signs a book when somebody is watching should sign it when nobody is.

---

Then he went up and wrote four lines and looked at the fourth one for a long time.

---

The first three are ordinary. A man of sixty-three, out of a business he sold, tracking a register search inside two working days and being pleasant about it.

The fourth is not ordinary and it is the reason he is going.

---

*Lunch.*

---

On the twenty-seventh of April, in the small room, Annie gave him one true thing about the man he is looking for and it was not a name.

He does not eat in front of people he is working on. It is not a discipline and it is not a superstition. He simply cannot do both at once, and he has never in his life noticed that it shows.

---

Every man in this city who has ever sat down to a meal with him and watched him eat is a man he was not working on.

---

Somebody has just invited Georgij to lunch.

---

He does not know whether Mr Yeom is that man, or one of that man's, or somebody who has been told to do a thing and not told why. He is not going to know that on Thursday either.

He knows one thing and it is worth the four hundred thousand won he is not now going to spend.

A table at half past twelve is a table at which somebody is going to have to decide whether to eat.

---

He went down at about eight and told Annie all of it in order, and she listened to the whole of it without moving.

---

"He telephoned this house," she said, at the end.

"He telephoned this house and asked for me by name and did not ask for you, and Mrs Seo came upstairs about it, which she has not done in nine years."

---

Annie looked at the window for a moment.

---

"Then it is done," she said. "You spent it on Friday morning at twenty past nine and it took four working days to arrive." She held his eye for it. "I want you to hear me say that I think you were right, because I am not going to be able to say it again after Thursday."

---

"Why not."

"Because after Thursday one of two things will be true," said Annie. "Either you have found the bottom of it, in which case nobody is going to care what I thought on a Tuesday. Or somebody sat down opposite you and did not eat, and after that neither of us is going to have the luxury of an opinion about anything."
