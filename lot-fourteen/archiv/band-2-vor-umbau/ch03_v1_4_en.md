# Chapter 3: We haven't met
*Lot Fourteen* · Version 1.4 · EN

## Day One Hundred and Fifty-Five · Saturday 7 March

Nobody had asked him for any of it.

On the Wednesday night Annie had asked for the Ulsan one by name, from the filings and then from somebody who had stood at that gate. That is three days of work for a man who is quick, and he had it by Thursday afternoon.

He spent Thursday evening, Friday and Saturday on something else.

---

He closed the Ulsan file at about four on the Thursday and had three days in front of him with nothing in them.

On the Monday she had stopped at the door on her way out and told him he was working again, and that nobody had told him what to want. She had meant it as a release. He had taken it as one, and he is fairly certain she did not mean this.

Then he went down and took the catalogue off the desk in the small room and carried it upstairs, which is the first thing he has ever taken out of that room without being handed it.

---

What he had was four references and three seasons, and no column for who paid.

Two of the references were the fourth of October. Eleven and fourteen, four lines apart, and he had been in the room for both of them and had been one of them.

The other two were older. One was three years back. One was the year before that.

---

The house was never going to tell him anything and he did not go near it.

What he went at instead was the outside of an evening.

An auction of that kind is four hours in a building with a public stair and a private entrance. The people who come to it arrive in cars that somebody has to park and to wait in. The men who wait get fed at somebody's expense and then invoiced for. There is a settlement desk and there are runners who go down to the seats. There is a florist. There is a firm that puts out the chairs and takes them away again.

None of those people are in the book. All of them have their own paper, and their paper has dates on it.

---

He had done this once before, in his first fortnight, for a charity gala with eight hundred people at it, and it had given him four hundred and thirty names and had not given him Choi Dae-ho.

He did it again for an evening three years old.

---

He did it at the desk in his own room, which has a window on the east side and a lamp that Mrs Seo replaced in November without being asked and without mentioning it.

The boy brought a tray up on the Friday at one because nobody had come down. He put it at the corner of the desk on the side where there was room, which is a thing that has to be worked out and not taught.

Georgij thanked him and did not look up, and that was the whole of the human part of Friday.

---

It took Friday and most of Saturday and it worked.

Cars, in the end. A firm in Jung-gu that supplies drivers by the hour keeps its dockets for seven years, on account of a tax dispute it lost and has not got over. A docket carries a pick-up address and a drop. A drop that is the same street four times in three years is a customer.

Then the drop is a building, and the building has one tenant that receives people in the evening.

Then it is a matter of which of the four dockets falls on the right night, and after that of whose account the hours went to.

---

The account was a company. The company was a nothing, two rooms and a secretary, and it took Georgij about an hour to get behind it because whoever set it up had not been trying very hard, which by itself said something.

Behind it was a woman of eighty-one who has been charming for forty years and has never in that time told anybody anything all the way through.

---

Georgij put the sheet down on the desk and did not do any more work for a while.

---

He had shaken her hand in a ballroom on the twenty-fifth of October. She had come at him from the left at the ten-minute mark. Three weeks before that she had sat two rows in front of the woman who bought him, and had turned round with everybody else when a man in the second row stood up.

*We haven't met.*

He had thought at the time that it was a courtesy, and that she was giving him the evening clean because she was the sort of woman who understood what an entrance costs.

It was not a courtesy. It was a woman with something on that page, being extremely careful in front of a man who had just come off it.

---

He worked out on Saturday afternoon what she had actually been doing, and it took him under a minute once he stopped being flattered.

She had not been protecting him. She had been finding out, in four words and without asking, whether the new one at that table knew what she was.

He had told her, with his face, that he did not. She had given him six or seven minutes of nothing at all after that and had gone away pleased.

---

What he does not have, and will not get out of a docket, is what was on the line.

A reference, a season and a stroke of a pen. Somebody stood in that building three years ago with a number over their head, and a woman of eighty-one bought them, and then paid a second time to have the fact taken out of the book.

There are two reasons a person pays for that, and one of them is the reason Annie had. From the outside of an evening they look exactly alike.

---

The other one, the fourth, is not there.

Nobody ordered a car. Nobody was fed. The chair firm invoiced for the room and the florist invoiced for the room and there is no third piece of paper anywhere with that evening on it.

Georgij went at it twice and then put it down and wrote a line to himself about the age of the records, which was reasonable.

---

He had three of the four and one name out of two, and the name is the wrong half.

Deterrence needs the other side of it. A page with Annie on it and a woman of eighty-one on it is a page with two people on it who can both be hurt, and that is not a deadlock, that is a queue.

What he needs is somebody so large that the page cannot be used at all, and that one is the one with no car, no dinner and no paper.

---

He considered going to her, for about as long as it takes to put a cup down.

Against it: she is eighty-one and has been careful since before he was born, and a woman like that gets warned once. Walk up her drive with that page in your head and she knows within a sentence that somebody has read it, and after that she is careful about a specific thing instead of about everything, which is worse.

For it: nothing at all. He has no currency she wants. He is a man with no money who has already given her, in a ballroom, the one piece of information she went there to get.

So it is one approach and he has not got it ready, and the rule for that is the one Hana said out loud on a Thursday in November about a different woman who receives nobody. Not quickly and not twice.

* * *

Annie came in at nine with the Yeouido people gone and her coat already off.

He gave her Ulsan.

---

The name is Nam Byung-hee. She has the firm, which is one lane and nothing else, and she has run it since her brother died. She also has the chair of the association of independent shippers on that lane, which has existed for twenty years and which nobody has ever wanted before, because until January there was nothing on that lane to negotiate about.

Six firms. She writes to them on the first of every month whether there is anything to write about or not.

Georgij had that last part from a man who has stood at the gate, which is what had been asked for.

---

Annie went through it and asked four things.

Two were about the six firms. One was about the association's own constitution, which he had read on the Thursday. It is mostly nothing, apart from a single clause about who may speak for the members in a dealing with a carrier.

The fourth was whether Nam Byung-hee had ever held anything outside this trade, and the answer is no, and Annie put her hand flat on the arm of the chair when she heard it.

"She writes on the first," she said.

"Every month. Twenty years of it. The last three say nothing, and she sent them anyway."

---

Annie looked at that for a moment.

"Then she is not somebody who is going to be told to be patient."

"No, Mistress."

"What have you been doing since Thursday afternoon?"

---

Georgij had known the question was coming since about four o'clock and had decided what he was going to do about it, and doing it turned out to be worse than deciding it.

"Something of my own. I will tell you, and I am not going to tell you tonight."

---

Annie did not move at all.

"Is it going to cost me anything?"

"Not yet. On the day it starts to, you will have all of it that day, and you will not have to ask me for it."

"And if I ask you now."

"Then I will tell you now." He said that at the same speed as the rest of it. "I would rather you did not ask, and I am saying so instead of letting you find it out."

---

The clock in the hall did the thing it does at the quarter.

"Keep it, then," said Annie.

---

She went past him and put her hand on the back of his neck on the way, the way she had twice in February, and went straight on to whatever she had been going to.

---

He stood in the hall afterwards and did the arithmetic, because he does.

He had given her a term. Not tonight, and on the day it costs you, and if you ask I will tell you. Three limits on a thing he was keeping back, all of them said out loud, all of them binding on him and on nobody else.

That is how he does it. He has done it since October: a ceiling, named counterparties, a date after which it is dead and I ask you again.

---

In six days she had stepped over two of his questions and had not put a date on either.

He went up and did not make anything of it. There is nothing to make of it that would survive being said out loud. And a man who counts what he is owed by the person he belongs to has started keeping a different sort of book.

---

He got as far as the landing before he heard which word he had used.

*Belongs.* On the seventh of March, by a man with no line, no owner and no date anywhere in the world.

He did not correct it.
