Book Two · Chapter 12: Sixteen days
Lot Fourteen · Version 1.1 · EN

Day One Hundred and Eighty-Two · Friday 3 April

He went down to the small room at nine with nothing to say, and that had not happened since the second of March.

Mrs Sunwoo is having lunch with people and does not need him. Cargo is moving on a lane in Ulsan and nobody is going to write about it. There is no document in the world that says what he is allowed to do, and there has not been since Tuesday.

For the first time in a month he stood in that room and there was no report.

* * *

Annie let it go on for about forty seconds, which is a long time in a room with two people in it and nothing on the desk.

"Sit down."

"I am all right standing."

"I know you are. Sit down."

* * *

He sat.

* * *

"You have been squaring things off since the eighteenth of March," said Annie.

* * *

"Mistress."

"Paper. Cups. The blotter, twice, in front of me. You did it to that letter in this room on the day it came, and I watched you do it, and I have watched you do it about a dozen times since." She was not making an accusation of it. "You do not fidget. You have never fidgeted in your life. You put things straight."

* * *

"Yes."

"Say the date out loud."

"The eighteenth of March."

"Sixteen days," said Annie.

* * *

He did not say anything to that, and she let it sit for a moment, and then she did the thing he had not expected, which was to try to take it off him.

* * *

"She is fifty-eight," said Annie. "She has four trucks and a yard with room for nine and one lane, and since January she has had nothing. You went down there and took the water off her and she stopped. That is normally the end of it."

"I know what she is."

"Then tell me what is left over, because from where I am sitting the matter is closed and has been closed for a fortnight."

* * *

Georgij put both hands on his knees and did not move them.

"She was right," he said. "Every word in that letter was true, and I said so in this room, and I have not changed my mind about one line of it. She was strangled by something she could not see and nobody would tell her and there was no office to write to. If somebody did that to you I would take that person apart and I would not need a second reason."

"Then we agree."

"We do not agree about what she did afterwards."

* * *

"Say it."

* * *

"She thought about it for four days." He said it perfectly evenly. "That is in her own writing, in the third paragraph, and she put it there because she wanted me to know she had not done it in temper. Four days. Then she took a sheet of association letterhead, which is not hers, and she wrote to you by name, and she signed it Chair, and she posted it registered so that there would be a record of it."

* * *

Annie did not move.

"That is what a careful person does."

"That is what a careful person does when she has decided." Georgij did not raise his voice at any point in the whole of it, and it is possible that he could not have. "She did not write to a company. She did not write to a lawyer. She sat in an office over a weighbridge and she thought for four days about how to put a hand on you, and then she did it in the way that could be proved afterwards."

* * *

"And the other half," said Annie. "You have got two and you have said one."

* * *

He looked at the desk for a moment.

"The thing she picked up and pointed at you was me."

* * *

Annie sat back very slowly.

* * *

"There are four lines on that page," said Georgij. "Two of them are yours. The one she was holding is the fourth of October. She did not know that, and she still does not know it in the way that matters, and none of that changes what the object was." He stopped, and then he did not stop. "She went looking for something that would make you do what she wanted. What came to hand was me. She picked it up and aimed it and wrote a letter with it, and she took four days over the wording."

"You are the weapon in that sentence."

"I am the weapon in every sentence. I have never minded that." He looked up. "I mind it when the hand is not yours."

* * *

Nobody said anything for a while after that.

* * *

Annie stood up and went round the desk and stood in front of him, which she does not do, and looked at him for a long time in a way that had nothing sentimental in it at all.

She was checking. He understood that she was checking and sat still for it.

* * *

"You are not angry," she said at last.

"Mistress."

"That is not a correction, it is a finding. Anger makes people quick and rough and I cannot use anybody in that condition." She had not stopped looking at him. "You have been carrying this for sixteen days. In those sixteen days you went to Ulsan and took the pressure off the one person who could hurt me. You gave an eighty-one-year-old woman one sentence and no more. You went back down there and said the thing that costs you everything you have out loud, in a room, so that she would have nothing to keep."

* * *

"Yes."

"You have not made one mistake." She said it flatly, and it was not praise. "That is not what a man in a temper does. That is what a man does who has decided something and is waiting."

* * *

"Yes," said Georgij.

* * *

He stood up then, because what came next could not be said sitting down in front of her.

* * *

"May I have this one, Mistress?"

* * *

"Say all of it."

"I want her. Not the letter, not the lane, not an apology, and not a settlement." He kept his hands where she could see them. "I want everything she has, and I want it done by people who are entitled to take it from her, and I want her to be somebody nobody in that trade will read a letter from ever again."

* * *

"And the price," said Annie. "Before you pay it. You agreed to that on the eleventh of March."

* * *

"The price is the arrangement."

* * *

She waited.

"I cannot tell you what a thing costs before I pay for it," said Georgij. "Not in this. Half of what I am going to do, I will not know I am doing until the morning it becomes possible, and the other half will be answering somebody else inside an hour. If I stop to come home and name a figure, I will lose the week." He did not soften it. "So I am asking you to take the ceiling off. You will have every part of it afterwards, in one piece, in order, and you can decide then what you think of me."

* * *

"That is the whole of what you are asking for."

"That is the whole of it."

* * *

Annie went back round the desk, which took her about four seconds, and he understood that she was buying them.

* * *

"What do I get," she said.

"The lane."

* * *

She looked up at that.

"Say it properly."

"There are six firms in that association and one of them is hers. When it is over the other five will be yours, completely, not as customers and not as an arrangement." Georgij did not decorate any of it. "They are dependent, they are frightened, and in about three weeks they are going to be extremely grateful to somebody. That somebody can be you and it will cost you nothing you have not already spent."

* * *

"You have thought about this for sixteen days."

"I have thought about nothing else for sixteen days, and I have not written any of it down."

* * *

"And you are giving me the lane so that this is not you spending my month on your own feelings."

"Yes." He did not look away. "It is also true that I want it. Both of those are the case, and the second one is not the smaller of the two."

* * *

Annie put her hand flat on the desk.

"Two conditions," she said, "and neither of them is about her."

* * *

"You will not say one untrue thing to that woman. Not one, not to make it faster, not to make it land harder, not at the end when she asks you why." She said it very precisely. "She does not come into it. That rule is the only thing you have ever had that is yours, and I am not going to watch you spend it on a haulier in Ulsan."

* * *

There was a pause of about two seconds.

"I will not need to," said Georgij.

"No," said Annie. "I know."

* * *

"The second."

"When it is finished you will come to this room and tell me whether you enjoyed it." She held his eye. "And you will tell me the truth about that, and I am aware of what I am asking for, and I am asking for it anyway."

* * *

"Yes, Mistress."

"Say it as a sentence."

"When it is finished I will tell you whether I enjoyed it, and it will be the true answer and not the one that is easier to say in this room."

* * *

"Then go," said Annie.

* * *

He was at the door when she said the last of it, and she did not get up to say it.

"Georgij."

"Mistress."

"I want her finished. I do not want her hurt for four months so that somebody can watch." She had picked up something off the desk and was not looking at it. "Park Sang-hoon takes eighteen months over a man because he enjoys the second year. You are not going to become that, and if you find that you are becoming that you will stop, and you will not wait for me to notice."

* * *

"It will not take four months."

"How long."

"Three weeks. Perhaps four if the second one is careful, and he will be, because he has been careful since October."

* * *

Annie put the thing down.

"You have two of them."

"I have two of them. She is first, because she is the one who touched you, and because taking her apart is the only way I get to look at the other one from underneath."

* * *

He went out to the cars at about ten.

Jang was at the near end of the garages with the bonnet of the second car up and nothing wrong with it, which is what he does when he wants to be findable.

* * *

"I am going to need people," said Georgij.

Jang wiped his hands and did not ask what for, which took a decision, because he is not incurious.

"What sort."

* * *

"Not the sort you are thinking of. I do not need anybody hurt and I am not going to have anybody frightened in a car park." Georgij put his hands behind his back. "I need to know which firms on one lane out of Ulsan are behind on what, who they owe it to, and which of them has a driver who has stopped being paid on time. That is six companies and about forty people, and none of it is in a filing anywhere."

* * *

Jang thought about that for a moment.

"Drivers," he said.

"Drivers."

"I have known every man who drives for a house in this city for the better part of thirty years. About half of them owe me something. The ones who do not will talk anyway, because nobody has ever asked them anything in their lives." He put the bonnet down. "Give me three days."

* * *

"Thank you."

"You will want the ones in Ulsan and not the ones here." Jang said it without any particular expression. "That is two telephone calls further and it is the same two calls. And Georgij."

"Yes."

"You are asking me and not Mrs Seo. I have noticed that, and I would like it to go on."
