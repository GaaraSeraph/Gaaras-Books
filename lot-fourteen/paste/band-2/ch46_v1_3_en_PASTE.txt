Book Two · Chapter 46: The post still comes
Lot Fourteen · Version 1.3 · EN

Day Two Hundred and Eighty-Three · Monday 13 July

He had the address for a week before he went, and the week was on purpose, and he wrote down why on the Monday night so that it could be held against him later.

* * *

Five kitchens in one trade know that somebody has been asking about a man who is liked by kitchens. If any of that has moved, it has had three weeks to move, and the first place it would arrive is the address where his post goes.

So: not this week. Next week, on a Monday morning, when a street is at its least interested in anybody standing in it.

* * *

It is in Seodaemun, on a hill, in a building of eight flats with a lift that was installed after the building was, in the way you can tell from outside because the shaft is bolted on.

* * *

Georgij did not go in.

* * *

He stood on the far side of the road for eleven minutes with a folded newspaper he was not reading, and he counted what a building tells you when you have not been invited into it. Which windows have curtains and which have blinds. Which balcony has a drying rack in use on a Monday. Which of the eight has a satellite dish, and which has nothing at all.

* * *

The fourth floor, on the left, has nothing at all. No rack, no dish, no plant, and a window that has been shut long enough that the inside of the glass has gone the colour glass goes.

* * *

The gas bill for a house in Gangwon-do has been arriving at that flat for four years.

* * *

He crossed the road at the eleventh minute, because a man who stands anywhere for twelve becomes a thing people describe afterward.

* * *

The letterboxes are in the entrance hall, on the wall, in two rows of four, and the entrance hall is the one part of a building of that kind that belongs to nobody. He was in it for less than a minute and he did not touch anything.

* * *

Six of the eight have names in the slot. One has a name and a piece of tape over it with a different name written on the tape. The fourth on the left has a name in the slot, printed, in the small typeface the building management uses, which means it has not been changed since the management put them all in.

* * *

It is the name on the gas account.

* * *

The box was full to the front edge and had been packed down at least once by somebody with a hand, the way you push post back in when you have taken some out and left the rest.

* * *

Georgij was on the pavement again inside ninety seconds.

* * *

Somebody empties it. Not often, and not completely, and the ones they leave are the ones that do not matter.

———

The woman who was watering the pots on the ground floor had already decided to talk to him before he had decided how to ask.

"Fourth floor, is it?" She had the can in both hands and did not put it down. "Nobody in it. Hasn't been for years, and they've never once sent anybody down for the cleaning rota. Not once! Everybody else does their week."

* * *

Georgij put his hands where she could see them, which costs nothing and buys about four seconds, and which she did not notice at all.

"You hear that? No respect for the neighbours." She moved along to the next one without looking at which. "There was a girl in there at one point. That's the one with the bicycle in the hall that everybody went over. Mrs Kwon on the second would know it all, only she's at her son's till Thursday. Oh, she never stops about that son! Hanyang University, and we hear it every time she's here. If mine was half that diligent. I'd take Ajou."

* * *

Georgij stood in a doorway in Seodaemun and let a woman water four pots.

"How many is everybody?"

"How many is what?"

"You said everybody comes looking for the fourth floor."

"Did I." She thought about it for exactly as long as it deserved. "Well, you're the third in four years and the other two had folders, so you're ahead of them." She stopped over one pot and frowned at it. "This one by the step has never took. Three I've put in there. My husband said it's the drain under it, and he was wrong about nearly everything and right about that."

"And nobody comes for the fourth floor at all."

"The post still comes, mind. That's the thing." She was saying it to the pot. "Every week, near enough. It falls out when the box gets full and I put it back through, because who else is going to."

"Who empties it?"

"He does."

"Who is he?"

"A man! Three times a year. Four." The can went down at last. "Not young. He's got the key to the box and none for the door, which is how I ever met him at all. Stood out there in the rain the first time, waiting for somebody to come out, and I was the somebody. I said you'll catch your death standing about like that. He said he'd been colder."

"What was he like when you let him in?"

* * *

She thought about that in a way that told him nobody had asked her a question about it in four years.

"Oh, he's lovely." She said it with her whole face. "Apologised for the trouble. Asked how the pots do in the winter, and then he listened to the answer, which men don't. And the next time he came he asked after the one by the step. A year later! He remembered which one it was."

* * *

Georgij stood in a stairwell doorway in Seodaemun on a Monday morning and did not let anything happen on his face at all.

"It had died," she said. "He was sorry. Properly sorry, you could see it on him. A man who remembers a plant. There's not many come through that door I'd say that about."

"Has he been recently?"

"Not since the spring. March, was it. It was still cold." She looked at him properly for the first time since he had come in. "You've gone a funny colour. Do you want to sit down? There's a chair just inside the door."

"No. Thank you." He got it out level. "You have been very helpful and I am not going to pretend you have not, and I would like to tell you one true thing in return, because you have given me three."

"Go on then."

"If that man comes back and asks you whether anybody has been here, tell him yes." Georgij said it clearly. "Tell him exactly what I look like and exactly what I asked. Do not be clever about it and do not leave anything out, and do not tell him that I told you to."

* * *

She put one hand on the rail.

"That's not what people say."

"No. People say the other thing, and then the person they said it to has to carry it." He stayed where he was. "You are watering pots in a building you live in. There is nothing here that is worth you being careful about, and if you are careful he will know you were careful, and that will be worse for you than the truth by a very long way."

* * *

The woman looked at him for a while and did not thank him, which he respected.

"You are not selling anything," she said.

"No."

"Then whatever it is, I hope you are the one who gets it."

———

He wrote it in the car before he started the engine, in the order it had come to him and not the order that made it sound like a discovery.

* * *

A flat nobody lives in, with post still coming, in her name.

A man with a key to the box and no key to the building, three or four times a year, six years of pots.

He asked about a plant, twice, a year apart. He was sorry it had died.

* * *

He put the pen down and did not write the fourth line for a while.

* * *

The same man. There is no version where it is not the same man. I have met him three times now without meeting him: in a kitchen near Sinsa, in a florist's carbon, and in a woman's account of her pots.

He is kind to everybody who is not the point. He has been kind to them for years, at intervals, without being asked, and none of it is performance, because a performance does not survive four years of somebody else's plants.

———

Annie had the door of the small room open when he got back, which meant Mrs Seo had told her the car was early.

"Say the worst of it first," she said.

"The flat is hers. Her name in the slot, in the original typeface, never changed." Georgij put his coat over the chair without meaning to. "She has not lived there in four years. The post still arrives and a man empties the box three or four times a year. He has a key to the box and none to the building, and he was doing it before she stopped living there."

* * *

Annie did not sit down.

"Say the part that has moved your face."

"He asked the woman on the ground floor how her pots do in the winter." He heard how it sounded and did not correct it. "And the next time he came, a year later, he asked whether the one by the step had survived, and it had not, and he was sorry."

* * *

The room did the thing it does.

"That is the same man," said Annie.

"It is the same man. Nine years of a cook's name and six years of a stranger's plants." Georgij put both hands flat on the back of the chair. "I have spent three weeks describing him to people as a habit and a signature. He is not a signature. He is a man who is genuinely pleasant to every single person in the world who is not the one he has been sent to."

"And you have just found out that you like the shape of him."

"I have found out that Mrs Bae likes him, and the cook likes him, and a woman with a watering can likes him, and none of them are wrong." He said it flatly. "That is not a weakness in them. That is what he is made of."

* * *

Annie let that stand for a moment before she took it apart, which is the difference between her and everybody else who has ever handled him.

"Then say the other half, because you have not said it and it is the half you drove home with."

* * *

Georgij took slightly too long.

"Somebody has kept that flat alive for four years," he said. "Her name is still in the slot. Her post still comes and is still sorted. There is a place in this city where she has not stopped existing, and the only person maintaining it is the man who empties the box."

"Say what that is."

"I do not know yet whether it is bookkeeping or something else, and I am not going to decide today because I want it to be the second one." He looked at the desk rather than at her. "A man who wants a person to go on existing on paper is one thing. A man who needs an address for the bills is another. They look identical from a stairwell."

* * *

Annie sat down at last.

"There is a third," she said.

"Say it."

"A man who keeps a flat in a woman's name because one day somebody is going to have to be able to say that she went somewhere of her own accord." She said it without any weight on it at all. "You do not sell a person and then keep her post coming for four years unless you have thought about what it would look like if anybody ever asked."

* * *

Georgij stood very still.

"That is worse," he said.

"It is worse and it is more likely, and I would have said it in the car if I had been in it, and you would have got there yourself by Wednesday." She turned her pen over once. "I said it now because you have started deciding what you want things to be before you know what they are, and that started this week and not before it."

* * *

He took that properly, because it was true, and because she had waited until he was sitting down to say it.

"Yes, Mistress."

"Then here is the one thing I want from today, and it is not about him." Annie put the pen down. "That woman with the watering can. You told her to tell him everything."

"I did."

"Say why, and say it as though I had not already worked it out, because I want to hear you do it."

"Because she cannot lie to him." Georgij did not hesitate at all. "He has six years of pots with her. He will know inside two sentences. Then she is a woman who lied to a pleasant man about a stranger, because I asked her to, and she will not be able to explain why. If she tells him everything she is a woman who answered a question, and there is nothing in that for anybody to do anything about."

"And it tells him you were there."

"It tells him a man was there and asked about the fourth floor." He kept his hands where they were. "He was going to find that out. The only thing I had any say in was whether somebody else paid for the four days it would have taken him to find it out slowly."

* * *

Annie looked at him for a long moment.

"Then write her on the second page," she said, "and write next to her that she is the first one you have put on it who is safe."

* * *

He did that, at the desk, before he took his coat off the chair.

* * *

A woman who waters pots in Seodaemun, since eleven o'clock this morning. Safe, because I made her safe on purpose, and it cost me four days I did not have.

* * *

And underneath it, because a page that only holds the tidy version is a different sort of page.

* * *

He knows by the end of this month. Possibly by Friday.

Everything from here is faster than I would like it to be, and I have known that since the twenty-second of June, and I am not going to write it down as though it surprised me.
