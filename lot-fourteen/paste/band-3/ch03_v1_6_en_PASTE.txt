Book Three · Chapter 3: At sixty
Lot Fourteen · Version 1.6 · EN

Day Six Hundred and Twenty-Eight · Wednesday 23 June

It took four weeks, and Georgij spent them getting ready, and none of it was any use.

He read every published thing that man has put his name to since 2011, which is nine speeches, two forewords and an address to a graduating year. There is not a sentence in any of them that he would not say again in a room with the people in it.

He asked Mr Chae what an office of that sort can do to a man without a case, and got a proper answer, and it did not help. Everything on the list was something Gong Doo-hyun was not going to do.

He did not ask Park Sang-hoon and he did not ask Chairman Woo.

A man who telephones two chaebol about a prosecutor has told the prosecutor something, and it is the one thing about himself that is true.

On the second Thursday he wrote one line in the book and did not put anything under it for a fortnight.

There is nothing behind him. Work out what that means before he asks.

He had not worked it out.

———

The telephone rang in the small room at ten past nine on the Monday, and Mrs Seo answered it in the hall as she does.

She did not come up.

She wrote it on the pad that is kept by the telephone for exactly this, in the hand she writes in, and she brought the page up with the tray at eleven.

Wednesday, half past three. He gave the address himself and he did not spell it.

Georgij read it twice.

The first time, a man from that office came to this door and carried it. The second time it was the man himself on the telephone.

The third time it will be Georgij going to him.

Each of the three is politer than the one before.

———

Four weeks to the day, and the same day of the week.

Nobody arranges that by accident and nobody mentions it afterwards.

———

The building is in Seocho and it is the one everybody has seen, and it looks like what it is.

He was through the door at twenty past three and in the room at half past, and nobody asked him for anything except his name, which he gave.

The office is on a corner and it is not large. There is a desk, two chairs on the near side of it, a shelf of bound volumes that have been used, and a photograph of a woman and two grown children on the sill behind him.

There is nothing on the walls at all.

Georgij reads a room off what is in it. It has worked in every room he has walked into for two years, and in most of them it took under a minute.

He read this one in about six seconds and got nothing.

A man who wants something buys something. There is nothing here that anybody gave him and nothing here that anybody could.

———

"Sit down," said Gong Doo-hyun.

Georgij sat down in the chair on the near side of the desk.

"There is a file and I am going to show it to you, because you are going to spend the next year guessing at it otherwise, and I would rather have the year for something else."

He turned it round on the desk and pushed it across, and did not keep a hand on it.

There are eleven pages in it.

The first of them is a photocopy of a page out of a book with about forty names on it.

The fourth name down that page is in a hand Georgij knows better than any other in the world, because it is his.

"That is where it starts," said Gong Doo-hyun. "You wrote it yourself and you knew what you were doing when you did it."

"Yes."

"The rest is nine pages of things that are all true and prove nothing, and one page that is a question I asked myself in April and have not been able to put down." He was not enjoying any of it. "I want you to see how thin it is. A thin file that does not go away is worse than a thick one, and you will understand why before you leave this room."

———

Georgij went through it at his own speed and did not hurry for the man watching him.

Six of the nine are dates. Two are companies with nothing wrong on the face of them. One is a list of five people who lost something between March and August of the year before last, with a line against each of them and no line joining them.

There is not one thing in it that could be put in front of anybody.

The eleventh page has one line on it in a small upright hand, and a date in April.

If none of it is an offence, what is it.

A man who wanted a conviction does not write that down. A man who wanted one would have stopped at the ninth page and gone to find a tenth that helped.

"May I say what it is?"

"Say it."

"It is a shape." Georgij put the pages square and did not push them back. "There is no offence in it. There is a shape, and the shape is mine, and you cannot get it any further than that."

"No."

"Then it is not the file that I am here for."

"No."

———

Gong Doo-hyun took the file back and put it square with the edge of the desk.

"I know you killed him," he said. "That is not the question I brought you here to answer."

Georgij did not move.

"Then say the question."

"A man in that house bought him at thirty-five. A man in that house finished him at sixty." He did not raise his voice anywhere in it. "He spent twenty-five years being what one afternoon in April made him. You have had two."

———

The building did the things a building of that size does at a quarter to four.

"I am not interested in whether you can justify what you did," said Gong Doo-hyun. "You can. I have read it four times and every line of it is true, and that is the worst part of the file and not the best."

He did not put a hand on it again.

"I am interested in what you are going to be at sixty."

———

Georgij has been asked a great many things across a desk.

He has never been asked one he could not answer with something true.

———

"May I ask you about the year after?" he said.

"Which year."

"Two thousand and three."

Gong Doo-hyun sat back for the first time since Georgij had come in.

"He came to the same place on a Thursday for about a year," he said. "He talked the way we all talked. He was still better at it than anybody at that table and he still knew what a bad indictment looked like, and he told us so, and he was right."

"And then?"

"And then he stopped coming, and there was no week in which it happened."

"There was no afternoon where he decided. It worked, and then it worked again." He said it without any weight on it. "By the third year the man who used to say what a bad indictment looked like did not say it any more. Nobody at that table was writing indictments."

Georgij did not say anything for about four seconds.

"That is what I brought you here for," said Gong Doo-hyun, "and you asked me for it yourself, and I would not have got it in front of you any other way."

———

"Next to her," said Georgij, and it was an answer to a question that had been asked six minutes earlier.

"That is who. I asked what."

Georgij did not answer.

"Say one thing you will be at sixty that does not have her in it."

Georgij had his hands where the man could see them and had not moved them since he sat down.

"I cannot."

Gong Doo-hyun did not write it down and did not need to.

———

"I know what I will do if somebody points anything at her."

"So did Choi," said Gong Doo-hyun.

———

Neither of them said anything for a while.

It is the first time in two years that a sentence has been put in front of Georgij that he could not take apart, and it took the man in the chair three words and no papers at all.

He did not try. Trying would have been an answer and there was not one.

Gong Doo-hyun did not follow it up.

He had brought a man in to find out one thing. He found it out at twenty to four, and there was nothing to put in the rest of the hour, and neither of them got up.

———

"You may go," said Gong Doo-hyun. "I am not going to ask you anything and I am not going to tell you not to do anything, and if you were expecting either of those you have misread the afternoon."

Georgij stood up.

"I would like to say one thing to you, and then I will go."

"Say it."

"You buried a man that nobody else would have buried," said Georgij. "I have been in a great many rooms this year and I have not seen anything like it."

Gong Doo-hyun looked at him for about two seconds.

"That is the second time you have said a true thing to me that costs you nothing." He had already picked up the file. "I would like you to notice that I have written both of them down."

———

He was on the expressway at twenty past four and did not say anything to Jang for eleven minutes.

There are two men in this country who have looked at what he does and wanted nothing for it. One of them wrote a letter in February in his own hand and posted it from a box that is not near his house.

The other one has eleven pages and a question, and he cannot be paid, and he is not going to get tired, and there is nothing in the world he wants.

Georgij worked out on that road what that leaves.

He did not write it down.

———

He told her all of it in order at about eight, and it took nineteen minutes, and he left nothing out of it.

Annie did not interrupt any of it, and she does not.

"Say the last part again."

"So did Choi."

"Yes." She let it be in the room for as long as it needed. "And what did you say to that."

"Nothing. There was nothing to say to it that would not have been a worse version of what I had already said."

"Then say what he is," said Annie.

"An honest man with a file and no case, who is not going to be bought and not going to get tired."

———

Annie put the pen down.

"That is what he is to you." She had not moved in the chair. "Say what he is to me."

———

Georgij did not answer for a long time.

"Nothing yet," he said.

Annie picked the pen back up.
