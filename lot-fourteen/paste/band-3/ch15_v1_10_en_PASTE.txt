Book Three · Chapter 15: Him
Lot Fourteen · Version 1.10 · EN

Day Eight Hundred and Two · Tuesday 14 December

Park Sang-hoon telephoned at eleven to say that he would come at four, which he does not do.

He came up that drive once before, on the twenty-ninth of December, the year before last. He telephoned at half past ten that morning and asked, and was given two o'clock, and arrived at four minutes to.

This time he did not ask. He said four.

A man who gives five hours' notice has decided already and wants the deciding to be seen.

Georgij spent the five hours not preparing anything.

There was a thing to be done with them and it was to find out what the man had. He did not do it. A visitor who walks into a room that has been made ready has been expected, and nothing he says afterwards is the thing he came to say.

———

What happened on the first of December was ordinary and it happened to Park Sang-hoon.

Hanseong Shipping put one of the two customers that matter to it with a company that has four ships. The notice went out as an operating matter and an operating matter does not go to holders.

Park Sang-hoon holds forty per cent of that company.

He has two men on that board and neither of them was in a room, because there was no room. A subcontracting is signed by an officer.

He would have had it from the December allocation sheet, the way any other holder would, in an envelope, a week after it happened.

———

The first allocation under the new arrangement went out on the second, and there was nothing wrong with it.

A man who has never made one for her before made it correctly, and there is nothing to be done with that except wait for the next one.

That is what the fortnight was.

———

She asked one thing at half past three.

"Is there a version of this where he is here about his own company."

"There is one and it is the one I would like it to be."

"And the other."

"The other is that he has worked out what this house is doing, and has come to be paid for not saying so."

Annie put the cap back on the pen.

"Then we find out which."

———

He did not sit down and he did not take the coffee.

Mrs Seo brought it because Mrs Seo brings it, and he thanked her properly, and the cup stayed where she put it and went cold there.

He kept his coat on. In that house that is not rudeness and both of them read it correctly: he is not going to be here long enough for a coat to matter.

———

"I am going to give it to you in the order I got it."

He was at the near end of the desk with one hand on the back of the chair he had not taken.

"There was no borrow left in two of those names in September. A dormant company in Jeju came onto a register I am entitled to look at. And your Mr Chae claimed one billion four hundred million against a company I own two fifths of."

He let go of the chair.

"I put those in a row on the eighth of this month, in a car, and it took about four seconds."

He was not making anything of it.

"Somebody has been paying for that borrow since the summer, and I have been angry about it for six days."

"That is three," said Annie.

"There is a fourth and it is not a fact."

He picked the cold cup up off the table and put it down again without drinking any of it.

"In April I asked in three places about a man and heard nothing back anywhere, and I put that down to nobody knowing."

His hand went back to the top of the chair and stayed there.

"Nobody knew. That is a different sentence."

———

He put one sheet on the desk and did not turn it round, because she reads upside down and both of them know it.

It is an order to sell short in the credit arm, made out to a broker who is not in this country, with a quantity on it and an instruction to execute on receipt of a further instruction. There is a date at the top and his own name at the bottom in his own hand.

He is not short the shipping company, and he is not short the two that lose money and are supposed to.

He is short the one that holds the others up.

A man who takes that one and not the others has not guessed.

The quantity is small.

A large position is a man who wanted money. A small one is a man who was told something and did not want to look as though he had enjoyed it.

The small one is the one that gets read out.

"That is what I have," said Park Sang-hoon. "I am not going to make it smaller than it is, and I am not going to tell you how long I have had it, because you would take the rest from the date."

Annie read it where it lay and did not put a hand on it.

"And the price."

"Him."

Nobody in that room did anything for about three seconds, and one of the three was working out how much of it had been arranged.

It was not arranged. That is the part Georgij has not been able to put down since.

———

"There is no line anywhere in this country that binds him to me," said Annie. "It was struck on the nineteenth of February and nothing has replaced it. He belongs to nobody except himself, and I have never once corrected a man who assumed otherwise."

"Then there is nothing standing in your way."

"I told him he was mine and I told him to build on it, and I said it in a room with nothing to write it on." Her hands had not moved. "So the answer is the one you got in December, and it is truer now than it was then."

"He is not for sale."

She let that stand where it was for as long as it needed.

"Do the other thing."

That is the price and she paid it in four words.

If he does it there is a witness with a date on him, and seven months of work in that house becomes a conspiracy on the morning it starts.

Park Sang-hoon did not look pleased and did not look sorry, and he has never in his life pretended to be either.

"I did not want it to be this," he said.

"I know that. It does not change anything and I am not going to be kind to you about it."

———

Georgij was at the end of the room where he stands, and nobody had asked him anything.

"Chairman Park."

"I do not like it when you call me Chairman." Sang-hoon did not turn round. "And I like it less when you ask whether you may say a thing. Both of them mean something is coming that I am not going to enjoy."

"May I ask you something?"

"You have never once waited for me to say yes."

"In March you told me that you do not pay what a thing is worth." Georgij did not move from where he was standing. "You said you pay what it costs to stop having to think about it."

He had that sentence on a page from the eleventh of March last year, four lines under something else, and he had not needed the page until this afternoon.

"I did."

"Do you want to start having to think about it again?"

Sang-hoon did not answer for longer than the question needed.

He looked at the window, and then at Annie, and then at the sheet on the desk. He did all three in the order a man does them when he is looking for a version he has not seen yet.

"No," said Park Sang-hoon.

"Thank you." Georgij did not put anything round it. "I consider you a friend, and I would like to go on doing it."

———

He picked the sheet up himself, which nobody had asked him to do.

He took a pen out of his own coat and drew a line through the quantity and a line through the execution instruction, and put his initials at the end of the second one.

He did not touch the date.

Then he put it back down, turned the right way round for her, which is not how it had lain the first time.

"You keep that."

"Why."

"Because it was on your desk with a price on it."

He put the sheet square with the edge of the desk with two fingers.

"There is going to be a morning when somebody asks who else had one of these ready. The line is mine and the date stays."

He put the pen back in the coat he had never taken off, and he did it before he said the rest of it.

"And I would like it on the record that the forty per cent are mine because of nineteen pages with four mistakes in them."

He was looking at the sheet on her desk and not at either of them.

"I found all four and I bought anyway, and I came here this afternoon to spend them on him."

———

He got to the door before he said the next part, and he said it to the door.

"You need a date."

"Yes," said Georgij.

"That is not what I expected you to say."

Sang-hoon looked at him then.

"It cannot be mine and it cannot come out of a broker. It has to come out of Han's own office on Han's own paper."

"It has been missing since the summer."

"Then you knew what you were waiting for before I did."

He put his hat on.

"And what I brought was a shortcut to a place you were already standing. That is the most expensive four seconds I have had this year, and I had them in a car on the eighth."

Then he had his hand on the door and did not turn round again.

"You are the second person to say that to me this year," he said, "and the first one wanted something."

His own car took him out at six minutes past five.

———

Annie put down the page she had not been reading, and stood up, and started across the room.

"Two men in this country who own a great deal consider you a friend," she said, "and one of them has just gone down my stairs." She was not hurrying any of it. "You told me once that you have no money and no name that opens a door and nothing in this country to telephone."

She finished it standing beside him, and looked at the side of his face.

"Yes, Mistress."

Annie kissed him on the cheek.

Georgij was not there for a moment.

The thing behind that face that counts everything had stopped, and there was nothing else in there.

Then he could not stand still.

He moved his weight, and put his hands behind his back, and took them out again and put them at his sides. He has only the two, and he went through both, and neither of them was any good.

Something went across his face, and this time it stayed.

A small smile got out. He was holding a much larger one down and he was not managing all of it.

"You cannot say that any more."

———

Mrs Seo had come in for the coffee that Sang-hoon had not drunk, and could not get out again without crossing in front of them, so she saw all of it.

She has run that house since before he came to it and she had never once seen that man be no use to anybody at all.

She put the tray down in the kitchen on the wrong table.

By the next evening there was nobody working in that house who had not been told, including the man at the gate.

———

The sheet stayed on the desk, turned the right way round, until eight.

Then Annie put it in the drawer with the two pages that have no date on them.

It is the only thing in that drawer that has one, and it is the only thing in that drawer that was never going to be used.

———

Georgij wrote two lines in the book at about ten.

He came to take the cheapest way there is and he took it off the table himself.

Not that date. Han's.

He did not write the other thing down.
