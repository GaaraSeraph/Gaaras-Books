Book Three · Chapter 13: Twice
Lot Fourteen · Version 1.10 · EN

Day Seven Hundred and Seventy-Four · Tuesday 16 November

The valve was ordered on the eighteenth of August and it is the last piece.

The charters stopped the charges arriving late. The valve stops what the late charges have already done to the plant.

With both of them there is nothing left on that account after January, and the word goes back to meaning what it means.

It was made in a town in Europe by a company that builds about four hundred a year, and nobody keeps one on a shelf, and it took seven weeks.

It weighs sixty kilos with the actuator on it and it travels in a crate the size of a desk.

———

It came off a ship at Busan on the fourth of November.

It has not moved since.

The date came back as the eleventh. On the tenth it came back as the nineteenth.

Nobody has done anything wrong. A box that is not urgent waits for the window it was given, and if the window moves it waits again.

There is no clause under a crate. That is the whole of why it is still there.

———

It came up on the Friday list as two lines and nobody in that building thought anything of it.

A crate that misses one window has missed a window. A crate that misses two has been touched.

Georgij has read that list every Friday since August and this is the second time he has gone anywhere because of it.

———

The old valve is still in the plant and the old valve is what it is.

It has been taken apart twelve times since May and it will be taken apart again in the last week of November.

Every week the crate stands on that stack is a week in which the thing she has stopped calling maintenance goes on being true.

———

Georgij went down on the Tuesday and took the boy.

He has taken him into thirty-one workshops and this is not a workshop.

A workshop is one man with both hands in an engine who will talk for twenty minutes because nobody has asked him anything in a year. A forwarder's office is four people who are behind and know they are behind.

He told him nothing in the car about what the crate was for.

———

Mr Bok has the operations desk and has had it for sixteen years.

There are four people at that desk. Three of them were on telephones about something that went wrong on Sunday and had been on them since eight.

Mr Bok is the fourth and the oldest, and the other three put things in front of him without looking up, which is a kind of standing and comes with nothing attached to it.

He kept one finger on the screen the whole time he talked and moved it down the list as he went, which is how he keeps his place.

Lee Jun stood where he could see the door and the hands and did not sit down when he was offered a chair.

———

"It is on the nineteenth," said Mr Bok.

"It was on the eleventh."

"It was on the eleventh and then it was not." He did not look up from the list. "That came down from Seoul on the ninth. There is nothing on this desk that says why."

He said all of that in front of the boy.

A boy of sixteen standing by a door with nothing in his hands is not a person in an office. He is a chair with a coat over it, and men talk over a chair.

Georgij has watched him arrange that in thirty-one workshops and stopped being pleased about it somewhere around the ninth of them.

Georgij did not ask him why.

He asked him what the movement had been booked as, and then what it was booked as now, and Mr Bok answered both without having to look, because a man with a finger on a screen has already looked.

———

Then Mr Bok said the part he had been carrying since the ninth.

"The girl who took the call wrote it on a slip and went home." He moved the finger down two lines. "Twenty-three years old. They do not put a name on anything now."

Georgij did not say anything about the girl. A man who is answered stops talking, and this one had not finished.

"Nine hundred boxes off that berth this month and not one came down from Seoul." Mr Bok took the finger off the screen for the first time. "That one did."

———

Mr Bok came round the desk to the door with them, which he did not have to do.

"They have moved the car park to the other side," he said. "Three hundred metres. Sixteen years I have had that desk, and now I walk it twice a day and three times if it rains."

It has nothing to do with anything and he said it to two men he had never met.

———

They were on the bridge before the boy said anything.

"He said Seoul twice, sir."

That is the fifth thing he has brought since September without being asked, and the first four were numbers.

Georgij waited.

"The first time was an answer. The second time he was telling somebody."

———

A berth window that moves is a man at a terminal deciding on the day.

There is nothing to read afterwards, because there was never anything to read. It is a judgement, and a judgement is weather.

An instruction from a head office is a person, a date and a route, and it sits on a system that keeps things for seven years whether anybody wants it kept or not.

Since the second week of May there has been nothing in any of it with a date on it.

There is now.

———

Georgij heard the first Seoul and put it where he puts things that are probably nothing.

He did not hear the second one at all.

He was working out what the movement had been booked as and what it was booked as now. That is the right question. He was asking it in the four seconds when the man was saying the other thing.

———

Georgij did not say any of that in the car.

He said one thing, and it was not about Hanseong.

"You have asked me twice this year what something was for, and both times I told you no."

"Yes, sir."

"This one you get."

———

Lee Jun did not say thank you, which is correct, and did not look pleased, which is not learned.

"A man who decides a thing on the day leaves nothing behind him," said Georgij. "A man who is told to decide it leaves the telling, and the telling is somewhere, and it has a date on it and a person under it."

"Yes, sir."

"You found the telling. Mr Bok gave it to you twice and I had it once."

It cost him nothing to say and he knows that, and he said it straight away, so that it could not turn into a consolation further along the bridge.

———

Lee Jun looked out of his side of the window for about two minutes.

"Then I have to know what it is for," he said.

"You do now."

"Yes, sir."

"That is not a reward and you are not going to enjoy it." Georgij had his hands on his knees and did not move them. "A man who knows what a thing is for cannot afterwards be a man who was only counting."

———

The boy did not answer that.

He has been in the house four months and six days. He has learned where the door is in every room he has been in, which he could already do, and he has learned to count a shelf, which he could not.

This is the first thing anybody has taken off him.

He did not ask a second thing, which he does not, and Georgij noticed the not asking for the first time.

———

Georgij gave it to her at eight in the order it had happened.

"Who found it."

"Lee Jun."

Annie looked up from what she was doing, which she had not done when he came in.

"Say the boy's part again."

"Mr Bok said Seoul twice. The second time was not an answer to anything, and the boy heard the difference and I did not."

———

Annie took her hand off the page.

"Then he is not a boy who counts things," she said. "You will have to work out what he is instead, and you have until he does it himself."

"Yes, Mistress."

Annie went back to the page and did not look up for the rest of it.

"And when you have worked it out you are going to want to give him something."

"Yes."

"You have nothing to give. That has never stopped you, and it is the only part of this house I have not been able to write down."

———

Nothing is going to be done about the crate.

It will come on the nineteenth or it will not, and if it does not it will come in December, and the plant runs on the old valve until it does.

She has looked at what that costs and left it where it is, which is the second thing this month she has decided to pay for instead of answering.

———

Seol Gwan-ik said in a borrowed room in October that the order would read as caused.

The order has changed since then. A foreign national goes to a retired prosecutor, and then nothing happens for three weeks, and then a company that has been quiet since May charters eleven ships out of its own pocket.

And then, on the ninth, an office in Seoul moves a crate.

A man reading that from outside has to explain why the chaebol moved second.

It clears nobody and it is not nothing, and it is the first thing this year that arrived without anybody in this house doing it.

———

Nobody has seen the instruction and nobody is going to ask for it.

It exists and it has a date. It went from an office in Seoul to a desk in Busan and a girl of twenty-three wrote it on a slip.

A man who wanted it could not get it and would ruin himself trying, and the only thing anybody needs from it today is that it is not weather.

———

It does not have to be got. It has to have been there.

Somebody with the standing to ask will ask for it one day, and on that day the only question is whether it existed on the ninth of November.

The answer to that is already fixed, and nobody in this house fixed it.

That is the difference between a case and a grievance, and an old man in Seodaemun gave it to him for nothing.

Georgij put four things in the book that night, and only three of them are about Hanseong.

He has written one.

The ninth of November, Seoul to Busan, a slip in a tray.

Sixty kilos of steel on a stack, and it is the cheapest thing he has ever moved.

The boy had it in the office and I had it on the bridge.

———

Lee Jun wrote nothing down in that office and nothing in the car.

He wrote it at the top of the back stairs, on a sheet out of the pad he was given for the workshops, and he put the date at the top of the page.

Nobody has told him to do that.
