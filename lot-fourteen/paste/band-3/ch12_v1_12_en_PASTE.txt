Book Three · Chapter 12: The other word
Lot Fourteen · Version 1.12 · EN

Day Seven Hundred and Sixty-Two · Thursday 4 November

Since the second week of May she has paid for it twice a month and called it maintenance.

The word is not untrue. A valve that fails is maintenance, the invoice says so, the account it goes on says so, and the man who signs it off has never had a reason to look at the line above.

Mr Chae's memorandum is two weeks old and has done nothing at all.

It was not built to do anything. It is two and a half pages, it names Hanseong Shipping four times, and it accuses it of nothing.

It is dated the twenty-first and it sits where such papers sit: with her bank, with her insurer, and with an auditor who will read it in March and ask one question about it.

———

From the first of November her cargo does not go on a Hanseong bottom.

Nobody has cancelled anything. The contract runs to the end of next year and no letter has gone anywhere about it.

The cargo goes on whatever else is sailing. What else is sailing has to be found, and finding it costs what it costs.

Eleven sailings a quarter are the tight sort. Those are the eleven that moved.

The rest has three weeks of slack in it and goes the way it has always gone, and that is the part that makes the whole of it look like nothing.

———

Nobody finds eleven slots by wanting them.

Her own logistics people did it over nine days and two of them did nothing else. The first was found in about ninety minutes because a box line had a gap and was pleased to fill it. The fourth took two days. The eighth was not found at all and the cargo went by air, which is not a thing anybody in that trade does twice.

The eleventh was bought from a company that lost a tender to her group two years ago.

They quoted her a price with a number in it that was not there to be reasonable. She took it inside the hour. The man who quoted it will tell that story at dinners for the rest of his life.

———

Space at four days' notice on a route that is already full costs about four times the berth.

Georgij worked it out on the Monday and gave her one number.

Then she asked for the other one, which is the quarter, and he gave her that as well.

Annie copied the second number onto the top sheet.

———

A charter is not a private act.

Somebody has to be asked whether there is room. Somebody quotes a price, and the quote goes into a book that other people read for a living.

One charter is a bad week. Eleven in a quarter, by a company that has held the same carrier for nine years, is a sentence.

Nobody has to write the sentence down. That is the whole of the arrangement.

———

The quarter goes out inside the group on the fifteenth.

Six people see it before it goes anywhere near a bank, and the line is in it, and it is in it under its own name and not spread across four others.

She was asked twice whether it could go in as freight and rate variance, which is what it is, and which would have been the end of it.

She said no both times and did not explain it either time.

One of the six will say it at a dinner inside a month without meaning anything by it. That is not a failing in any of the six. That is what six people are.

———

"They will not read it as bad luck," said Georgij.

"No."

"They will read it as a company that has stopped relying on its carrier, and the first thing anybody does after that is ask why."

Annie had the quarter in front of her and had not turned the page.

"And the second thing they do is ask what else I am not saying."

———

The letter that went to all eleven customers is four lines long.

From the first of November your material moves on chartered space. The additional cost is ours and does not pass to you. The dates in your contract are unchanged and will be met. We will write again when it returns to the scheduled service.

It names nobody. It does not use the word carrier once.

A man who reads it and knows the trade knows exactly what has happened to her. A man who does not know the trade knows only that his supplier has gone to some trouble on his behalf, and writes back to say so.

Four of the eleven wrote back to say so.

———

Mr Chae put a second letter in front of her on the morning the eleven went out, and she did not send it.

It was addressed to Hanseong Shipping, it was two paragraphs, and every line of it was accurate. It asked for an explanation of the allocations between May and October and reserved her position under the contract.

It is the only satisfying thing anybody in that house could have written this year.

"Not while the charters are the only thing on the table," said Annie.

Mr Chae took it back without any argument, because he had known the answer when he brought it and had brought it anyway, which is what she keeps him for.

———

Mr Namgung telephoned on the Tuesday and came on the Thursday.

He has bought the same precursor from that group for nineteen years. He is about sixty and he came on his own.

She saw him in the small room, which is hers and which she does not lend out, and she kept him an hour and twenty minutes.

———

He put his glasses on to read a page he has known by heart since Tuesday.

It is the October invoice, and there is a line on it that was not there in September.

"You have moved us onto something else."

"I have."

"And you are paying for it."

"I am paying for it."

———

Mr Namgung took the glasses off and folded them and put them on the arm of the chair.

"In two thousand and sixteen this group kept me running for eleven weeks when nobody else would take the cargo," he said. "Two of my competitors were down for a month. I have told that to a great many people who did not want to hear it."

"I know the eleven weeks."

"Everybody knows the eleven weeks. Fifty-one charges." Mr Namgung has counted his life in charges since he was thirty and does not know that he does it. "Fifty-one, and not one of them short, and I was the only man in that segment who did not stop."

He was pleased with the sentence and did not hide it.

———

Then he did the other thing, in the same voice, without any join between the two.

"I am going to have to put a second source in front of my board in January."

Annie did not move at all.

"That is not about you," said Mr Namgung. "You will understand that better than anybody at that table."

———

Georgij said nothing in that room from beginning to end.

He was there because Mr Namgung has met him twice and does not find him strange, and because a second set of ears in that hour was worth more than anything he could have added to it.

He wrote nothing down while the man was there.

———

At the door Mr Namgung said the eleven weeks again.

He said it shorter the second time, the way a man does when he has already had the good of it, and he shook her hand and thanked her twice and went out into a wet afternoon perfectly content.

He had come to say thank you. He had said it. He had also, from January, taken about seven billion won a year off her book, and he left the house believing he had done the first thing and not the second.

———

Georgij has spent three months building a thing that has cost nobody anything, and none of it has been spent, and that is what it was built to be.

The first thing that has actually happened in six months is hers, and it went out of her account on the third of November, and it is going to go out again in February.

What that arrangement says about him is a thing he settled with himself in August and has not opened since.

———

Annie was at the desk at half past six.

"What did it cost."

"Four times the berth on eleven sailings, and it will be twelve next quarter." Georgij had his hands behind his back. "And Mr Namgung, in January, and two more who have not telephoned yet because they are waiting to see whether he does it first."

"That is the money."

"That is the money."

"Now the rest."

———

Georgij was not quick with it.

"Say the word that is in Mr Chae's paper," he said.

"You have read the paper."

"I have read it. I have not heard you say it."

———

The clock in the hall did the thing it does at the quarter.

"Interference," said Annie. "That is his word and it is a good one. An insurer uses it, and it does not require anybody to have meant anything by it."

"And yours."

Annie put the pen across the page and left it there.

"Mine is that a man I have been in a room with four times has been doing this to me since May."

"Yes."

"I have been paying the invoices and calling it something else." She did not soften it. "I did that because it was cheaper than saying it out loud, and I have now found out what it costs the other way, and it is more."

———

Annie turned the quarter face down.

———

"What happens now."

"Nothing from me."

"Say why."

"Because the first thing anybody does with eleven charters is decide what to do about them, and whatever he decides will be the first thing he has done since May that has a date on it." Georgij did not move. "Everything before this was a berth window, and a berth window is somebody's judgement on the day."

"And a judgement is not a document."

"No."

———

She picked the pen back up.

"Then we wait for him to write one."

———

Nothing came back from Hanseong that week.

No letter, no telephone call, no man in a car at the end of the drive. The berths did not move at all between the first and the seventh, and that is the first thing in six months that could mean two things.

Either somebody has stopped, or somebody is deciding.

The thing in that house has still not been pulled.

But it is not standing still any more, because it is not the only thing in the room, and the other thing in the room is a line on an invoice that eleven people have now read.
